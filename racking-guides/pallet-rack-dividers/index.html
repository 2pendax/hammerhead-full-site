Pallet Rack Dividers

Pallet rack dividers separate approved products within a bay, especially long, upright or similar goods. They are not automatically designed to stop a falling or heavily leaning load: height, stiffness, attachment, lateral force, rack compatibility and handling clearances must be verified.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 25 November 2026

Plan Your Rack Specification →
The short answer

Select pallet rack dividers by defining the products to be separated or supported: dimensions, weight, center of gravity, orientation, quantity, surface sensitivity and how operators place or remove them. Decide whether the divider is only a visual separator, a guide, a light stabilizer or an engineered restraint; these functions require different stiffness and force capacity. Identify the rack system, beam or deck support, divider base, height, spacing, attachment and any new vertical, lateral or torsional forces. Check that goods cannot roll, slide, snag or project into aisles, flues or adjacent bays, and that forklifts or manual handlers have safe clearances. Use compatible cradles, lips or back containment where the hazard demands more than division. Coordinate sprinkler discharge and flue spaces. Install to approved drawings, trial representative products, post operating limits and inspect for bent members, loose attachments, broken welds, displaced units, overload and unauthorized field changes.

A divider can perform very different duties

The word “divider” can describe a simple visual separator or a structural restraint. Define the required function and credible forces before selecting the product.

Divider forms and support methods

  • Vertical rod or tube divider for approved light separation duties.
  • Frame-style divider for greater height or stiffness.
  • Mesh divider for broader separation and smaller objects.
  • Deck-mounted divider compatible with the deck and its support.
  • Beam-mounted divider using a system-specific attachment.
  • Purpose-built cradle or compartment where rolling or lateral force is significant.
  • Removable divider only where the lock and operating control prevent displacement.

Descriptions vary by supplier. Use drawings, load data and attachment details rather than the product name alone.

Lean, impact and lateral-load assumptions

  • Normal gentle product contact during storage.
  • Intentional leaning from product geometry.
  • Load shift after banding or packaging failure.
  • Operator push/pull during retrieval.
  • Fork or lifting-device contact.
  • Rolling or sliding from an inclined or smooth support.
  • Seismic or other site actions where applicable.
  • Accidental impact, which may be outside a standard divider's design duty.

Document which actions are included. If credible force exceeds the divider's verified duty, redesign the support or handling method rather than relying on the divider.

Rack, beam and deck compatibility

  1. Identify the exact rack manufacturer, upright and beam system.
  2. Record the divider base, hooks, clamps, bolts or weld detail.
  3. Check vertical, lateral and torsional forces at the attachment.
  4. Verify beam, deck, support, frame and anchor capacity as relevant.
  5. Keep beam connectors and safety locks accessible.
  6. Confirm the divider cannot lift out or rotate unintentionally.
  7. Obtain manufacturer or qualified engineering approval.
  8. Record the approved position and spacing.

Divider height, spacing and product projection

  • Set spacing from the largest approved product plus handling tolerance.
  • Provide enough height for the defined separation or stability function.
  • Keep product center of gravity and credible lean within the approved envelope.
  • Prevent goods from projecting into the operating aisle or adjacent bay.
  • Keep rear projection, flues and back-to-back clearances compliant.
  • Provide room for slings, forks, hands or lifting aids.
  • Label each compartment's product family and load limit where needed.

When a divider is not enough

  • Add an approved cradle or chock for rolling products.
  • Use back mesh, netting or restraint for a defined rear-fall hazard.
  • Use front retention only when it can be operated safely and is designed for the load.
  • Provide a purpose rack for sheets, coils or unstable assemblies.
  • Improve packaging or banding where load integrity is the root issue.
  • Change storage orientation if it reduces instability and handling risk.
  • Separate people from the fall zone rather than relying on a light divider.

Flue spaces, sprinklers and stored products

Dividers and the products they organize can affect flues, sprinkler discharge and storage arrangement. Coordinate the complete bay with the approved fire strategy.

  • Maintain required longitudinal and transverse flues.
  • Keep divider frames and products from obstructing sprinkler patterns.
  • Confirm commodity, packaging and maximum storage height.
  • Review mesh or solid divider surfaces.
  • Keep exits, fire equipment and inspection access clear.
  • Control changes in product size or compartment density.

Damage and warning signs

  • Bent, bowed, twisted or cracked divider.
  • Broken weld or damaged hook, clamp or bolt.
  • Loose, missing or improvised fastener.
  • Divider lifted, rotated or displaced from its approved position.
  • Repeated product or fork impact.
  • Product leaning harder than the intended duty.
  • Corrosion, heat or chemical damage.
  • Unauthorized cutting, extension or field welding.

Unload and control the affected compartment where stability is uncertain. Assess the product, divider and supporting rack—not only the visibly damaged member.

Divider selection workflow

  1. Profile the product, bundle and handling method.
  2. Define separation, guidance, stabilization or restraint duty.
  3. Calculate or specify credible contact and lateral forces.
  4. Select divider form, height, spacing and attachment.
  5. Verify rack, deck, beam and frame compatibility.
  6. Coordinate clearances, flues and other containment.
  7. Install to approved drawings.
  8. Trial representative loading and retrieval.
  9. Label limits and complete an inspection baseline.
  10. Control future product and divider changes.

Pallet rack dividers: common questions

Can a rack divider stop a falling load?

Only if it is specifically designed, attached and rated for the credible force. Many dividers are intended only for separation or light guidance.

How tall should a divider be?

Height depends on product geometry, center of gravity, compartment width, lean, handling and required function. Use verified project data.

Can dividers be attached to wire decking?

Only when the deck, divider and attachment are compatible and the deck/support system is verified for the resulting loads and forces.

Are dividers suitable for pipes?

They may separate compatible bundles, but rolling products often need cradles, chocks, banding or purpose restraints in addition to dividers.

Can a bent divider be straightened?

Not without an approved repair method. Control the load, inspect the attachment and rack, and replace or repair only after competent assessment.

Information to send when specifying dividers

Send product and bundle dimensions and weights, orientation, center of gravity and credible lean, photos, quantity per compartment, handling method, rack and beam details, deck/support arrangement, required divider function, aisle and rear clearances, flues, sprinklers and any existing damage.
