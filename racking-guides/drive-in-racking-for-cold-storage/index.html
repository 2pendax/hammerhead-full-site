Drive In Racking for Cold Storage

Drive in racking for cold storage can reduce refrigerated aisle volume by storing pallets several positions deep, but its value depends on SKU batch size, lane fill, rotation rules and disciplined forklift operation. A dense drawing is not a useful solution if lanes remain partly filled, pallets vary, trucks cannot align safely, frost hides damage, or refrigeration and fire systems are obstructed.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 25 November 2026

Plan Your Rack Specification →
The short answer

Plan drive in racking for cold storage by defining temperature and moisture cycles, pallet dimensions and maximum weights, underside and runner geometry, SKU count, pallets per SKU and batch, expiry policy, inbound/outbound peaks and desired lane depth. Assign each lane to compatible pallets and inventory so deep positions can be filled without excessive honeycombing. Confirm whether single-entry LIFO operation is acceptable or whether drive-through access and another rotation method are required. Design lane width, rail spacing, bearing, entry, depth pitch, level elevations, stops, top ties and guidance around the actual cold-rated forklift and pallet tolerances. Coordinate insulated floor and anchors, evaporator airflow and access, defrost drainage, sprinklers and flues, doors, lights, services and egress. Specify low-temperature materials and corrosion protection; protect entries and bases; and implement load notices, lane rules, impact reporting and inspections that remain practical under frost and reduced visibility.

What determines a cold-storage drive-in lane?

Every lane combines inventory, pallet support, truck movement and cold-room interfaces.

LIFO, FIFO and expiry control

Single-entry drive-in lanes usually favor last-in, first-out operation because the front pallet blocks deeper positions. Drive-through lanes have access from two ends but still require disciplined loading and unloading.

  • State FIFO, FEFO, LIFO or batch-release requirements.
  • Assign one compatible SKU/batch pattern per lane.
  • Record production and expiry dates at receiving.
  • Prevent a new batch from blocking older stock improperly.
  • Plan quarantine and partial-lane rules.
  • Verify warehouse-management logic matches physical access.

How does honeycombing reduce density?

When a lane is reserved for a SKU but not fully occupied, its empty deep positions may be unusable for other products. This is honeycombing.

  1. Profile pallets per SKU and batch over time.
  2. Test several lane depths, widths and level counts.
  3. Reserve capacity for quarantine and peaks.
  4. Calculate average and worst-case lane fill.
  5. Compare usable positions with alternative systems.
  6. Include replenishment and staging space.

A shallower lane can outperform a deeper lane when batch sizes are smaller or more variable.

Which pallet data controls lane geometry?

  • Maximum loaded width, depth, height and weight.
  • Runner direction, width, spacing and underside openings.
  • Product bulge, overhang and center of gravity.
  • Frozen pallet distortion, damage and stiffness.
  • Rail bearing length and permitted placement tolerance.
  • Fork entry and withdrawal clearance.

Test the worst approved pallet, not only a nominal sample. Pallet quality is a structural and operational input.

Cold-rated forklift and lane-clearance checks

  • Truck body, wheel, mast, carriage and overhead-guard envelope.
  • Fork length, thickness, spacing and lift path.
  • Residual capacity at every rail elevation.
  • Steering tolerance, sway and entry alignment.
  • Guidance rails or wheel-guide requirements.
  • Cab visibility, fogging, lighting and protective clothing.
  • Battery, hydraulics, tires and electronics at temperature.

Check the moving truck at the narrowest brackets, guides and base protection—not only the nominal lane opening.

Frost, condensation and corrosion in deep lanes

  • Warm-air infiltration can frost rails, bases and floors.
  • Defrost water can collect at entries or hidden rear positions.
  • Ice can reduce traction and conceal anchor or impact damage.
  • Deep lanes can be difficult to light, dry and inspect.
  • Coating damage at guides and uprights needs prompt repair.
  • Steel, locks, plastics and accessories need low-temperature verification.

Protecting drive-in rack from forklift impacts

  • Design entry guides and protectors around the approved truck envelope.
  • Use high-visibility lane identifiers and load limits.
  • Keep floor guidance, rails and stops clear of ice and debris.
  • Train operators on entry, lift, placement and withdrawal sequence.
  • Require immediate reporting after any contact.
  • Unload and quarantine urgent damage under a site procedure.
  • Inspect hidden rails, brackets, braces and anchors after impacts.

Cold-storage drive-in planning workflow

  1. Define temperature, moisture, pallets and inventory rotation.
  2. Model SKU batches and lane fill across peaks.
  3. Select lane depth and count using usable capacity.
  4. Verify pallet rails, bearing, stops and level elevations.
  5. Check cold-rated forklift envelope and operations.
  6. Coordinate airflow, refrigeration, fire, floor and building services.
  7. Verify structure, anchors, finish and protection.
  8. Install, inspect and commission with representative pallets and trucks.
  9. Issue lane rules, load notices, training and inspection plan.

Drive in racking for cold storage: common questions

Is drive-in rack always the highest-density cold-storage option?

Not always. Actual density depends on lane fill, SKUs, rotation, partial lanes, forklift access and building interfaces.

Can drive-in rack provide FIFO?

Single-entry lanes generally favor LIFO. Drive-through access can support another flow pattern, but inventory rules and layout must be verified.

How deep should a drive-in lane be?

Choose depth from batch size, SKU profile, rotation, peak stock and honeycombing analysis—not from the maximum that fits physically.

Can mixed pallet sizes use one lane?

Only if every approved pallet fits the rail bearing, clearance, capacity and operating rules. Separate lane types are often safer.

How is damage inspected inside deep lanes?

Plan lighting and safe access, inspect after every reported impact, and include rails, brackets, braces, anchors and rear positions in routine checks.

Information to send for cold-storage drive-in rack

Send temperature and moisture conditions, pallet underside and maximum loaded dimensions/weights, SKU/batch/expiry profile, FIFO/LIFO rules, daily and peak moves, target stock, desired lane depths, forklift specification, building and insulated-floor drawings, refrigeration, sprinkler, door, drain and service layouts, and approval requirements.
