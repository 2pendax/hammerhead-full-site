Cantilever Racks for Lumber Storage

Cantilever racks can separate lumber by species, grade, size and order while keeping long packs accessible to forklifts. The specification should account for pack dimensions, moisture, deformation, dunnage, weather exposure and the way partial packs are handled.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 23 November 2026

The short answer

Cantilever racks for lumber storage support timber packs, boards, panels and long wood products on projecting arms with an open loading face. Buyers should provide product type, length, width, pack height, maximum weight, moisture/seasoning condition, stiffness and allowed deformation, dunnage, partial-pack method, supports per load, arm length and spacing, levels, forklift or side-loader data, aisle, indoor/outdoor exposure, drainage, floor or foundation and installation scope.

Why use cantilever racks for lumber?

The open rack face allows forklifts or side-loaders to place long packs across several arms without front columns. Separate levels can organize sizes, grades, species or customer orders and reduce floor stacking.

For general components and configuration, see the cantilever racking system guide.

DimensionsMinimum/maximum length, pack width and height
WeightMaximum full and partial pack weight
ConditionMoisture/seasoning and packaging
SupportDunnage positions and deformation sensitivity
SeparationSpecies, grade, size, treatment or order
RotationReceipt date and stock-aging requirements

Use the heaviest credible condition, which may be wetter material rather than the dry shipping weight.

How should arms and dunnage support lumber packs?

  • Arm spacing suits the shortest and most deformation-sensitive product.
  • Dunnage aligns with suitable support points and remains stable.
  • Arm length supports pack depth without excessive projection.
  • Overhang is controlled for each length family.
  • Level clearance includes pack, forks and dunnage.
  • Partial packs remain stable and do not create loose projecting pieces.

Do not assume a pack will stay flat because it is rigid when new. Moisture, time and uneven dunnage can change its shape.

Where several dunnage lines are used, document their positions so packs are supported consistently during receiving, storage and dispatch. Misaligned supports can twist a pack or concentrate load on fewer arms than intended. Include spacer handling and replacement in the operating procedure instead of leaving dunnage placement to individual preference.

How does lumber handling affect rack layout?

  1. Record forklift or side-loader model, capacity and lift height.
  2. Define fork length and spread for each pack family.
  3. Check turning and long-load sweep through the full route.
  4. Reserve end clearance and staging space.
  5. Plan how partial packs are opened, re-banded or contained.
  6. Separate pedestrian and vehicle activity where practicable.

Aisle width must suit the vehicle, load and approach—not only the rack depth.

Outdoor use needs location-specific structural and foundation review. A roof or rack cover also changes interfaces and environmental design.

How should lumber locations and partial packs be managed?

  • Label species, grade, size, treatment and receipt date.
  • Keep unlike stock physically and visually separated.
  • Define safe containment for opened or partial packs.
  • Remove loose bands and dunnage from aisles.
  • Inspect wet, damaged or unstable packs before put-away.
  • Review new product dimensions before assigning a location.

The rack layout and warehouse system should use the same location rules so operators do not place short or heavy packs in unsuitable zones.

What should a lumber-storage rack quote include?

  • Lumber schedule and maximum load assumptions.
  • Plans/elevations with product zones, arms and levels.
  • Support spacing, dunnage and overhang rules.
  • Uprights, bases, bracing, anchors and protection.
  • Complete-system limits for each configuration.
  • Finish, cover and outdoor/foundation scope.
  • Engineering, freight, unloading, installation and inspection.
  • Final drawings, load notices and operating information.

Compare suppliers against the same lumber families, location count and service scope.

Cantilever racks for lumber storage: FAQs

Can wet and dry lumber use the same capacity assumption?

Do not assume so. Use the maximum credible pack weight and account for condition, support and product requirements.

How many supports does a lumber pack need?

It depends on length, stiffness, moisture, weight, dunnage and allowed deformation; provide actual product data.

Can short boards share a long-lumber row?

Only where the shorter pack reaches the required approved support points and remains stable.

Can lumber cantilever racks be used outdoors?

Potentially, when environmental actions, durability, drainage, foundations, cover and maintenance are addressed.

What improves a quotation?

Send lumber types, dimensions, maximum weights, moisture condition, dunnage, quantities, handling equipment, site and floor/foundation data, weather exposure, destination and installation scope.
