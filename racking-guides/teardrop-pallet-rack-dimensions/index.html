Teardrop Pallet Rack Dimensions

Teardrop pallet rack dimensions must be tied to clearly defined measurement points. Beam clear span is different from overall beam length; frame depth is different from pallet depth; bay width is different from run length; and upright height alone does not describe beam elevations, hole pitch, baseplates or required operating clearances.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 24 November 2026

The short answer

Record teardrop pallet rack dimensions with a labeled survey rather than a list of unlabeled numbers. Measure upright height, face width, side depth, material thickness and teardrop-hole pitch; frame depth between upright centerlines and overall outside depth; beam clear span between upright faces, overall beam length, beam profile and connector dimensions; baseplates, anchors, braces and row spacers; every beam-top elevation; pallet size, overhang and load height; bay and run widths; aisle, lift, sprinkler, ceiling and building clearances. Note units, tolerances and datum points. Dimensions help identify compatibility and plan layout, but they do not prove capacity; confirm the manufacturer, component series and engineering data separately.

How should a teardrop upright be measured?

  • Overall post height from its support datum to the cut end.
  • Face width and side depth at undamaged straight areas.
  • Material thickness using an appropriate method and allowing for coating.
  • Vertical pitch between equivalent points in consecutive teardrop holes.
  • Hole length, width, orientation and any alternating pattern.
  • Frame depth, brace pattern, brace section and connection locations.
  • Baseplate length, width, thickness and hole layout.
  • Splice or extension details and manufacturer markings.

Do not measure a dented or impact-deformed location as the nominal profile. Record damage separately.

Which teardrop beam dimensions identify a replacement?

  • Clear span between upright faces when installed.
  • Overall length, separating the structural beam body from connectors.
  • Beam face height, width and closed or open profile shape.
  • Material thickness and any step, ledge or decking seat.
  • Connector plate height, thickness, hook count and hook spacing.
  • Safety-lock type, position and engagement.
  • Weld arrangement and readable labels or stamped codes.

A beam with the same clear span and face height is not necessarily compatible. Connector geometry, steel specification, welds and manufacturer tolerances must also match.

Measure actual loaded pallets, including product bulge, packaging and irregular overhang. Nominal pallet dimensions may not represent the operating envelope.

Which clearances must be added around the rack?

  • Horizontal and vertical pallet placement clearances.
  • Lift-truck aisle width, turning envelope and mast reach.
  • Flue spaces and sprinkler clearances required by the project.
  • Ceiling, light, duct, pipe and cable-tray separation.
  • Column, wall, door, guard and egress clearances.
  • Floor-joint, slab-edge and embedded-service offsets.
  • Rack protector, barrier and pedestrian-route space.
  • Seismic separation or other locally required allowances.

Clearances are project inputs, not universal numbers. Confirm them with the equipment supplier and responsible fire, building and safety parties.

Field measurement workflow

  1. Assign a rack, row, bay and level identifier before measuring.
  2. Choose a consistent floor and horizontal datum.
  3. Record units, tool, date and surveyor.
  4. Photograph labels, connectors, locks, baseplates and the complete bay.
  5. Measure several nominally identical components to detect variation.
  6. Separate nominal dimensions from measured condition and damage.
  7. Mark obstructions, slopes, joints and services on the layout.
  8. Check the survey against original documents and resolve conflicts.
  9. Issue a controlled dimension schedule for design or procurement.

Why should a dimension schedule include tolerances?

Steel fabrication, coating, floor level, installation and measurement all introduce variation. A single rounded dimension can cause a replacement beam to bind, a connector to seat poorly or a run to exceed available space.

  • State whether dimensions are nominal, measured, minimum or maximum.
  • Keep enough precision to distinguish component series.
  • Record floor slope and rack plumb separately.
  • Use approved fabrication and installation tolerances.
  • Do not force incompatible parts to fit by grinding, bending, drilling or welding.

Teardrop pallet rack dimensions: common questions

Are teardrop pallet racks standard dimensions?

No single standard covers every manufacturer and series. Common sizes exist, but profiles, pitch, connectors, spans and tolerances vary.

Is beam length the clear opening?

Not necessarily. Specify whether the value is clear span, beam-body length or overall length including connectors.

How is frame depth measured?

It is often defined between front and rear upright centerlines, but confirm the project convention and also record overall outside depth.

Can dimensions prove two brands are compatible?

No. Dimensions assist identification; compatibility also depends on materials, connector behavior, welds, tolerances, locks and verified engineering data.

Do rack dimensions determine capacity?

No. Capacity additionally requires the exact component specification, loading, beam elevations, bracing, connections, anchors, floor and condition.

Information to send for layout or replacement review

Send dimensioned rack elevations and plans, clear beam spans, frame height and depth, upright and beam profiles, hole pitch, connector photographs, all beam-top elevations, pallet size and maximum weight, aisle equipment, building and sprinkler clearances, floor information and site photographs. State which measurements are nominal or field verified.
