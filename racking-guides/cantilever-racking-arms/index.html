Cantilever Racking Arms

Cantilever racking arms project from columns to support timber, pipe, steel, panels and other long loads without front uprights. Correct selection starts with the load and support layout, then verifies arm length, capacity, deflection, column connection, inclination, end restraint and handling clearance as one rack system.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 25 November 2026

Plan Your Rack Specification →
The short answer

Select cantilever racking arms from the maximum unit load, bundle length, depth, weight distribution, stiffness and centre of gravity. Decide how many columns and arms support each load, then calculate the reaction at every support rather than dividing total weight blindly. Confirm arm projection beyond the load bearing point, clear length, upward inclination if specified, end-stop type and usable clearance. Capacity must be verified for the exact arm section, connection, column system, projection and load position; a longer projection or load placed near the tip increases bending moment. Check arm deflection, local bearing, torsion from off-centre placement and the combined effects on columns, bases, anchors and bracing. Arms and connectors must match the rack system without field drilling, welding or unapproved substitution. Position loads across the intended arms, avoid impact and dragging, keep end stops within their rated purpose and inspect arms, welds, pins, bolts, column slots and stops for bending, cracks, looseness or displacement. Any change in product, spacing or arm level needs a renewed capacity and stability check.

How a cantilever arm carries long loads

The load bears on an arm, the arm transfers bending and shear through its connector into the column, and the column transfers combined actions through the base, bracing and anchors.

Support spacing and unequal arm loads

Long loads are not always rigid or uniformly weighted. Support reactions depend on product stiffness, support positions, overhang and centre of gravity. Do not assume every arm receives exactly the total weight divided by the arm count.

  • Map the actual bearing line on every arm.
  • Keep support spacing within the product's allowable span.
  • Check end overhang and bundle sag.
  • Model concentrated features and eccentric weight.
  • Allow for placement tolerance and missed supports.
  • Verify the worst credible reaction at an individual arm.
  • Confirm the product itself will not buckle, roll or become unstable.

Projection, load depth and usable clearance

Arm projection must support the defined load bearing area without creating unnecessary bending demand or handling interference. Distinguish overall arm length, clear projection from the column face and usable length inside an end stop.

  • Keep the load centre of gravity within the approved support zone.
  • Verify rear clearance from column, bracing and stored loads.
  • Include front end-stop thickness and required freeboard.
  • Check forks can enter and withdraw without striking the arm.
  • Avoid excessive projection beyond the supported load.
  • Recheck capacity whenever projection or load position changes.

Use traceable manufacturer or engineering data for the exact component and loading condition. An arm label alone does not establish the capacity of the entire rack bay.

Inclination, end stops and rolling loads

Some arms incline upward or use removable/fixed stops to help retain compatible loads. These features are not permission to push products against them or to store unstable round material without a designed restraint system.

  • Confirm the approved arm angle and its effect on usable height.
  • Match stop height to product geometry and handling clearance.
  • Verify stop, socket and arm capacity for the intended action.
  • Use cradles, dividers or restraints designed for rolling products.
  • Keep loose pieces bundled or contained.
  • Do not use end stops as routine forklift alignment targets.
  • Assess consequences to people and aisles if a load shifts.

Arm-to-column compatibility

  1. Identify the manufacturer, rack series and column face.
  2. Match hook, slot, bolt or pin geometry exactly.
  3. Confirm the required locking or retaining device.
  4. Install the arm in the approved orientation and level.
  5. Do not mix visually similar arms from another system.
  6. Never drill, flame-cut or field-weld without engineered approval.
  7. Check the column at the connector for tears, distortion and wear.
  8. Record arm identity and level capacity after installation.

Single-sided, double-sided and handling clearances

  • Verify aisle width for the actual forklift and longest load.
  • Provide lift clearance above arms and stored products.
  • Prevent rear loads from clashing across double-sided columns.
  • Coordinate arm levels with column bracing and restraints.
  • Check floor, anchors and base reactions for the chosen side loading.
  • Protect exposed columns and arm ends from credible impacts.
  • Separate pedestrians from long-load handling zones.
  • Maintain sprinkler, roof, wall and emergency clearances.

Common cantilever-arm selection mistakes

  • Dividing total load equally without checking product stiffness.
  • Using overall arm length instead of clear usable projection.
  • Applying a rating from a shorter arm to a longer projection.
  • Ignoring load centre, torsion and placement tolerance.
  • Treating an end stop as an impact-rated barrier.
  • Mixing arms and columns from different rack systems.
  • Changing levels without checking total column demand.
  • Ignoring bases, anchors, bracing and outdoor actions.
  • Dragging or dropping bundles onto arms.
  • Reusing a bent arm after visual straightening.

Cantilever racking arms: common questions

How is cantilever arm capacity selected?

Use the worst individual support reaction, load position, arm projection, connector and exact rack-system capacity data, then check the column and system.

How long should cantilever arms be?

The clear projection should support the approved load depth and centre of gravity while allowing end stops and handling clearance without unnecessary length.

Can total weight be divided by the number of arms?

Not automatically. Product stiffness, support positions, overhang and eccentric weight can create unequal reactions.

Do arm end stops prevent every load fall?

No. Stops have a defined purpose and capacity; unstable, rolling or loose loads may require designed cradles, dividers, bundling or restraint.

Can a cantilever arm be moved to another level?

Only after the new level, connection, clearances and combined column/system capacity are verified and the retaining device is correctly reinstalled.

Information to send for arm selection

Send product and bundle drawings, maximum weight and centre of gravity, material stiffness, desired support spacing and overhang, arm projection and levels, column/rack identification, connector details, end-stop needs, single/double-sided layout, forklift and aisle data, environment, floor and anchor information and photographs.
