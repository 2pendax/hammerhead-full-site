Drive Through Pallet Racking

Drive through pallet racking provides access at both ends of a multiple-depth lane. One face can load while the opposite face retrieves pallets.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 16 November 2026

Plan Your Rack Specification →
The short answer

Drive through pallet racking stores pallets one wide and multiple positions deep on side rails, with forklift access from both ends of each lane. A warehouse can load at one face and retrieve at the opposite face to support first-in, first-out flow. FIFO is not automatic: lane assignment, pallet movement, stock records and traffic rules must preserve the intended direction.

How does drive through pallet racking work?

Drive-through rack keeps both ends of the lane open. Pallets rest on rails running through the structure, while forklifts enter from the loading or retrieval aisle beneath the supported levels.

The RMI glossary describes drive-through rack as multiple-depth storage on arms and rails without the rear anchor section used in drive-in rack, allowing loading and unloading from both ends.

Does drive-through racking guarantee FIFO?

No. Two-ended access enables FIFO, but people and systems must keep pallets moving in the intended direction. Loading from the wrong face or mixing batches can break the sequence.

  1. Assign one loading face and one retrieval face.
  2. Label lane, level and flow direction clearly.
  3. Keep one compatible SKU and rotation group in each lane where practical.
  4. Record batch or date at receiving and at the storage location.
  5. Load the next permitted position without bypassing older stock.
  6. Audit blocked, mixed or incorrectly entered pallets.

Warehouse software should distinguish the lane positions and prevent an operator from treating both ends as unrestricted access. FIFO depends on the complete operating method.

Which products fit drive-through pallet storage?

The system is a candidate when repeated pallets require lane density and date or batch rotation benefits from two-ended flow.

Map peak pallets by SKU and batch into full lanes. A two-ended rack still loses usable positions when the stock mix cannot share lanes without blocking rotation.

How should loading and retrieval traffic be arranged?

Both lane faces need a defined equipment route, staging rule and pedestrian plan. Avoid creating a situation where trucks enter the same lane from opposite ends at the same time.

  • Define travel direction and right-of-way at each aisle.
  • Control lane occupancy so opposing equipment cannot meet inside the rack.
  • Keep staging pallets outside required clear routes and lane entries.
  • Provide lighting and location labels visible from both faces.
  • Separate people and vehicles according to the site risk assessment.
  • Protect exposed entries and create a rapid impact-reporting process.

SafeWork NSW transport guidance recommends planned traffic patterns, separation of people and moving plant, visible load signs and collision protection. Apply the requirements relevant to the actual location.

Which pallet, rail and forklift dimensions are required?

The rack supplier and equipment supplier should review one shared interface drawing for both lane faces.

  • Maximum pallet and load width, depth, height, weight and overhang.
  • Pallet underside, runner direction, bearing width and condition rules.
  • Truck width, mast, overhead guard, forks and load-centre data.
  • Rail elevation, clear distance and pallet set-down positions.
  • Upright, arm, guide and load clearances through the lane.
  • Floor condition, approach alignment and lift height at both faces.

Do not assume that a truck and pallet approved for one drive-in layout automatically fit a new drive-through layout. Verify the actual system geometry and operation.

How should usable drive-through capacity be calculated?

Count designed positions, then test whether inventory and rotation rules can use them. Reserve the second working aisle and all required building routes before comparing layouts.

  1. Draw lane quantities, depths and levels.
  2. Remove positions affected by columns, services and clearances.
  3. Reserve loading, retrieval, staging and cross-aisle space.
  4. Allocate peak stock by SKU and rotation group.
  5. Model throughput at both faces during the busiest period.
  6. Compare total project cost per usable position.
Illustrative arithmetic only

Eight lanes with four levels and six positions in depth contain 8 × 4 × 6 = 192 designed positions. This does not establish usable inventory capacity, structural capacity or achieved throughput.

Drive through pallet racking: common questions

Is drive-through rack always FIFO?

No. It supports FIFO when one face loads, the opposite face retrieves and the inventory system preserves that direction.

Can forklifts enter from both ends at once?

Lane occupancy and traffic procedures must prevent conflicting vehicle movements. Define how each lane is controlled.

Does it need two aisles?

It needs usable working access at both lane faces, which must be included in the dimensioned warehouse layout.

Can different batches share a lane?

Only when their sequence remains controlled and traceable. Mixing can block older stock or break FIFO.

What information improves the first design?

Send pallets and weights, quantities by SKU and batch, rotation rules, a floor plan, forklift details, throughput and site location.
