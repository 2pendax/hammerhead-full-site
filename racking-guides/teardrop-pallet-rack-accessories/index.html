Teardrop Pallet Rack Accessories

Teardrop pallet rack accessories include beam safety locks, wire decks, pallet supports, row spacers, dividers, back stops, guards, labels and containment products designed for particular rack interfaces. The teardrop name does not guarantee that an accessory fits every upright, beam or generation, so attachment geometry, load path and operating clearance must be confirmed.

The short answer

Select teardrop pallet rack accessories by defining the required function and positively identifying the upright, beam and rack generation. For safety clips or pins, match the beam connector and lock provision and confirm full engagement. For wire decks and pallet supports, match beam profile, step dimensions, front-to-rear spacing, pallet base and verified load distribution. For row spacers, dividers, back stops, mesh and guards, confirm attachment points, new horizontal or vertical forces, clearances, flues and rack/frame capacity. Protection anchored to the floor also requires compatible traffic clearance and verified supporting concrete. Labels and load notices must reflect the approved configuration and remain visible without hiding damage or locks. Obtain manufacturer documentation or qualified engineering where interchangeability is uncertain, install to the approved drawing, trial representative loads and inspect for displacement, loose fasteners, broken welds, impact and unapproved modification.

Accessory choice starts with its attachment

Teardrop accessories connect to different parts of the rack. Each interface has a different compatibility and load question.

Record model, generation and interfaces

  • Find manufacturer labels, stamps, drawings and purchase records.
  • Photograph upright holes, profile and frame bracing.
  • Record teardrop pitch, orientation and secondary slots.
  • Photograph beam connector hooks, plate, lock and step profile.
  • Measure frame depth and front-to-rear beam spacing.
  • Record existing spacers, decks, supports and guards.
  • Note approximate installation age and past extensions.
  • Do not assume parts from different generations are interchangeable.

Wire decks and pallet supports

  • Match deck edge and support channels to the exact beam step/profile.
  • Match panel depth to front-to-rear beam spacing.
  • Define pallet base, orientation, footprint and maximum weight.
  • Use verified ratings for the exact load distribution.
  • Do not apply an evenly distributed rating to concentrated feet without approval.
  • Confirm pallet-support bars seat and attach as specified.
  • Check beam and frame capacity separately.
  • Coordinate mesh, channels, pallets and flues with fire protection.

Back stops, guides and dividers

  • Define whether the accessory is a guide, stop, separator or engineered restraint.
  • Do not use a light stop for repeated uncontrolled pallet impact.
  • Check attachments for horizontal and torsional forces.
  • Keep pallet support and beam locks unobstructed.
  • Maintain flues and rear clearances.
  • Provide compatible cradles for rolling or unstable goods.
  • Check product and forklift clearance in normal and misaligned placement.
  • Investigate repeated contact as a root-cause issue.

Back mesh, netting and end containment

  • Define object size, weight, credible fall and protected area.
  • Verify mesh/net capacity and attachment to the rack or independent structure.
  • Keep required rear pallet clearance and flues.
  • Provide inspection and sprinkler access.
  • Do not allow containment to hide leaning or damaged pallets.
  • Review loads above workstations, walkways or public access.
  • Use qualified design when restraint forces enter rack frames.

Location labels and load notices

  • Use unique location codes visible from the operating position.
  • Match load notices to upright, beam, level and accessory configuration.
  • Show prohibited or limited pallet types where relevant.
  • Keep labels from covering holes, connectors, locks or damage.
  • Use durable materials suitable for the environment.
  • Update signs after an approved configuration or load change.
  • Archive the drawing or capacity data supporting each notice.

Decks, flues and sprinkler interfaces

Accessories can change rack openings and stored-product geometry. Coordinate the complete assembly with the approved fire-protection design.

  • Maintain longitudinal and transverse flues.
  • Confirm commodity, packaging, pallet and storage height.
  • Review wire and solid decks, back mesh and dividers.
  • Keep sprinklers, valves and inspection points accessible.
  • Do not add plywood, cardboard or sheet covers without review.
  • Control future accessory and product changes.

Common damage and mismatch signs

  • Missing, broken or wrong beam lock.
  • Deck or support not seated on the beam.
  • Bent row spacer, divider, stop or protector.
  • Loose, missing or improvised fastener.
  • Cracked weld, torn mesh or displaced net.
  • Accessory interfering with a connector, flue or aisle.
  • Repeated impact marks or product contact.
  • Unapproved drilling, cutting, welding or field adaptation.

Inspect the underlying rack after impact. Replacing a protector or accessory does not prove the frame, anchor or floor is undamaged.

Teardrop accessory selection workflow

  1. Define function, load, hazard and success criterion.
  2. Identify rack manufacturer, model and generation.
  3. Record upright, beam, lock and frame interfaces.
  4. Select the accessory using verified drawings and ratings.
  5. Check new forces, clearances, flues and fire effects.
  6. Confirm floor support for independent guards.
  7. Install to approved instructions.
  8. Trial representative loads and equipment.
  9. Update notices and inspection records.
  10. Control future substitutions and modifications.

Teardrop pallet rack accessories: common questions

Are teardrop rack accessories universal?

No. Upright holes, beam steps, locks, frame depths and attachment details vary by manufacturer, model and generation.

Can any safety clip fit a teardrop beam?

No. Use the approved device for the exact beam connector and confirm it fully engages after the beam is seated.

Can old wire decks fit replacement beams?

Only if beam profile, step, front-to-rear spacing, channels, edge form, load and capacity are verified for the combination.

Do row spacers increase rack capacity?

Do not assume so. Their positions and forces are part of the approved row configuration; capacity requires verified system data.

Can accessories from another brand be used?

Only after fit, attachment, load path, capacity, clearance and system effects are documented and approved.

Information to send for teardrop accessories

Send rack manufacturer/model and age, upright hole and profile photos, beam connector/step and lock details, frame depth and row gap, pallets and maximum loads, required accessory function, existing decks/supports, equipment and aisle clearances, floor data, flues, sprinklers and photos of damage.
