Teardrop Pallet Rack Uprights

Teardrop pallet rack uprights use repeated teardrop-style perforations to receive compatible hooked beam connectors. The familiar opening does not define the complete frame: hole pitch, orientation, column profile, material, bracing pattern, baseplate, frame height and depth, beam spacing and load configuration all affect compatibility and capacity.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 25 November 2026

The short answer

Select teardrop pallet rack uprights by identifying the exact rack system and treating the upright as a complete braced frame rather than a perforated column alone. Record teardrop hole shape, orientation and vertical pitch; column face, depth and bends; material and markings; frame height and depth; horizontal and diagonal brace pattern; splice, baseplate and anchor details. Define beam connector geometry, beam elevations, pallet and level loads, row arrangement, back-to-back ties, handling-equipment exposure and applicable site actions. Obtain verified frame capacity data or calculations for that exact configuration. A beam that hooks into the perforation does not prove that the connector bears correctly or that the frame is adequate. Inspect holes, columns, braces, fasteners, bases and anchors for impact, tears, dents, bow, twist, corrosion and alteration. Isolate serious or uncertain damage, use approved components and repairs, and document any substitution before loading.

A teardrop upright is more than its hole shape

Frame identity depends on several linked features. Matching only the perforation outline can produce an incomplete or unsafe interchange decision.

Perforation shape, pitch and condition

  • Photograph the hole pattern square-on with a scale.
  • Record vertical pitch between repeating locations.
  • Identify one or multiple perforation rows.
  • Record orientation and any secondary slots or lock holes.
  • Check which surfaces receive connector hooks.
  • Inspect elongation, tearing, crushing or burrs.
  • Compare several undamaged positions, not one impacted hole.
  • Do not drill or reshape perforations to accept another beam.

Column section and material identification

FeatureWhat to record
Column faceWidth, perforation rows and connector-bearing surfaces
Profile depthOverall depth, returns and bends
MaterialVerified thickness, grade/coating data and markings
Seams/joinsProfile construction and visible condition
SpliceLocation, plate, fasteners and approved detail
Base connectionColumn-to-baseplate weld or fastener arrangement

Field dimensions help identify a product but do not replace certified material and capacity data.

Height, depth and bracing pattern

  • Record overall frame height and clear depth.
  • Map horizontal and diagonal brace positions.
  • Identify brace section, orientation and end connection.
  • Check frame-width spacers or ties where included.
  • Record any lower-brace clearance zone provided by design.
  • Do not remove braces for pallet or forklift access.
  • Compare the installed pattern with approved frame drawings.
  • Check frame squareness, plumb and visible distortion.

Teardrop beam connector interface

  1. Record connector plate and hook count, shape and spacing.
  2. Check each hook bears on the intended upright surface.
  3. Confirm full seating without forcing or excessive play.
  4. Use the approved safety clip or lock.
  5. Inspect perforations and connectors together.
  6. Check beam offset, pallet support and accessory fit.
  7. Obtain verified compatibility and capacity approval.
  8. Document approved beam/upright combinations.

A successful trial fit confirms only part of the geometry; it does not establish frame or beam capacity.

Baseplates, anchors and supporting floor

  • Match baseplate dimensions and attachment to the upright system.
  • Obtain maximum frame reactions for floor/foundation review.
  • Use the specified anchor type, diameter, embedment and position.
  • Coordinate joints, slab edges, pits and embedded services.
  • Check approved shims or grout where applicable.
  • Inspect baseplate bending, weld damage and corrosion.
  • Investigate loose, missing, pulled or repeatedly damaged anchors.
  • Do not substitute a larger plate or extra anchor without engineering approval.

Why similar-looking frames can have different ratings

  • Profile bends and material data may differ.
  • Hole geometry changes the column section and connector bearing.
  • Brace pattern and connections affect frame stability.
  • Beam spacing changes unsupported length.
  • Baseplate and anchor details affect support behavior.
  • Rack height and row arrangement affect global stability.
  • Site actions and application rules vary.
  • Damage or alteration invalidates visual capacity assumptions.

Use manufacturer tables or qualified calculations for the exact upright, frame and rack configuration.

Controlled replacement or repair

  1. Unload affected bays and provide temporary stability as required.
  2. Record upright, bracing, base, beam levels and loads.
  3. Inspect connected beams, ties, anchors and adjacent frames.
  4. Obtain an identical approved frame where possible.
  5. For substitution, verify beams, locks, capacity, bases and row behavior.
  6. Follow an engineered sequence for dismantling and reinstallation.
  7. Inspect plumb, anchors, connections and protection.
  8. Update component, layout, load and repair records before release.

Protection and traffic controls

  • Provide compatible upright protection at assessed entry risks.
  • Use row-end barriers for cross-aisle or turning exposure where appropriate.
  • Keep protection from obstructing lower connectors or required aisle width.
  • Separate pedestrians and vehicles with defined routes.
  • Improve visibility at row ends, doors and intersections.
  • Investigate repeated strikes as a system problem.
  • Inspect the upright and anchor after protector impact.

Checks before loading

  • Correct upright profile, holes, frame height and depth.
  • Approved bracing pattern and complete fasteners.
  • Baseplates and anchors to the verified detail.
  • Frames plumb, aligned and spaced correctly.
  • Compatible beams, connectors and locks.
  • Beam levels and loads match frame capacity.
  • Ties, protection, flues and clearances complete.
  • Load notices and inspection baseline current.

Teardrop pallet rack uprights: common questions

Are all teardrop uprights interchangeable?

No. Hole pitch, profile, bracing, baseplate, beam connector interface and capacity can vary by manufacturer, model and generation.

Can any teardrop beam fit any teardrop upright?

No. Connector hooks, bearing, pitch, plate and safety lock must match, and the beam/frame capacity must be verified.

Can damaged teardrop holes be repaired?

Only with an approved manufacturer or engineered method. Unload and isolate uncertain damage; do not drill, weld or reshape holes informally.

Can brace patterns be changed?

Not without a verified redesign. Braces are part of frame stability and the approved capacity basis.

Can a replacement frame use a different profile?

Only after beam compatibility, loads, ties, bases, anchors, stability and capacity are documented and approved for the complete row.

Information to send for teardrop upright matching

Send upright face and profile photos with scale, hole pitch and orientation, manufacturer markings, frame height and depth, bracing pattern, baseplate and anchor details, beam connectors and locks, beam elevations and loads, row arrangement, floor data and photos of damage.
