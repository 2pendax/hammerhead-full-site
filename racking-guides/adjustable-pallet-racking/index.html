Adjustable Pallet Racking

Adjustable pallet racking lets approved beam levels be positioned for different pallet heights. Every change still needs a load and configuration review.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 16 November 2026

The short answer

Adjustable pallet racking is a pallet storage system with beams that connect to slotted uprights at selectable elevations. It can accommodate changing pallet heights when the proposed beam spacing, loads, components and clearances remain within an approved configuration. Adjustable does not mean unrestricted: review the revised arrangement before moving, adding or removing beam levels.

What makes pallet racking adjustable?

Most adjustable pallet rack uses upright frames with repeated connection points and removable beam pairs. This lets a documented system place supported levels at selected elevations instead of welding each beam permanently in one position.

The available slots show possible connection locations, not automatic approval for every layout. Upright capacity, unsupported column length, beam span, connector behaviour, load placement and the complete bay arrangement work together.

What process should be followed before moving beam levels?

Treat a beam move as a controlled configuration change. Record the current rack first, define the proposed loads and obtain the review required for the project location and identified system.

The SafeWork NSW pallet racking fact sheet says modifications should be made by a competent person with supplier information, account for load-limit effects and be approved by the manufacturer, supplier or a qualified engineer with relevant experience. It also calls for procedures, signs and drawings to be updated.

Why can a beam-height change affect rack capacity?

Beam spacing affects how the upright frame carries load. Adding another loaded level can also increase the total load assigned to a bay even when no individual pallet becomes heavier.

  • Unit load: the loaded pallet, including goods and packaging.
  • Beam level: the combined load and placement supported by one beam pair.
  • Bay load: the total assigned loading across the approved levels.
  • Vertical spacing: the distance between supported levels and from the floor to the first beam.

SafeWork NSW identifies maximum unit load and total bay capacity as critical limits and asks suppliers to provide the level and bay working limits plus maximum beam spacing information. Apply the standards and documentation relevant to the installation location.

Illustrative arithmetic only

Three pallets at 800 kg on a level equal 2,400 kg of stored mass. Adding a second identical level would assign 4,800 kg across those two levels. This arithmetic does not establish the permitted beam, upright or bay capacity.

What information is needed for an adjustable rack review?

Photographs help identify a rack, but they do not replace component records and the existing configuration drawing. Gather enough information to compare the current and proposed arrangements.

  1. Manufacturer, system name and component references if available.
  2. Current upright height and depth, bay width, beam sizes and beam elevations.
  3. Current and proposed pallet dimensions, maximum weights and underside details.
  4. Number of pallets and load placement on every level.
  5. Proposed beam elevations, added or removed levels and accessories.
  6. Forklift, lift height, aisle and building constraints.
  7. Existing drawings, load signs, inspection records and visible damage.

The Rack Manufacturers Institute FAQ explains that storage rack is a complete system of frames, beams, connections, rows, bays and levels. It also warns that similar-looking components from different manufacturers should not be assumed interchangeable.

How should buyers compare adjustable pallet rack proposals?

Ask each supplier to show both the starting layout and the rules for future changes. A quotation that only lists steel components leaves the operator without a clear configuration baseline.

Adjustable pallet racking: common questions

Can warehouse staff move pallet rack beams themselves?

Do not move them as an informal adjustment. Follow the site's change process and obtain the required review for the identified system before work begins.

Does moving a beam higher increase capacity?

Not automatically. The complete upright, beam spacing, connection and loading arrangement must be reviewed; a visual change does not establish capacity.

Can an extra beam level be added if there is space?

Only after the additional level, pallets, bay load, clearances and proposed configuration have been reviewed and documented.

Can beams from another rack brand be used?

Similar appearance or physical engagement is not proof of compatibility. Use identified components approved for the rack system or obtain the necessary documented engineering review.

What should be changed after an approved reconfiguration?

Update the configuration drawing, load information, signs, location records and operating instructions, then inspect the completed work.
