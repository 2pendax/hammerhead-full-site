Structural Pallet Rack Capacity Chart

A structural pallet rack capacity chart is valid only for the manufacturer, product series, steel specification, component codes, connection details and design assumptions printed with it. The chart is a lookup tool—not a universal rating—and must match the installed beam span, upright frame, beam elevations, loading, anchors, floor support and rack condition.

The short answer

Use a structural pallet rack capacity chart by first verifying the chart source, revision, units, design criteria and component series. Identify the exact structural beam, connector, upright column, bracing, baseplate and anchors. For beams, match clear span, section, load distribution and deflection criterion. For frames, match column section, frame height, bracing and every beam elevation because vertical spacing changes unbraced length and capacity. Then check maximum pallet weight, positions per level, total bay load, connection limits, anchors and floor reactions. Apply all footnotes and reductions. Do not interpolate unless the chart author permits it, do not mix manufacturer data, and do not use a chart for damaged or modified rack. If any required identity or configuration input is missing, treat capacity as unverified and obtain project-specific engineering review.

How should a structural rack capacity chart be used?

A reliable lookup begins with identity and ends with a controlled load notice. Skipping directly to the largest number can select the wrong row, column or capacity type.

Which capacity chart are you reading?

ChartTypical inputsTypical output
Beam tableBeam series, clear span and load arrangementPermitted load per beam pair
Column tableColumn section and unbraced lengthColumn axial capacity under stated criteria
Frame tableUpright, bracing, frame height and beam spacingMaximum frame or bay load
Connector tableConnector, hooks, weld and engagementEnd-connection reaction or limit
Base/anchor scheduleReactions, baseplate, anchor and concrete assumptionsApproved support detail

These outputs are not interchangeable. A beam-pair value does not prove upright-frame, connector, anchor or floor capacity.

What makes a capacity chart traceable?

  • Manufacturer or responsible engineering organization.
  • Product series and exact component designations.
  • Issue date, revision number and superseded-document control.
  • Units, material grades and design method.
  • Applicable region, loading criteria and safety factors.
  • Deflection or serviceability limits where relevant.
  • Footnotes, exclusions and required accessories.
  • Approval, authorship or other required technical responsibility.

A cropped screenshot with no header or footnotes is not adequate evidence. Keep the complete source document with the project record.

How do you read structural pallet rack beam capacity?

  1. Identify the beam section, material and connector code.
  2. Measure clear span between upright faces.
  3. Confirm whether the chart rating is per beam, beam pair or level.
  4. Match the number and location of pallets or other point loads.
  5. Apply the stated distribution, support-bar and decking assumptions.
  6. Check strength, deflection and connector limits.
  7. Use the lower applicable value and retain all footnotes.

Do not assume that an evenly distributed load rating applies to a few concentrated pallet runners. Do not extend a shorter-span value to a longer beam.

Why does a frame chart require beam-level spacing?

Beam connections restrain upright columns. The distance from floor to first beam and between later beam levels affects unbraced length, stability and the permitted frame load.

InputWhy it matters
Upright sectionArea, shape, thickness, grade and perforations
Frame heightOverall stability and bracing demand
First-beam heightOften controls the lower column segment
Subsequent spacingDefines restraint and column effective lengths
Bracing patternTransfers frame forces and stabilizes uprights
Load distributionDetermines reactions across levels and frames

Moving beams after approval can invalidate the selected chart row even if the total stored weight does not increase.

Capacity-chart footnotes that can change the answer

  • Maximum permitted beam spacing or first-beam elevation.
  • Required minimum number of beam levels.
  • Evenly distributed load versus specified point loads.
  • Mandatory safety locks, ties, row spacers or bracing.
  • Deflection criterion and cosmetic or functional limits.
  • Reductions for seismic demand, height, impact or environment.
  • Anchor, baseplate and concrete assumptions.
  • Prohibition on interpolation, component substitution or field modification.

When should a chart not be used?

  • The rack manufacturer or component series is unknown.
  • Beam, connector or upright codes do not match.
  • The chart revision, units or footnotes are missing.
  • Span or beam spacing falls outside tabulated limits.
  • Loads are concentrated, dynamic or arranged differently from the chart.
  • Components from different systems have been mixed.
  • Frames are damaged, corroded, modified or out of plumb.
  • Anchors or floor support differ from the approved assumptions.

In these cases, obtain traceable data or a project-specific calculation rather than choosing the nearest-looking value.

From chart lookup to safe operation

  1. Survey and identify the installed rack.
  2. Confirm maximum unit loads and positions.
  3. Complete and check the lookup worksheet.
  4. Verify connections, bracing, anchors and supporting floor.
  5. Resolve damage and unauthorized modifications.
  6. Complete required professional review and approval.
  7. Post a load notice defining per-pallet, per-level and per-bay limits.
  8. Train operators and require review before any change.

Keep the source chart, worksheet, drawings, inspection and approval together so future changes can be assessed against the same evidence.

Structural pallet rack capacity chart: common questions

Is there one universal structural pallet rack capacity chart?

No. Capacity data is specific to a manufacturer, series, component specification, configuration and design basis.

Can I interpolate between two chart values?

Only if the chart author explicitly permits a defined interpolation method. Otherwise use an approved tabulated case or obtain a calculation.

Does a beam chart include upright capacity?

Usually not. Beam pair, connector, frame, anchor and floor limits must be checked separately.

Can a chart be used after moving beam levels?

Only after the new elevations are checked because beam spacing changes upright restraint and frame capacity.

Can a competitor’s chart be used for similar structural rack?

No. Similar appearance or dimensions do not prove matching steel, perforations, connectors, welds, bracing or design criteria.

Information to send for capacity-chart review

Send the full capacity chart, manufacturer and series, component labels, beam and upright dimensions, clear spans, frame height and depth, every beam elevation, maximum pallet weights and positions, connector and base photographs, anchor details, floor information, site location and inspection records.
