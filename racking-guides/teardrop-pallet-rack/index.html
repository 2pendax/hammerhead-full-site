Teardrop Pallet Rack

Teardrop pallet rack is named for the repeated teardrop-shaped openings in its upright columns. Connection shape alone does not prove component compatibility.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 16 November 2026

Plan Your Rack Specification →
The short answer

Teardrop pallet rack is a selective pallet rack style whose upright columns have repeated teardrop-shaped openings for beam-end connectors. The boltless connection can support quick assembly and beam-level adjustment, while a lock helps prevent accidental beam disengagement. Products that look like teardrop rack are not automatically interchangeable; identify the manufacturer and obtain documented compatibility, configuration and load information.

What is a teardrop pallet rack connection?

The name describes the upright-column punching pattern. A beam-end connector engages the column openings, and the beam pair supports a pallet level. The precise pin, connector plate, column profile and locking device belong to the manufacturer's system.

Teardrop describes the connection family, not a universal component standard or a guaranteed capacity. Read the supplied drawings and installation instructions for the identified product.

Which parts make up a teardrop pallet rack bay?

A useful proposal identifies the complete system rather than calling every part simply "teardrop rack."

Are all teardrop pallet rack components interchangeable?

No. A similar hole and connector shape is not documented compatibility. The connection properties used in rack analysis can depend on details that are not obvious from a warehouse photo.

The RMI FAQ on component interchange states that manufacturers commonly use proprietary column shapes, punching patterns and mating beam connectors. It warns that apparently compatible products are not automatically interchangeable and calls for a qualified design engineer to determine whether components can be connected.

  1. Look for labels, stamps, stencils and original purchase records.
  2. Photograph the column, punching, beam connector, lock, base plate and bracing.
  3. Record component dimensions without guessing a manufacturer.
  4. Compare references with manufacturer documentation.
  5. Obtain the required compatibility and configuration review before mixing parts.
Simple rule

"It fits" describes assembly. "It is approved for this load and configuration" requires documentation.

What load information should a teardrop rack quotation include?

Do not transfer a capacity from a different beam, span or configuration. Provide the maximum loaded pallet and show how every level is intended to be used.

  • Pallet: overall width, depth, height, maximum weight and underside.
  • Beam level: beam length, number of pallets, placement and total level load.
  • Bay: beam elevations and combined loading across approved levels.
  • Frame: height, depth, bracing and floor-to-first-beam spacing.
  • Site: layout, equipment, installation address and applicable project requirements.

A manufacturer's published beam table applies only to its stated product and conditions. For example, the Ridg-U-Rak teardrop product page distinguishes beam profiles and notes that its displayed ratings assume uniformly distributed loading. Those values do not establish the capacity of another supplier's rack.

For a broader load brief, see heavy duty pallet racking.

How should teardrop pallet rack offers be compared?

Ask suppliers to quote from the same pallet schedule and floor plan. Compare usable positions and documentation, not only the number of frames and beams.

  1. Identification: manufacturer, product series and component references.
  2. Layout: rows, bays, aisles, working areas and pallet positions.
  3. Configuration: beam elevations, supports, locks and accessories.
  4. Loading: stated pallet, level and bay assumptions.
  5. Project scope: freight, unloading, installation and handover responsibilities.
  6. Future changes: procedure for reviewing beam moves and replacement parts.

If direct pallet access is the main question, compare the selective pallet racking guide. If later beam moves are the focus, use the adjustable pallet racking change guide.

What should operators check after installation?

Follow the manufacturer and site inspection program. Routine observations should make missing locks, disengaged beams, impact damage and unauthorised changes easy to report.

  • Locks are present and engaged as intended.
  • Beam connectors are seated and show no obvious damage.
  • Uprights and bracing have not been struck, bent or altered.
  • Load signs remain legible and match the current configuration.
  • Stored pallets match the approved load information.
  • Changes and damage are reported rather than improvised.

When structural condition is uncertain, isolate the affected area according to site procedures and arrange an appropriate evaluation. This page is a buying and identification guide, not an inspection standard.

Teardrop pallet rack: common questions

Why is it called teardrop pallet rack?

The upright column openings resemble a teardrop. Beam-end connectors engage the repeated openings to form supported levels.

Is teardrop rack the same as selective pallet rack?

Teardrop describes a connection style. It is commonly used in selective pallet rack, while selective describes the direct-access storage arrangement.

Can any teardrop beam fit any teardrop upright?

Do not assume so. Identify both products and obtain documented compatibility and configuration review before combining components.

What does the beam safety lock do?

It helps prevent accidental beam disengagement. Use the lock specified for the identified system and replace missing or damaged parts correctly.

What should I send for a replacement beam enquiry?

Send manufacturer and component references, clear connector and upright photos, exact dimensions, current configuration, pallet loads and site location.
