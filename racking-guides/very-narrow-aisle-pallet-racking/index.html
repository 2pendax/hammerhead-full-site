Very Narrow Aisle Pallet Racking

Very narrow aisle pallet racking combines compact storage rows with specialist handling equipment. Truck, guidance, floor and transfer areas need one coordinated plan.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 16 November 2026

Plan Your Rack Specification →
The short answer

Very narrow aisle pallet racking is a selective storage arrangement served by specialist equipment designed for tight travel corridors. A turret truck can handle pallets to either side without turning its complete body for each placement. The installation may use rail or wire guidance, depending on the chosen truck and application. Set dimensions and floor requirements from the equipment review, then coordinate rack geometry, pallet loads and transfer operations.

What makes a VNA installation different?

The compact aisle is one part of the project. Equipment movement, guidance interfaces, floor condition, pallet clearance and the handoff at each aisle entrance determine whether the warehouse can use the planned storage.

Compare the broader narrow aisle layout guide before deciding whether a specialist VNA installation is justified.

Which equipment should serve VNA pallet positions?

Separate full-pallet storage from case or item picking. A truck described as an order picker may serve a different task from a full-pallet turret truck.

  • Maximum load and its dimensions.
  • Required capacity at actual lift height and load centre.
  • Fork, turret or attachment movement envelope.
  • Operator position and visibility.
  • Required guidance and floor conditions.
  • Charging, maintenance and service access.

Toyota's turret truck description illustrates a rotating head that handles loads to the left, right and centre. Its published ratings belong to that product and do not establish suitability for another application.

Toyota's guidance explanation describes both mechanical rail guidance and sensor-based following of a powered floor guidewire. Guidance helps constrain travel; it does not by itself establish autonomous operation or remove the need for a site operating plan.

Why should the floor be reviewed before ordering rack?

Obtain the truck supplier's floor flatness, levelness and tolerance requirements for the proposed height and operating conditions. Agree the survey method and who evaluates the results.

  1. Collect existing slab records and floor condition information.
  2. Confirm the intended truck and equipment requirements.
  3. Survey the relevant travel and transfer areas.
  4. Identify any remedial work and its acceptance criteria.
  5. Coordinate rails, guidewire, rack bases and other floor interfaces.
  6. Record the accepted condition before commissioning.

Do not assume a visually smooth floor meets a specialist equipment requirement. An early survey helps expose civil-work scope before the rack and truck are delivered.

How should pallets move into and out of the VNA area?

A compact storage corridor still needs workable transfer space. Decide whether the VNA truck travels outside its aisles or exchanges pallets with other equipment at designated locations.

  • Define replenishment and retrieval handoff positions.
  • Reserve turning and approach space.
  • Keep waiting pallets outside required clear routes.
  • Coordinate truck and pedestrian movements.
  • Include peak receiving and dispatch demand.
  • Plan equipment charging and service without blocking access.

Evaluate the complete movement from receiving to storage and dispatch. A fast storage cycle will not solve a bottleneck at a crowded transfer point.

Which load and rack details belong in the shared drawing?

Use one approved load envelope for both the rack and equipment application. Include pallet overhang and packaging as well as the pallet footprint.

  • Pallet width, depth, underside and maximum loaded weight.
  • Load height, stability and centre-of-gravity information.
  • Beam spans, elevations and supported positions.
  • Frame height, depth and protection details.
  • Clear travel boundaries and guide interfaces.
  • Building columns, services and access constraints.

Changes to pallets or goods may affect clearances even if the rack remains unchanged. Keep the permitted load dimensions available to receiving and warehouse teams.

Compare total project scope rather than rack steel alone. Ask each supplier to state exclusions, unresolved inputs and responsibility for coordinating the truck, floor, guidance and rack.

Count usable positions from the final plan and test peak inventory allocation. Additional rack height or reduced aisle width does not guarantee a particular capacity gain or dispatch rate.

Very narrow aisle pallet racking: common questions

Is there one correct VNA aisle width?

No. Confirm the exact truck, load, guidance, placement movement and site requirements.

Can a normal counterbalance truck use VNA rack?

Do not assume it can. The equipment supplier must confirm the intended operation in the available clear space.

Does wire guidance mean the truck is autonomous?

No. A guidance function and an automated operating system are different capabilities. Confirm the supplied controls.

Is a floor survey useful before purchasing?

Yes. Compare measured conditions with the selected equipment requirements and allocate any remedial scope.

What information improves a first proposal?

Send pallet and load data, target height, inventory, throughput, building and floor records, equipment preferences and location.
