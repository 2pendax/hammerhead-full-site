Pallet Rack Wire Decking

Pallet rack wire decking spans between rack beams and may support approved loads or help control fall-through. It is not automatically suitable for every pallet or carton: beam profile, deck dimensions, support-channel orientation, load footprint, point loading and fire-protection requirements must match the verified application.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 25 November 2026

The short answer

Select pallet rack wire decking by identifying the beam type and clear opening, then measuring the required deck width, depth, waterfall or edge fit and support-channel seating. Define the exact load: pallet dimensions and base direction, maximum weight, contact points, overhang, whether pallets bear primarily on the rack beams, and whether loose cartons or other items will be placed on the mesh. Obtain verified capacity for the specific deck, support channels, span and load distribution; do not apply an evenly distributed rating to concentrated feet or small cartons without confirmation. Check compatibility with step or box beams, pallet supports, dividers and back restraints. Coordinate mesh, channels and stored commodities with the fire-protection design rather than assuming that wire deck alone solves sprinkler obstruction. Install decks in the approved orientation, keep them seated, post limits where required, and inspect for broken welds, bent channels, permanent sag, corrosion, displacement, overload and unauthorized cutting.

How wire decking transfers load

Load transfer depends on the pallet and deck design. In many rack arrangements, an approved pallet bears on the front and rear beams while the wire deck provides additional support or fall-through control. Other approved loads may bear through mesh and support channels into the beams.

Beam type and opening measurements

  • Identify step beam, box beam or other system-specific profile.
  • Measure clear distance between the front and rear beam faces.
  • Measure beam length and the deck's intended bay position.
  • Record step dimensions or seating surfaces.
  • Check beam elevations and level configuration.
  • Identify pallet supports, back stops or dividers already present.
  • Confirm the wire-deck supplier's fit drawing for the exact beam arrangement.

Load rating and distribution

  • Use verified data for the exact deck width, depth, mesh, wire and support channels.
  • Match the rating's beam spacing and seating condition.
  • Understand whether the published load is evenly distributed or based on a specific pallet.
  • Do not place small feet, wheels or point loads on mesh without explicit approval.
  • Check how multiple pallets or cartons divide across deck panels.
  • Verify rack beam and frame capacity separately.
  • Account for impact, dropping or sliding only if included in the design basis.
  • Do not stack extra panels to claim higher capacity.

Beam-bearing pallets versus deck-supported loads

Define whether the pallet is intended to bridge and bear on both rack beams. Wire deck should not be used to make an unsuitable pallet base appear compatible without verified design.

  1. Inspect representative pallet bases and runners.
  2. Confirm the approved orientation in the rack.
  3. Check front and rear beam bearing.
  4. Check the relationship to deck support channels.
  5. Identify broken, undersized or nonstandard pallets.
  6. Specify extra pallet supports only where approved for the system.
  7. Reject or re-palletize incompatible loads under operating rules.

Mesh, stored commodities and sprinkler design

Open mesh may allow more water passage than solid decking, but fire-protection adequacy depends on the complete storage arrangement. Do not select wire deck from an assumed universal open-area rule.

  • Confirm commodity, packaging, pallet type and storage height.
  • Coordinate mesh, channels and pallet loads with sprinkler design.
  • Maintain required longitudinal and transverse flues.
  • Review in-rack sprinklers, obstructions and clearances.
  • Do not cover mesh with cardboard, plywood or sheet material without review.
  • Control future product or packaging changes.
  • Obtain qualified fire review and local approval where required.

Installation orientation and seating

  • Install the deck with channels in the specified direction.
  • Seat channel ends and waterfalls fully on the intended beam surfaces.
  • Keep panels square without rocking or excessive gaps.
  • Use approved fasteners or retention where specified.
  • Do not force an oversized panel or leave an undersized panel unsupported.
  • Keep deck edges from interfering with beam connectors or safety locks.
  • Verify pallet-clearance and flue requirements after installation.
  • Do not cut mesh or channels on site without an approved detail.

Wire-deck damage and warning signs

  • Broken welds between wires or channels.
  • Bent, twisted or detached support channel.
  • Permanent sag or local collapse.
  • Panel displaced from the beam.
  • Cut wires, enlarged openings or site modification.
  • Corrosion, heat or chemical damage.
  • Repeated impact from pallets or forks.
  • Loads with small feet pressing into mesh.
  • Cardboard, plywood or debris covering the deck.

Unload questionable panels and follow the site damage procedure. Replace with a verified compatible deck; do not straighten or weld without an approved repair method.

Checks before loading

  1. Confirm beam type, bay opening and deck model.
  2. Verify panel dimensions, channel count and orientation.
  3. Check full seating and specified retention.
  4. Confirm pallet or product load and distribution.
  5. Verify beam, frame and rack-level capacity.
  6. Check flues, sprinklers and fire-protection basis.
  7. Remove damaged panels and incompatible pallets.
  8. Record the approved arrangement and inspection baseline.

Pallet rack wire decking: common questions

Does wire decking increase pallet-rack capacity?

Not automatically. Beam and frame capacity remain separate, and deck capacity applies only to the verified deck, span, seating and load arrangement.

Can cartons be placed directly on wire deck?

Only when the deck is rated for their footprint and distribution, the mesh contains them, and product, ergonomic and fire requirements are satisfied.

Which way should deck channels face?

Follow the specific deck design and supplier drawing. Channel orientation and seating affect the load path and cannot be guessed.

Can wire deck be cut to fit?

Do not cut wires or support channels without an approved engineered or manufacturer detail; modification can invalidate capacity and corrosion protection.

Can plywood be placed over wire decking?

Not without review. It can change load distribution, fire behavior, sprinkler water passage and housekeeping conditions.

Information to send when specifying wire decking

Send rack and beam system details, clear front-to-rear opening, beam length and profile, deck photos or drawings, pallet dimensions, base and orientation, maximum weights, loose-item footprints, supports or accessories, stored commodities, sprinkler information and photos of any damage.
