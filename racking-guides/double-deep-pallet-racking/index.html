Double Deep Pallet Racking

Double deep pallet racking stores one pallet behind another at each lane and level. It reduces aisles but also reduces immediate access to rear pallets.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 16 November 2026

Plan Your Rack Specification →
The short answer

Double deep pallet racking stores pallets two positions deep from one aisle face. The front pallet must usually be removed before the rear pallet can be reached, so the layout trades some selectivity for fewer aisles and potentially more usable positions. It requires equipment able to handle the specified pallet at the rear position and an inventory plan that keeps compatible pallets together.

How does double deep pallet racking work?

Each storage lane has a front position and a rear position. In a single-sided row, the forklift reaches both from the same aisle. Back-to-back arrangements can create four pallet positions across the combined depth.

The MHI rack overview describes double-deep storage as accommodating two pallet loads in depth. Treat it as a system of rack, equipment and inventory rules.

Which stock profile fits double deep storage?

Double deep works best when enough pallets of the same or compatible stock can share front and rear positions without creating excessive reshuffling.

What forklift is needed for double deep pallet racking?

The chosen equipment must place and retrieve the actual load at both the required depth and lift height. A standard reach truck should not be assumed to reach the rear position.

  1. Provide maximum pallet weight, width, depth, height and load centre information.
  2. Define front and rear placement dimensions from the proposed rack drawing.
  3. Confirm equipment capacity at the required lift height and reach condition.
  4. Check mast, pantograph or telescopic mechanism, outriggers and pallet supports.
  5. Obtain aisle and operating-clearance requirements for the exact truck and load.
  6. Plan battery, charging, maintenance, training and traffic requirements.

Toyota's reach-truck specification example lists separate double-reach and single-reach models with different stated capacities and dimensions. It illustrates why the exact equipment model must be checked; its values do not define another project.

Does double deep rack always double storage capacity?

No. Reducing aisles may create room for more rack, but columns, cross aisles, staging, equipment, building services and SKU allocation affect the final result.

  • Develop single-deep and double-deep layouts for the same storage area.
  • Count positions from the dimensioned drawings.
  • Remove positions blocked by site constraints.
  • Map peak inventory into front and rear locations.
  • Check the equipment route and operating throughput.
  • Compare total project cost per usable position.
Illustrative arithmetic only

If a single-deep option has 500 usable positions and a double-deep option has 650, the increase is 150 positions or 30%. That result belongs only to that example; it does not establish a universal density gain.

How are pallets supported in double deep rack?

The proposed rack must show where each pallet bears and how it is placed at the rear position. Pallet dimensions, underside construction, deflection, load distribution and equipment movement all affect the support choice.

  • Record pallet type and underside photos.
  • Show the front and rear pallet set-down locations.
  • Identify beams, pallet supports or decking by location.
  • Confirm clearances between load, rack and adjacent pallet.
  • Address damaged, weak or non-standard pallets in operating rules.

Do not add an accessory merely because it is marketed for double-deep rack. The support must be specified with the actual pallet, beams and placement. Steel King's double-deep product page is one manufacturer example that identifies special handling equipment and optional supports; those details remain product-specific.

What operating rules change with two-deep storage?

Warehouse software and location labels should distinguish front and rear positions. Operators need a clear rule for which pallet moves first and where a front pallet may wait during rear retrieval.

  1. Label front and rear locations separately.
  2. Assign compatible stock to paired positions.
  3. Prevent temporary pallets from blocking aisles and escape routes.
  4. Define staging for the front pallet during rear access.
  5. Record damage, misplacement and blocked locations promptly.
  6. Review allocation when SKU quantities or pallet dimensions change.

Measure both position use and extra handling. A denser plan may underperform if frequent rear access creates too many relocations or waiting moves.

Double deep pallet racking: common questions

Can every pallet be accessed directly?

No. The front pallet normally blocks direct access to the rear pallet in the same lane and level.

Does double deep rack require a special forklift?

It requires equipment confirmed for the rear reach, load, lift height and aisle. A suitable double-reach or comparable solution is commonly evaluated.

Is double deep storage FIFO?

It does not provide automatic FIFO movement. The warehouse must define allocation and rotation rules for front and rear pallets.

Can different SKUs share one two-deep lane?

They can only be paired when access and rotation requirements remain workable. Frequent rear retrieval may create extra handling.

What information improves the first layout?

Send pallet dimensions and weights, quantities by SKU, a floor plan, throughput needs, equipment details, lift height and site location.
