Cantilever Racking for Pipe Storage

Cantilever racking gives forklifts or cranes open access to long pipe and tube, but round materials introduce rolling and containment risks. The specification must separate loose pipe, strapped bundles and stillages, then define support and handling for each case.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 23 November 2026

Plan Your Rack Specification →
The short answer

Cantilever racking for pipe storage uses projecting arms to support long pipe or tube across multiple uprights without front columns blocking loading. A buyer should list pipe length, outside diameter, wall/material, maximum bundle or stillage weight, loose or strapped condition, stiffness, allowed overhang, arms per load, arm length and spacing, level quantities, rolling or end-restraint needs, forklift/crane method, aisle, floor or foundation, indoor/outdoor environment and installation scope.

Why use cantilever rack for pipe and tube?

The open loading face lets long pipe span several arms and be placed or removed without front posts between positions. It can organize pipe by diameter, grade, length or order status while keeping material off the floor.

For general components and layouts, review the cantilever racking system guide.

Which pipe details should the buyer provide?

InputRequired detail
LengthMinimum, typical and maximum pipe or bundle length
DiameterOutside diameter range and nested condition
WeightMaximum bundle, stillage or loose-group weight
FormLoose, strapped bundle, cradle or stillage
StiffnessMaterial and sensitivity to support spacing
SurfaceFinish, coating or damage concerns
IdentificationGrade, heat, batch or order separation needs

Do not combine unlike pipe groups into one average. Identify the governing weight and the flexible product that may govern support spacing.

How should loose pipe and bundles be restrained?

Round products can roll or spread when banding is removed. The approved storage condition should state whether pipe remains bundled, sits in a cradle or needs suitable arm-end or other containment details.

  • Define who removes or replaces bands and where.
  • Do not rely on improvised blocks or loose packing.
  • Keep restraints compatible with loading and withdrawal.
  • Prevent individual pieces from falling into aisles.
  • Separate unstable or partially used bundles until safely contained.

The restraint is part of the operating method. It must not be removed simply because it obstructs an unplanned handling approach.

How are arm length and upright spacing chosen?

  • Arm length supports the bundle depth without excessive projection.
  • Upright spacing limits pipe sag and provides stable support.
  • Arms per load suit the minimum and maximum stored lengths.
  • Overhang stays within the approved arrangement.
  • Vertical level spacing includes pipe height, restraint and handling clearance.
  • Arm and upright loads are checked for every zone.

Short pipe stored on a layout designed only for long pipe may have too few supports. Define approved locations by length family.

Forklift, side-loader or crane?

MethodKey questions
Side-loaderFork spread, lift height, travel aisle and bundle stability
ForkliftTurning, approach, long-load sweep and end clearance
CraneTop access, sling method, building clearance and exclusion zone
Manual piecesUnit weight, reach, restraint and team handling

Map delivery, staging, placement and withdrawal. Define whether pipe is moved as a complete bundle, stillage or individual pieces.

What floor and environment details are required?

  • Slab or foundation records, joints, edges and embedded services.
  • Base and anchor locations with any foundation work.
  • Indoor or outdoor exposure, drainage and corrosion environment.
  • Material protection from water, contamination or surface damage.
  • Aisle, rack-end and pedestrian controls.
  • Inspection and maintenance access.

Outdoor storage requires review of environmental actions, foundations, drainage and durability for the actual location.

What should a pipe-storage rack quote include?

  • Pipe schedule and maximum load assumptions.
  • Plans/elevations with approved pipe zones, arms and levels.
  • Arm spacing, length, restraints and allowed overhang.
  • Uprights, bases, bracing, anchors and protection.
  • Component and complete-system limits.
  • Floor/foundation and outdoor work required from others.
  • Engineering, freight, unloading, installation and inspection scope.
  • Final drawings, load notices and operating information.

Compare suppliers against the same pipe schedule and handling method, not a simple price per bay.

How should pipe storage be controlled after installation?

  1. Label approved length, weight and storage zones.
  2. Keep pipe contained in the documented condition.
  3. Inspect arms, restraints, uprights, bases and anchors.
  4. Report impact, deformation or displaced components.
  5. Isolate unsafe areas until reviewed.
  6. Review new pipe profiles before storage.

Do not change arm levels or remove restraints without checking the revised configuration and operating method.

Cantilever racking for pipe storage: FAQs

Can loose pipe be stored on ordinary flat arms?

Only when the approved design and operating method provide suitable containment for the pipe condition and loading process.

How many supports does a pipe bundle need?

It depends on length, stiffness, weight, bundle form and allowed overhang; provide actual material data for project review.

Can different pipe lengths share one row?

Potentially, when each length family has approved support positions, loads and identification.

Can pipe rack be installed outdoors?

Potentially, when environmental actions, durability, drainage, foundations or floor and maintenance are addressed.

What improves a quotation?

Send pipe lengths, diameters, materials, maximum weights, loose/bundled condition, quantities, handling equipment, desired positions, site and floor/foundation data, environment, destination and installation scope.
