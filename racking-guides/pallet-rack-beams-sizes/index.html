Pallet Rack Beams Sizes

Pallet rack beams sizes describe more than one dimension. A replacement or new level may require the clear span between upright faces, overall beam length, beam-section height and depth, connector plate and hooks, step dimensions, pallet opening and installed elevation. Measuring only the front face can produce a beam that fits poorly or has the wrong capacity.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 25 November 2026

The short answer

Record pallet rack beams sizes with a dimensioned sketch and the rack system identity. Measure the clear span between the inside faces of the uprights at the beam level, then record overall length including connector plates, beam-section height and front-to-back depth, step or ledge dimensions, connector plate height and width, hook count and spacing, safety-lock feature and beam elevation. Separately define the required pallet opening from pallet width, quantity, placement clearance and upright width; do not assume the nominal beam label equals usable clear space. Photograph markings, welds and connectors. Size alone does not establish capacity: material, section geometry, connector, weld, span, load positions and rack configuration matter. Use verified manufacturer data or qualified calculations for the exact beam. Check decks and pallet supports against the measured beam profile, install matched pairs and approved locks, and update the load notice after any approved change.

Which beam dimensions must be recorded?

A beam has system dimensions, section dimensions and usable-opening dimensions. Keep these separate in enquiries and inspection records.

Beam measurement terminology

TermPractical meaning
Clear spanDistance between inner upright faces at the beam level
Overall lengthEnd-to-end dimension including connector plates
Section heightVertical depth of the beam body
Section depthFront-to-back beam-body dimension
Step/ledgeRecess or seat for compatible deck/support
Connector heightVertical extent of end plate and hook pattern
PitchVertical spacing between connector hooks or upright holes
Beam elevationInstalled level relative to floor or datum

Field measurement workflow

  1. Unload or control the bay according to site procedure.
  2. Identify the rack manufacturer/product and select an undamaged beam.
  3. Measure clear span between upright faces.
  4. Measure overall beam length and section height/depth.
  5. Record step or ledge dimensions.
  6. Photograph and measure connector plate, hooks and lock feature.
  7. Record markings, welds and installed beam elevation.
  8. Repeat critical measurements on another undamaged bay.
  9. Create a labeled sketch; do not send unlabeled numbers alone.

Pallet width, count and horizontal clearance

The required clear opening depends on the approved pallets and placement tolerances, not only a common nominal beam length.

  • Record maximum loaded pallet width, including overhang.
  • Define the number of pallets per level.
  • Provide pallet-to-upright and pallet-to-pallet clearances required by the system and rules.
  • Account for connector/upright geometry that reduces usable space.
  • Check forklift visibility and placement accuracy.
  • Coordinate flue spaces and adjacent loads.
  • Verify that larger future pallets are not assumed without review.

Beam height and depth are identifiers, not ratings

  • Measure the complete formed section, not only the visible face.
  • Record bends, seams and closed/open features.
  • Identify step depth and support seating surfaces.
  • Record material and wall thickness only from verified data or suitable measurement.
  • Note reinforcement or internal features where documented.
  • Photograph labels, stamps and batch markings.
  • Do not infer capacity from section height alone.

Why equal dimensions can carry different loads

  • Material grade and verified thickness may differ.
  • Section bends, seams and internal geometry may differ.
  • Connector hooks, plate and welds may differ.
  • Clear span and load positions affect bending and deflection.
  • Deck or pallet supports change load distribution.
  • Manufacturing tolerances and product testing differ.
  • Upright/frame configuration may govern the level.
  • Damage, corrosion or alteration invalidates visual comparison.

Use manufacturer capacity data or qualified calculations for the exact beam, connector, span and load arrangement.

Deck and pallet-support dimensions

  • Match wire-deck channel and edge to the exact beam profile.
  • Match front-to-rear deck depth to rack spacing.
  • Verify step or ledge seating dimensions.
  • Confirm pallet-support length, orientation and attachment.
  • Keep beam locks and connectors accessible.
  • Check back stops/dividers for new forces and clearances.
  • Verify accessory rating and beam/frame capacity separately.

Replacement-beam specification checklist

  1. Rack manufacturer, product line and approximate age.
  2. Clear span and overall length, clearly labeled.
  3. Beam-section height, depth and step dimensions.
  4. Connector plate, hooks, pitch, weld and lock details.
  5. Upright profile and perforation photos.
  6. Pallet dimensions, count, orientation and maximum weights.
  7. Beam elevations and frame configuration.
  8. Decks, supports and back-stop details.
  9. Required capacity evidence and approval.

Common beam-sizing mistakes

  • Calling overall length the clear span.
  • Measuring between frame outside faces instead of the required datum.
  • Ignoring connector hooks and safety lock.
  • Choosing by beam-face height alone.
  • Using nominal pallet width instead of maximum loaded width.
  • Forgetting pallet-to-pallet and pallet-to-upright clearance.
  • Assuming old decks fit a new beam step.
  • Copying a capacity from a visually similar beam.
  • Measuring a bent or damaged component.

Checks before loading

  • Approved beam model, clear span and matched pair.
  • Correct connector fully engaged in compatible uprights.
  • Safety locks installed at every beam end.
  • Beam elevations match the approved frame configuration.
  • Pallet openings and clearances verified.
  • Decks/supports seat correctly.
  • Loads match the capacity basis.
  • Load notice and component record updated.

Pallet rack beams sizes: common questions

How is pallet-rack beam length measured?

State whether you mean clear span between upright faces or overall length including connectors. A dimensioned sketch prevents ambiguity.

Does a taller beam carry more weight?

Not necessarily. Section geometry, material, connector, weld, span and load arrangement all affect capacity; use verified data.

How wide should a two-pallet beam be?

Calculate the clear opening from maximum loaded pallet widths plus required side and intermediate clearances for the specific rack and operation.

Can a same-size beam from another brand fit?

Do not assume so. Connector geometry, upright pattern, safety lock, material and capacity may differ even when span and face size match.

Can old wire decking be reused?

Only after beam profile, step, front-to-rear spacing, channel seating, deck capacity and pallet load are verified.

Information to send for beam sizing

Send a labeled measurement sketch, rack manufacturer/system, upright and connector photos, clear span, overall length, section and step dimensions, safety lock, pallet dimensions/count/weights, beam elevations, decks/supports, handling equipment and photos of damage.
