Double Sided Cantilever Racks

Double sided cantilever racks create long-material storage on both faces of a central row. They can use floor area efficiently, but both-side load combinations, two handling aisles and the complete upright/base configuration must be documented.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 23 November 2026

Plan Your Rack Specification →
The short answer

Double sided cantilever racks have storage arms projecting from both sides of central uprights, allowing material access from two aisles. Buyers should define material dimensions and maximum weights for each face, arms per load, arm length and spacing, vertical levels, simultaneous two-side loading cases, total upright and base limits, aisle and handling equipment on both sides, row-end clearance, floor or foundation, anchors, protection, indoor/outdoor environment and installation scope. Do not assume equal loading on both sides cancels structural demand.

How do double sided cantilever racks work?

Arms extend from both faces of shared uprights. Each aisle can serve a different material family, or both sides can hold the same stock. Loads from both faces pass through uprights, bracing, bases, anchors and the supporting floor or foundation.

For component fundamentals, see the cantilever racking system guide.

When is a double-sided row useful?

  • A central row can be served by two practical handling aisles.
  • Long materials need more locations without using a wall line.
  • Stock can be divided logically between two faces.
  • The building or yard has enough end clearance for long-load turns.
  • Shared uprights and bases can be designed for the full load plan.

A double-sided row may be inefficient when one aisle is unusable, the site is too narrow for two handling routes or stock flow naturally favors a wall-side single row.

How should aisles and row ends be laid out?

  1. Map the longest load and handling vehicle on both faces.
  2. Check approach, fork spread, turning and long-load sweep.
  3. Reserve end-of-row staging and escape space.
  4. Keep columns, fences, doors and utilities out of load paths.
  5. Separate pedestrian routes from both loading zones where practicable.
  6. Check whether two vehicles may work opposite the same row simultaneously.

Operating rules should address visibility and coordination if both faces can be loaded at the same time.

How should arms, uprights and bases be configured?

  • Arm length and spacing suit each material family.
  • Short stock reaches the required number of supports.
  • Vertical clearances allow forks, slings and restraints.
  • Upright limits cover the complete two-face arrangement.
  • Bases, anchors and bracing match the approved configuration.
  • End restraints or separators suit round or unstable products.

Similar-looking arms on opposite faces are not proof of equal capacity or interchangeability.

Which floor or foundation information is required?

  • Slab, pavement, foundation or soil information.
  • Joints, edges, slopes and buried services.
  • Base and anchor positions.
  • Indoor/outdoor environmental criteria.
  • Drainage and water paths around bases.
  • Installation tolerances and inspection access.

Central rows can create concentrated reactions along one line. Verify the actual support rather than assuming ordinary warehouse paving is suitable.

What should a double-sided cantilever quote include?

  • Material and load schedules for both faces.
  • Plans/elevations showing rows, arms, levels and both aisles.
  • Component and complete-system limits.
  • Uprights, arms, bases, bracing, anchors and restraints.
  • Floor/foundation and site work by others.
  • Finish, environment and protection scope.
  • Engineering, freight, unloading, erection and inspection.
  • Final drawings, load notices and operating information.

Compare the same two-face position count and scope. A single-sided price doubled is not necessarily a valid double-sided system proposal.

How should double-sided rows be operated?

  • Label approved material and load zones on both faces.
  • Keep both aisles and row ends clear.
  • Coordinate simultaneous loading activity.
  • Inspect arms, uprights, bracing, bases and anchors from both sides.
  • Report impact, deformation or changed materials.
  • Review arm-level changes against the complete two-face load plan.

Do not remove bracing or assume an empty face allows unapproved overload on the other face.

Double sided cantilever racks: common questions

Are double-sided racks twice the capacity of single-sided racks?

Do not assume so. Capacity depends on arms, uprights, bases, bracing, anchors, configuration and combined load cases.

Must both faces carry equal loads?

No, but the approved design must cover the actual possible load arrangements, including uneven loading.

Can different materials be stored on each face?

Potentially, when each side has documented supports, loads, restraints and handling routes.

Can one aisle be narrower than the other?

Potentially, if the assigned material and handling equipment can operate safely within the approved layout.

What improves a quotation?

Send materials and maximum loads by face, desired arms and levels, handling equipment for both aisles, site plan, floor/foundation data, environment, destination and installation scope.
