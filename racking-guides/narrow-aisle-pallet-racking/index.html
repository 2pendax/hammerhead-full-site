Narrow Aisle Pallet Racking

Narrow aisle pallet racking allocates less floor area to handling aisles. The rack, forklift, pallet and working routes must be planned together.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 16 November 2026

Plan Your Rack Specification →
The short answer

Narrow aisle pallet racking is a pallet-storage layout designed around handling equipment that can operate in reduced aisle space. It can release floor area for additional storage while retaining access to aisle-facing positions. Determine clear aisle width from the exact truck, attachment, largest load, lift height and operating method. Compare usable positions and peak handling performance before choosing the layout.

How does a narrow aisle layout create storage space?

Aisles occupy part of the warehouse footprint. If suitable equipment can work in a smaller operating envelope, a revised plan may accommodate more rack rows. That possibility must be tested against the actual building geometry.

Reserve receiving, dispatch, staging, cross aisles, exits and equipment areas before fitting additional rack. A row that creates a blocked route does not add useful storage.

How wide should a narrow pallet-rack aisle be?

Obtain the selected truck supplier's application requirement for the intended load and operation. A truck's body width alone does not describe its stacking envelope.

InputWhat to confirm
EquipmentExact model, mast, forks and attachment
LoadLargest dimensions, overhang, weight and load centre
OperationStacking movement and lift height
BoundariesStored-load projections, guards and rack faces
SiteFloor condition, intersections and visibility

The SafeWork NSW pallet racking fact sheet says layout should match the handling equipment, including aisle width and forklift turning needs. Its guidance also addresses emergency access and lighting.

Should aisle width be measured between rack frames or loads?

The drawing must identify the clear boundaries used in the equipment review. A rack-to-rack dimension can overstate usable space where pallets, goods or protection project into the aisle.

  1. Draw the rack footprint accurately.
  2. Add the largest permitted load projection.
  3. Locate guards and other fixed obstructions.
  4. Show the remaining clear operating width.
  5. Have the equipment application reviewed against those boundaries.

Record the permitted pallet and load envelope in warehouse instructions. Later introduction of wider goods can invalidate the original aisle assumption even when the rack has not moved.

Which forklift should be selected?

Compare equipment capable of handling the real loads at the required height. Reach, articulated and specialist aisle trucks have different envelopes and infrastructure requirements.

  • Capacity at the intended lift height and load centre.
  • Required stacking and approach clearance.
  • Visibility and accurate high-level placement.
  • Floor and environmental requirements.
  • Charging, maintenance and service access.
  • Training for the actual machine and site.

Ask the truck and rack suppliers to review one dimensioned drawing. Their assumptions should agree before a purchase order is placed.

Does reducing aisle width always improve capacity?

It may increase designed positions, but usable storage also depends on columns, pallet heights, work areas and stock allocation. Count positions from final candidate layouts.

For example, a change from 600 to 690 usable positions adds 90 positions, or 15%. This is illustrative arithmetic, not a guaranteed gain from narrow aisles.

  1. Use the same building and inventory brief for both options.
  2. Reserve operational areas and required clear routes.
  3. Map peak stock into compatible positions.
  4. Check simultaneous replenishment and retrieval.
  5. Include truck, floor and project costs.
  6. Compare storage gain with required throughput.

What changes in daily warehouse traffic?

Reduced space can affect passing, turning and temporary staging. Define permitted movement and pedestrian routes with the site team.

  • Keep aisle entrances and intersections visible.
  • Reserve space for end turns and waiting loads.
  • Prevent temporary pallets and waste from narrowing routes.
  • Provide lighting for travel and load placement.
  • Identify exposed rack ends and protection needs.
  • Report impacts and changed operating conditions promptly.

Check the busiest operating period rather than an empty warehouse. A compact drawing may need revision when receiving and dispatch overlap.

State whether existing equipment will remain or new equipment is included. Compare total project responsibility with the other storage systems under consideration.

Narrow aisle pallet racking: common questions

Can an existing forklift be retained?

Possibly, when its exact configuration can perform the intended operation within the confirmed clear aisle. Obtain the application review.

Is there a universal narrow aisle width?

No. The required space depends on equipment, load, placement movement and site conditions.

Can pallets overhang into the aisle?

Any permitted projection must be included in the clear-boundary drawing and equipment review.

Does narrow aisle mean very narrow aisle?

Terminology varies. Specify actual equipment, clearances and operating requirements rather than relying on the label.

What is needed for a first layout?

Send a floor plan, pallet dimensions and weights, peak quantities, lift height, equipment details, throughput and location.
