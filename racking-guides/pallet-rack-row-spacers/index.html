Pallet Rack Row Spacers

Pallet rack row spacers connect adjacent frames in a back-to-back rack row and maintain the separation defined by the approved layout. They are system components, not improvised links: length, hole pattern, fixing, quantity and vertical position must match the rack, pallet geometry, required flue space and engineering design.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 25 November 2026

The short answer

Choose pallet rack row spacers from the approved back-to-back rack configuration. Confirm frame type and depth, required separation, pallet rear overhang, flue requirements, spacer connection and hole pattern, fasteners, length, quantity and installation elevations. Nominal spacer length is not automatically the usable flue width because uprights, bracket offsets, pallet overhang and tolerances affect the geometry. Spacers do not replace frame bracing, floor anchors, row ties or stability assessment. Never drill, weld or substitute a similar-looking part without approval. Set out, plumb and align frames before installing spacers; do not force misaligned members together. Tighten fixings by the approved method and measure the completed clearance. Keep pallets, loads, mesh and services out of the required flue. Inspect damaged or missing spacers, their connections and the surrounding rack before replacement.

What row spacers do in back-to-back pallet rack

Row spacers maintain a designed relationship between two frames placed back to back. Their geometry helps establish the row separation and provides a defined connection between adjacent frames. The rack designer decides whether they also participate in the system's stability model.

Spacer length is only one part of the rear gap

Define exactly where spacer length is measured: overall length, hole centre to hole centre, inside faces or another system reference. Bracket offsets and upright geometry can make two spacers with the same nominal length produce different frame separation.

  • Use the approved drawing's dimension references.
  • Include pallet overhang on both sides of the flue.
  • Allow for installation and manufacturing tolerances.
  • Check the narrowest clear point, not only the spacer body.
  • Keep loads and accessories outside the required clear zone.
  • Confirm that the selected gap works throughout the full row.

Quantity and vertical positions require system approval

There is no universal number of pallet rack row spacers for every frame pair. Rack height, frame stiffness, loading, stability model, seismic or wind actions and manufacturer details can change the required arrangement.

  1. Identify every paired frame and any unpaired row end.
  2. Use the project elevation drawing for spacer heights.
  3. Avoid clashes with frame bracing and beam connectors.
  4. Keep connections accessible for installation and inspection.
  5. Coordinate row ties or building ties as separate components.
  6. Record deviations and obtain approval before moving a spacer.

FinishCompatible coating and site environment

Do not weld to an upright, enlarge holes or replace a bolt with an unverified alternative. These changes may alter both the connection and the rack frame.

Coordinate pallet overhang, flue space and sprinklers

The visible frame gap is not necessarily the clear flue after pallets are stored. Check the worst credible pallet position, load projection and accessories.

  • Verify pallet depth and rear overhang.
  • Define a clear placement rule for operators.
  • Coordinate longitudinal and transverse flue requirements.
  • Check sprinklers and their water-distribution path.
  • Keep mesh, stops and safety devices outside the flue.
  • Inspect protruding cartons, wrap and shifted loads.
  • Confirm local fire and insurer requirements.

Row spacers do not replace anchors, bracing or engineering

A spacer may connect two frames, but that fact alone does not prove the row is stable or has a particular capacity. The rack system still depends on approved frame bracing, beam connections, floor anchors, row configuration and the design actions used for the project.

  • Confirm whether the engineering model relies on spacer forces.
  • Do not treat a back-to-back pair as two independent rows without review.
  • Assess unpaired frames and changes at row ends.
  • Keep building ties and row ties distinct from ordinary spacers.
  • Review alterations to height, beam levels, loads or aisle configuration.
  • Use the rack supplier or qualified engineer for nonstandard conditions.

Installation and commissioning workflow

  1. Confirm the approved rack plan, elevations and component list.
  2. Set out both rows to the correct grid and separation.
  3. Assemble, anchor, plumb and align frames as specified.
  4. Install the correct spacer at each approved elevation.
  5. Do not pull misaligned frames together by overtightening bolts.
  6. Tighten and retain fasteners using the approved procedure.
  7. Measure the completed rear gap at representative and critical points.
  8. Trial pallet placement and verify overhang without blocking the flue.
  9. Record component identity, deviations and final inspection.

For a retrofit, identify the rack system from drawings and component markings. Similar colour or overall length is not evidence of compatibility.

Common row-spacer specification mistakes

  • Ordering by nominal length without defining measurement points.
  • Ignoring bracket offset and upright-face geometry.
  • Using the rear frame gap as the guaranteed clear flue.
  • Forgetting pallet and load overhang on both rows.
  • Choosing the quantity from habit instead of the approved elevation.
  • Field drilling, welding or substituting fasteners.
  • Forcing skewed frames together with the spacer connection.
  • Confusing row spacers with row ties or wall/building ties.
  • Assuming spacers increase capacity without engineering evidence.
  • Replacing a damaged spacer without inspecting the surrounding rack.

Pallet rack row spacers: common questions

What is a pallet rack row spacer?

It is a component connecting adjacent frames in a back-to-back row to maintain the separation and arrangement defined by the rack design.

How long should a row spacer be?

Use the approved dimension based on frame references, bracket offsets, required rear clearance, pallet overhang and tolerances; nominal length alone is insufficient.

How many row spacers are needed per frame pair?

The approved rack elevation must define the quantity and heights because rack geometry, stability and design actions differ by project.

Do row spacers guarantee a fire flue?

No. The usable flue also depends on pallet placement, load projection, accessories and local fire requirements.

Can a similar spacer from another rack system be used?

Only after compatibility and design approval confirm its dimensions, brackets, holes, fasteners, material and intended force path.

Information to send for row-spacer selection

Send the rack manufacturer/system, layout and elevations, frame depth and profile, upright hole details, required back-to-back gap, pallet and load dimensions, rear overhang, flue and sprinkler requirements, spacer positions, fastener information, site design actions, photographs and any damaged or missing components.
