Automated Pallet Storage Systems

Automated pallet storage systems coordinate storage structures, handling machines and software. Define the complete material flow before selecting an architecture.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 16 November 2026

The short answer

Automated pallet storage systems use controlled equipment to place and retrieve unit loads within a storage structure. Architectures can include crane-served pallet racks or shuttle-based storage with associated transfers and conveyors. Select from pallet quality, SKU quantities, peak inbound and outbound demand, building constraints, software interfaces and service requirements. Agree the automation boundary and measurable acceptance criteria before comparing complete project offers.

Which operations should the automated system cover?

Start with the pallet journey from receiving or production to dispatch. Identify where automation begins, where people remain involved and who owns each handoff.

Include rejected loads, returns, maintenance access and recovery routes in the scope. These functions affect space and operation even when they are used infrequently.

Why is pallet quality central to automation?

Equipment must engage, transport and identify loads reliably. A forklift operator may accommodate a damaged or irregular pallet differently from an automated transfer.

  • Outer dimensions, loaded height and overhang.
  • Weight range and load distribution.
  • Underside construction and bearing surfaces.
  • Load stability and packaging condition.
  • Identification method and label position.
  • Inspection and rejection criteria.

Agree representative load samples and define the route for rejected pallets. State whether re-palletising or correction work is included, and who performs it.

Which throughput data should drive the design?

Provide time-based demand rather than annual pallet totals alone. Storage size and handling capacity answer different questions.

  1. Normal and peak inventory by SKU.
  2. Inbound pallets by hour and shift.
  3. Outbound demand and order release patterns.
  4. Simultaneous receiving and dispatch peaks.
  5. Batch, expiry and sequencing rules.
  6. Expected growth and seasonal variation.

Ask suppliers to explain how queues, transfers and shared equipment affect peak performance. A machine's isolated cycle rating does not establish end-to-end warehouse throughput.

What software interfaces need agreement?

Identify which system owns inventory identity, location allocation, movement requests and completion records. Product names alone do not define these responsibilities.

  • Messages and data required at inbound verification.
  • Storage and retrieval request ownership.
  • Task status and completion confirmation.
  • Handling of failed, cancelled or repeated requests.
  • Inventory reconciliation and exception reporting.
  • Testing, support and change responsibilities.

The Mecalux automated shuttle overview describes coordinated rack, shuttle, aisle handling and inbound/outbound conveyors. The integration of these elements must be specified for each project.

How should availability be evaluated?

Define required operating hours and acceptable interruption behaviour with the project team. Consider the effect of one unavailable machine, blocked transfer or communications fault on the rest of the flow.

  • Maintenance access and planned service windows.
  • Spare parts and technical support arrangements.
  • Authorised recovery and load-access methods.
  • Data backup and restart responsibilities.
  • Operational response while equipment is unavailable.
  • Training for operators, supervisors and service personnel.

Agree any availability measure in writing, including its calculation and exclusions. Avoid comparing offers from an unexplained uptime percentage.

What should system acceptance demonstrate?

Acceptance should connect to the approved loads, inventory and operating scenarios. Define the test period, starting conditions and evidence before procurement.

  1. Verify installed equipment and interfaces against final documents.
  2. Use representative accepted pallets and load variations.
  3. Demonstrate agreed inbound and outbound scenarios.
  4. Check inventory and movement records.
  5. Review defined exception and restart functions.
  6. Record unresolved issues and corrective actions.

Separate equipment checks from overall performance acceptance. A successful movement of one pallet proves less than a coordinated flow under the agreed peak conditions.

Automated pallet storage systems: common questions

Is every pallet shuttle installation fully automated?

No. Semi-automated systems can retain forklifts and operator commands. Confirm the project boundary.

Can the system accept damaged pallets?

Only loads within its approved envelope should enter. Define inspection, rejection and correction routes.

Does more storage guarantee greater throughput?

No. Throughput depends on handling equipment, transfers, queues and demand patterns.

Is WMS integration automatically included?

No. Specify the functions, data interfaces, responsibilities and acceptance scope explicitly.

What information improves a first proposal?

Send loads, stock snapshots, time-based demand, sequencing rules, building records, software interfaces and location.
