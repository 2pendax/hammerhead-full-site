Mold Storage Racks

Mold storage racks keep injection molds, casting molds and production tooling identified, protected and ready for changeover. A reliable layout connects verified mold dimensions and weights with shelf format, handling equipment, maintenance status, floor support and the route between storage and the machine.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 23 November 2026

The short answer

Mold storage racks are purpose-planned systems for injection molds, die-casting molds and other dense tooling assemblies. Buyers should provide a mold register with ID, length, width, height, verified operating weight, base or support footprint, center-of-mass concerns, maintenance status and retrieval frequency; then define fixed or pull-out levels, crane, forklift, mold cart or transfer equipment, aisle and extension clearances, maximum level and bay loads, base and floor reactions, identification, installation and inspection scope. Capacity must match the documented rack configuration and operating condition.

Which mold storage rack type fits the mold room?

Choose the storage format from the movement sequence, not from mold size alone. Fixed heavy-duty levels can suit complete-unit handling by fork or crane. Pull-out shelves can expose a mold for overhead lifting. Cart-transfer levels may align storage with a matched changeover cart. The best choice reduces extra lifts while keeping people away from suspended loads and pinch points.

For concentrated press-tool applications, compare heavy duty die storage racks.

What data should a mold register contain?

InputRequired detail
IdentityMold ID, product, machine family and ownership
EnvelopeLength, width, height and projecting connections
WeightVerified stored weight including base, fittings and retained parts
SupportBase footprint, contact points and required orientation
HandlingLifting points, fork pockets, cart interface and transfer direction
StatusReady, cleaning, repair, quarantine or long-term storage
FrequencyChangeover demand and preferred slotting priority

Include the heaviest stored condition. Auxiliary plates, hoses, fixtures or components can change both the weight and required clearance.

Fixed or pull-out mold storage shelves?

FormatPotential useControl to verify
Fixed shelfMolds loaded as whole units by fork or craneClear extraction path and support distribution
Manual pull-outSuitably rated molds with controlled accessOperating force, extension capacity and lock
Powered pull-outFrequent changeovers or higher operating loadsControls, guarding, isolation and maintenance
Cart-transfer levelDirect movement to a matched mold cartHeight, alignment, restraint and interface

Ask for the rated load in the actual stored and extended conditions. Do not treat a static shelf rating as proof of pull-out suitability.

How should molds move between rack and machine?

  • Overhead crane: record hook approach, lifting points, clearance and the controlled landing sequence.
  • Forklift: verify truck capacity at the required load center, fork entry, visibility, aisle and rack clearances.
  • Mold cart: match deck height, transfer direction, braking, alignment and restraints.
  • Roller or powered transfer: define end stops, controls, guarding, isolation and recovery steps.

Assign responsibility for cranes, trucks, carts, slings and other third-party devices. Rack capacity alone does not validate the complete handling operation.

Which mold rack loads require engineering review?

  • Maximum mold load per shelf or storage position.
  • Maximum bay and complete rack load in the approved arrangement.
  • Load distribution through mold feet, shelf members, frame, base and floor.
  • Forces created during shelf extension and mold transfer.
  • Base reactions, anchors and supporting-floor capacity.
  • Vehicle or equipment interaction near the storage cell.

Dense molds can create high reactions over a small area. The rack, anchorage and floor form one load path and should be reviewed together.

How can storage support mold condition and traceability?

  • Use stable support points that match the approved mold orientation.
  • Keep cooling connections, electrical plugs and projecting fittings inside protected clearances.
  • Control corrosion protection, leakage and contamination according to the maintenance plan.
  • Label each position with mold ID, status and documented capacity.
  • Separate ready, repair and quarantine tooling to prevent the wrong mold from reaching production.
  • Provide access for inspection without climbing on the rack.

How should mold racks fit changeover and maintenance flow?

  1. Map unloading, inspection, cleaning, repair, storage, staging, machine loading and return.
  2. Slot frequent molds for efficient access without exceeding approved loads.
  3. Reserve the full envelope for crane movement, shelf extension or cart docking.
  4. Separate pedestrian paths from mold transfers where practicable.
  5. Create clear locations for ready, dirty, repair and quarantined molds.
  6. Maintain housekeeping and inspection access around bases and mechanisms.

A dense layout is useful only when it shortens safe changeover work. Count movements and congestion points before approving the final arrangement.

What should a mold storage rack quotation include?

  • Mold register and documented design assumptions.
  • Rack dimensions, shelf type, position count and operating clearances.
  • Shelf, bay and system capacity limits.
  • Stops, restraints, locks, interlocks, controls and guarding included.
  • Base, anchor and floor requirements.
  • Handling-equipment interfaces and responsibility boundaries.
  • Engineering, freight, installation, commissioning, training and final documents.
  • Inspection, maintenance and spare-parts information.

Compare proposals against the same mold list and changeover process, not a generic price per shelf.

Mold storage racks: common questions

How is mold rack capacity chosen?

Use verified stored mold weights, support footprints, maximum position loads and the approved rack geometry; capacity should not be estimated from appearance.

Can all molds use pull-out shelves?

No. The mold weight, shelf mechanism, extended condition, operating force, interlocks, rack and floor all require documented suitability.

Should ready and repair molds share positions?

A clear status and location system should prevent quarantined or incomplete tooling from being selected for production.

Can a forklift load mold racks?

Potentially, when truck capacity, load center, fork interface, visibility, aisle, clearances and the operating method are compatible.

What information improves a quotation?

Send mold IDs, envelopes, verified weights, support footprints, lifting or transfer interfaces, frequency, status workflow, layout, floor data, destination and installation scope.
