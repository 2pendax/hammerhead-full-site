Structural Pallet Racking

Structural pallet racking uses hot-rolled structural steel members for pallet storage. Choose it from the load, handling environment and project design—not appearance alone.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 16 November 2026

The short answer

Structural pallet racking is an industrial storage rack made with hot-rolled structural steel members, commonly channel sections, for uprights and beams. Connections are often bolted. It may suit demanding pallet loads or high-activity environments, but structural steel does not create a universal capacity or make impact harmless. Compare a project-specific structural design with roll-formed alternatives using the same loads, layout and site requirements.

What does structural pallet racking mean?

In this product category, "structural" refers to the hot-rolled steel members used to form the rack. A complete system still includes upright frames, horizontal load beams, connections, bracing, base plates, anchors and any required pallet supports or accessories.

The Rack Manufacturers Institute FAQ describes storage rack as a building-like, non-building structural system assembled from members and connections into rows, bays and levels. It includes systems made from cold-formed or hot-rolled structural steel.

How does structural pallet rack differ from roll-formed rack?

The material-forming method is one difference, but buyers should compare the complete proposed systems. Either category must match the actual loads, configuration, site and applicable requirements.

When should structural pallet racking be evaluated?

Include structural rack in the comparison when the application has demanding loads, frequent material-handling activity, a preference for bolted connections or an operating environment where member robustness is important. These are reasons to evaluate—not proof that a particular design is suitable.

  • High pallet weights or long beam spans that require a documented solution.
  • Busy forklift routes with significant exposure to handling activity.
  • Cold, industrial or high-throughput operations with project-specific needs.
  • Selective, drive-in, push-back or flow arrangements proposed with structural members.
  • Company standards that call for particular member or connection types.

Steel King's pallet rack product overview describes its structural product as using hot-rolled channels for uprights and beams. That is a manufacturer-specific example, not evidence that all structural products share its details or performance.

Which loads and site facts govern a structural rack design?

Structural steel does not remove the need to document every design input. Provide the heaviest pallet and the intended distribution of loads throughout the rack.

  1. Unit loads: pallet dimensions, weight, height, underside and stability.
  2. Rack geometry: bay width, frame depth and height, beam elevations and row length.
  3. Handling: forklift type, load dimensions, turning needs and operating clearances.
  4. Building: floor plan, slab information, columns, doors, services and clear routes.
  5. Environment: indoor or outdoor use, temperature and other relevant exposure.
  6. Location: installation address and requirements identified with the local project team.

Do not copy an upright or beam rating without its stated spacing, connection, load pattern and other limitations. Steel King's structural upright example, for instance, explicitly conditions its listed ratings on beam spacing, indoor and non-seismic use, anchoring and plumb installation. Those conditions cannot be transferred to a different product.

Does structural pallet racking eliminate forklift damage?

No rack should be sold as damage-proof. A heavier member can still be struck, bent, displaced or have its connection and anchorage affected. Design the operation to reduce contact and create a clear response when damage occurs.

  • Separate pedestrian and equipment routes where the site plan requires it.
  • Use suitable end-of-row and upright protection based on the risk assessment.
  • Keep aisles, turning areas and staging positions usable.
  • Train operators to report contact immediately.
  • Isolate and evaluate uncertain rack condition under the site's procedure.
  • Use identified repair or replacement components and approved methods.

Do not weld, drill or heat a structural member as an improvised repair. Obtain the required assessment and repair documentation for the affected system.

Structural pallet racking: common questions

Is structural pallet rack always stronger than roll-formed rack?

Do not judge capacity from category alone. Compare engineered proposals for the same loads, geometry, site and requirements.

Is every structural rack connection bolted?

Bolted connections are common, but confirm the exact upright, beam and connection detail supplied by the manufacturer.

Can structural rack be installed outdoors?

Only when the project is specifically designed for the outdoor environment and its relevant loads and exposure. A standard indoor proposal should not be moved outside.

Can damaged structural rack be welded in place?

Do not improvise a weld repair. Isolate the affected area as required and obtain the appropriate assessment and documented repair method.

What improves a structural rack quotation?

Send pallet dimensions and weights, desired positions, a dimensioned floor plan, equipment details, site location and required project scope.
