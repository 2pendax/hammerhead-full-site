Galvanized Pallet Racking for Cold Storage

Galvanized pallet racking for cold storage can improve corrosion protection, but “galvanized” is not a complete specification. Zinc process, coating requirement, steel chemistry, fabrication sequence, venting and drainage, cut edges, weld and thread treatment, storage before installation, condensation exposure, cleaning chemicals and repair acceptance all affect performance.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 25 November 2026

The short answer

Specify galvanized pallet racking for cold storage by first mapping temperature, humidity, door cycles, condensation, defrost water, washdown chemicals and wetting/drying at bases and horizontal surfaces. Select a zinc-coating route suitable for the component and fabrication process—such as pre-galvanized material or post-fabrication hot-dip galvanizing—without treating them as interchangeable. State the applicable coating standard, required coating mass or thickness, surface and appearance acceptance, sampling and test method, and approved repair procedure. For post-fabrication galvanizing, coordinate steel chemistry, drainage and vent holes, distortion control, threads, moving fits and connection tolerances. For pre-galvanized components, address cut edges, punched holes and welded zones. Protect newly galvanized material from trapped moisture and poor ventilation to reduce white-rust risk. Verify low-temperature steel, rack capacity, connections, anchors, floor, fire and building interfaces separately; then inspect coating condition throughout transport, installation and service.

What determines galvanized rack performance?

Durability is the result of exposure, zinc system, component detailing, fabrication quality and maintenance.

Do not compare the word “galvanized” without comparing process, standard, coating requirement and component details.

What belongs in a galvanizing specification?

  • Applicable zinc-coating and fabrication standards.
  • Component list and coating route for each item.
  • Required coating mass or thickness and sampling plan.
  • Base steel, surface preparation and steel-chemistry requirements.
  • Appearance, roughness, runs, bare areas and drainage acceptance.
  • Thread, hole, connector, lock and moving-fit requirements.
  • Approved repair materials, extent and acceptance criteria.
  • Records, certificates, inspection and traceability.

Why do venting and drainage details matter?

  • Closed or overlapping volumes need safe vent paths.
  • Trapped zinc or process fluids can add weight and create defects.
  • Poor drainage can leave sharp runs and obstruct connectors.
  • Asymmetric sections can distort during heating and cooling.
  • Threads, precision holes and moving parts may need allowances.
  • Drain and vent locations must not reduce structural capacity.

Coordinate holes before fabrication with the galvanizer and structural designer. Never add field holes solely for drainage without approval.

Fabrication and coating quality hold points

  1. Approve drawings, component route and special details.
  2. Confirm steel and welding consumables are suitable.
  3. Inspect fabrication before coating, including vents and drains.
  4. Control cleaning, fluxing, immersion and withdrawal.
  5. Inspect coating thickness, coverage and functional fits.
  6. Correct roughness or defects without damaging the component.
  7. Apply and inspect authorized local repairs.
  8. Protect, mark and pack components for dry transport.

What causes white rust on galvanized rack?

Fresh zinc surfaces can develop white corrosion products when water is trapped with limited air circulation, particularly in tightly nested or wrapped bundles.

  • Store material dry, separated and ventilated.
  • Prevent rain or condensation from becoming trapped in packaging.
  • Use packing that drains and allows airflow.
  • Inspect bundles promptly at delivery and after storage.
  • Assess deposits by an approved procedure rather than hiding them with paint.
  • Correct the moisture source before installation.

Appearance alone does not determine remaining protection; severity and acceptance require competent assessment.

Galvanized rack connections and accessories

  • Verify connector seating after coating thickness is added.
  • Keep beam locks, bolts and moving parts functional at low temperature.
  • Confirm anchor materials and corrosion compatibility.
  • Avoid unreviewed grinding that removes protective zinc.
  • Isolate incompatible metals where the design requires it.
  • Protect cut threads, field damage and approved drilled areas.
  • Ensure labels and load notices adhere and remain readable.

What galvanizing does not prove

  • Beam, connector, upright, bracing or frame capacity.
  • Steel suitability at the minimum cold-room temperature.
  • Anchor and insulated-floor performance.
  • Seismic, impact or other project actions.
  • Sprinkler, flue and fire-protection compliance.
  • Compatibility between mixed manufacturers or component series.

Complete structural and building verification independently, using the final coated component configuration.

Cold-storage galvanized rack inspection plan

  1. Record baseline coating condition after installation.
  2. Inspect bases, horizontal ledges, crevices and impact zones.
  3. Check after leaks, defrost failures, washdown or warm-up cycles.
  4. Remove deposits using approved methods that preserve zinc.
  5. Document bare steel, red rust, white deposits and coating damage.
  6. Repair only after cleaning and competent assessment.
  7. Track repeated damage and change protection or operations where needed.

Galvanized pallet racking for cold storage: common questions

Is galvanized rack rust-proof?

No. Zinc provides sacrificial corrosion protection, but service life depends on coating, environment, damage, drainage, chemicals and maintenance.

Is hot-dip galvanizing always better than pre-galvanized steel?

No. Each route has benefits and constraints. Select by component geometry, fabrication, coating requirement, fits and exposure.

What is white rust?

It is a zinc corrosion product commonly associated with trapped moisture and poor ventilation on fresh galvanized surfaces. Severity requires assessment.

Can galvanized components be welded on site?

Only with authorized structural, safety, fume-control and coating-repair procedures. Unapproved welding can alter capacity and protection.

Does galvanizing make rack suitable for every freezer?

No. Steel, accessories, anchors, floor, capacity, fire and operating conditions still require project-specific verification.

Information to send for galvanized cold-storage rack

Send operating/minimum temperatures, humidity and door cycles, condensation, defrost and washdown conditions, chemicals, pallet dimensions and maximum weights, rack type and layout, component list, floor and building drawings, fire requirements, installation location, expected service life and inspection documentation requirements.
