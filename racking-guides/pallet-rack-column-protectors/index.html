Pallet Rack Column Protectors

Pallet rack column protectors reduce the likelihood or consequence of certain forklift, pallet or cart impacts at vulnerable uprights. They do not make a poor aisle layout safe, restore a damaged column or guarantee that every impact is harmless; protector type, force path, height, setback, clearance, anchorage and inspection procedure must suit the actual traffic risk.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 25 November 2026

The short answer

Select pallet rack column protectors after mapping vehicle routes, turn points, bay entries, pallet movement, sight lines and impact history. Distinguish rack-mounted protectors that attach around or to an upright from floor-anchored guards, bollards and row-end barriers with an independent load path. Confirm upright profile, baseplate and anchor access; protector height and coverage; forklift, wheel and pallet contact geometry; aisle and fork clearances; visibility; attachment method; and any force transferred into the rack or floor. Verify the supporting concrete for floor anchors and keep guards from creating a snag, reducing required aisle width or hiding column/base damage. Protection supplements traffic separation, driver rules, lighting and maintenance. After a significant strike, isolate the area as required and inspect the protector, upright, bracing, baseplate, anchors, beams and floor. Replace damaged units under an approved method and investigate repeated impacts at the same location.

Rack-mounted versus floor-anchored protection

The first decision is where impact force goes. A rack-mounted device interacts with the upright; an independent guard transfers force through its anchors into the floor.

Height, width, setback and contact zone

  • Identify credible contact from wheel, chassis, fork, pallet and load.
  • Set protector height to the assessed exposure, not appearance.
  • Cover vulnerable column/base zones without blocking inspection.
  • Keep the protector within approved aisle and turning clearances.
  • Prevent forks or pallets from snagging behind the device.
  • Check access to anchors, beam locks and lower braces.
  • Make the protector visible from normal approach directions.
  • Consider vehicle deflection and protector movement under impact.

Protector-to-upright compatibility

  • Match upright width, depth, face and base geometry.
  • Confirm approved clip, strap, bolt or attachment.
  • Do not drill or weld the upright without an approved detail.
  • Check whether the device covers existing dents or corrosion.
  • Keep frame bracing and beam connectors unobstructed.
  • Verify impact forces included in the rack design basis.
  • Use the manufacturer's installation orientation and height.
  • Remove and inspect after significant impact where required.

Independent guards, anchors and concrete

  1. Define design impact direction and duty.
  2. Obtain baseplate and anchor reactions.
  3. Locate slab joints, edges, cracks and embedded services.
  4. Verify concrete/foundation capacity and anchor detail.
  5. Maintain required setback from the rack and traffic path.
  6. Install anchors to the specified position, embedment and torque/process.
  7. Inspect concrete damage, loose anchors and baseplate movement.
  8. Repair or replace only through an approved method.

Protection does not replace traffic management

  • Lay out aisles for the actual vehicle and load.
  • Separate pedestrians and vehicles.
  • Improve lighting, mirrors, signs and line marking.
  • Set speed, priority and one-way rules where suitable.
  • Maintain pallets, forks, brakes and steering.
  • Train operators for rack entry and reporting impacts.
  • Remove staging and debris from travel paths.
  • Investigate repeated strikes rather than adding more guards blindly.

What to inspect after a protector strike

  • Protector deformation, cracks, displacement and fasteners.
  • Upright dents, tears, twist, bow and hidden contact.
  • Lower frame braces and connections.
  • Baseplate welds, bearing and distortion.
  • Rack anchors and surrounding concrete.
  • Floor-guard anchors, baseplate and slab cracking.
  • Nearby beams, connectors, pallets and row alignment.
  • Impact cause, vehicle and operating conditions.

Isolate or unload the affected rack according to the site's damage procedure. A damaged guard does not prove the upright is safe.

Column-protector selection workflow

  1. Map traffic, load and impact history.
  2. Identify vulnerable rack/base zones.
  3. Choose rack-mounted or independent force path.
  4. Define impact duty, coverage and visibility.
  5. Verify upright attachment or floor anchorage.
  6. Check aisle, pallet, pedestrian and egress clearances.
  7. Install to approved drawings.
  8. Trial vehicle and pallet movement without contact.
  9. Create inspection and post-impact procedures.
  10. Monitor recurring strikes and revise the system.

Common protection mistakes

  • Choosing by upright width alone.
  • Reducing the forklift aisle with a bulky guard.
  • Drilling or welding uprights without approval.
  • Anchoring into weak, cracked or edge concrete without verification.
  • Allowing a protector to hide existing column damage.
  • Assuming a struck guard means the rack was untouched.
  • Replacing the guard without investigating repeated impacts.
  • Using column protectors instead of row-end traffic protection.
  • Blocking egress, fire equipment or inspection access.

Pallet rack column protectors: common questions

Do rack column protectors prevent all damage?

No. They reduce selected risks within their design duty. Impact severity, direction and geometry may still damage the protector, rack, anchors or floor.

Should protectors attach to the upright or floor?

It depends on the risk and approved force path. Rack-mounted and independent floor-anchored designs have different compatibility and support requirements.

Can a protector cover a dented upright?

No as a repair. Existing damage must be assessed first and remain inspectable.

Can guards reduce aisle width?

They occupy space, so verify the actual forklift, pallet, turning and required clearances before installation.

What should happen after a protector is hit?

Report and inspect the protector, upright, braces, baseplate, anchors, concrete and adjacent rack under the site's impact procedure.

Information to send for column-protection planning

Send rack layout and upright/base details, forklift and pallet dimensions, routes and turning, aisle widths, impact history/photos, desired force path, floor drawings and concrete data, joints/services, pedestrian and emergency routes, existing guards and damage.
