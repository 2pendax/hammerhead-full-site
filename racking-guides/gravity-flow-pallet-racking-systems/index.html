Gravity Flow Pallet Racking Systems

A working gravity-flow installation combines structural support, rolling lanes, movement control and the loading equipment. Specify their interfaces together.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 16 November 2026

Plan Your Rack Specification →
The short answer

Gravity flow pallet racking systems combine a rack structure with inclined rolling lanes and components that control pallet movement and presentation. A complete specification connects pallet geometry and weight range to the rollers, speed control, separation, end stops, loading equipment and acceptance process. Agree who owns each interface before fabrication, then validate the installed package using representative pallets and documented criteria.

What should a gravity-flow system package include?

Begin with a system boundary. Identify who supplies the structural rack, rolling modules, control components, installation and commissioning. When different suppliers are involved, nominate one party to coordinate interface information.

Which interfaces need an agreed drawing?

Show pallet contact surfaces, rack support points, lane elevations and equipment approach together. Record tolerances and responsibility for each dimension. The drawing should make it possible to find the owner of a mismatch without guessing at the installation site.

Include the supporting floor and building constraints in the rack design inputs. Show loading and retrieval work areas, columns, services and required routes on the layout.

How should the accepted pallet envelope be documented?

Define the intended range rather than submitting one average pallet. The lightest, heaviest and least favourable underside may behave differently on the same lane.

  • Overall dimensions and runner orientation.
  • Bottom-board contact surfaces, gaps and permitted damage.
  • Minimum and maximum loaded mass.
  • Load distribution, stability and packaging overhang.
  • Temperature, moisture and other exposure.
  • Inspection and rejection rules at receiving.

Assign references to representative sample pallets. If mixed pallet constructions are needed, have each reviewed. A nominally equal width and depth does not establish equivalent rolling behaviour.

What should movement-control documentation explain?

Ask the supplier to identify how the specified system controls travel and presents the front pallet for retrieval. Record the purpose of each selected component and its operating limitations.

Interroll's manufacturer overview shows speed-control and pallet-release technologies within its pallet-flow offering. These examples illustrate separate system functions; their product settings and ratings must be confirmed for the actual application.

  • How the pallet travels within its permitted load range.
  • How front-pallet retrieval is separated from following loads where specified.
  • How the load is stopped and presented at the retrieval face.
  • How abnormal movement is recognised and reported.
  • Which actions require a trained service person.

Keep adjustment settings and service methods in the supplied technical documentation. Warehouse staff need clear operating instructions rather than an invitation to experiment with the mechanism.

What should representative-load acceptance cover?

Agree the acceptance plan before production so the supplier and buyer understand what evidence is required. The plan should relate directly to the approved pallet envelope and intended operation.

  1. Identify sample loads and their recorded condition.
  2. Check installed geometry against the approved drawings.
  3. Verify specified components and protection are present.
  4. Observe loading, travel, stopping and retrieval under the supplier's procedure.
  5. Record exceptions and corrective work.
  6. Confirm operating information and training handover.

Define who witnesses the trials and who signs acceptance. Do not treat one successful movement of one pallet as proof for every weight, underside and operating condition.

How should quotations and handover files be compared?

Use one scope matrix for every bidder. Separate supplied equipment, installation, trials, training, documents, freight and exclusions. Ask who coordinates the structural rack and dynamic components.

Gravity flow pallet racking systems: common questions

Can the rack and rollers come from different suppliers?

They can be procured separately when the interfaces, design information and coordination responsibilities are explicitly agreed and reviewed.

Does gravity flow mean no control components?

No. The selected system may use speed control, release, separation and stopping components to manage movement.

Is one sample pallet enough for acceptance?

It may not represent the intended range. Agree representative loads covering relevant pallet and weight variations.

Can operators change the lane slope?

Configuration changes require the supplier's review and authorised procedure. Slope affects the complete movement system.

What should be sent for an initial proposal?

Send pallet details and samples, weight range, inventory, throughput, equipment, floor plan, environment, location and required project scope.
