Cantilever Racking System

A cantilever racking system combines bases, uprights, arms and bracing to store long or bulky materials with an open loading face. The correct configuration follows the material and handling process, not a standard rack label.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 23 November 2026

The short answer

A cantilever racking system stores pipe, tube, bar, timber, panels, profiles and other long or irregular goods on projecting arms fixed to uprights. A complete system includes bases, uprights, arms, bracing, connections, anchors, load notices and any restraints or protection required for the approved material. Selection depends on material dimensions, maximum bundle weight, stiffness, supports per load, arm length and spacing, levels, single- or double-sided rows, handling equipment, aisle, floor or foundation, environment and installation scope.

Which parts make up a cantilever racking system?

A cantilever system works as an assembly. Arms carry the material and transfer load into uprights; uprights connect to bases and bracing; bases and anchors transfer reactions to the supporting floor or foundation.

For high-load applications, also review the heavy duty cantilever racking guide.

What material data should drive system selection?

Material propertyRequired information
LengthMinimum, typical and maximum
WeightMaximum bundle or unit—not average
Depth/diameterBundle size, nesting and overhang
StiffnessFlexible, rigid or sensitive to support spacing
SurfaceDamage, slip or separation concerns
PackagingBanding, dunnage, stillage or loose pieces

Prepare a material schedule. One rack row may need different approved zones for different product families.

Single-sided or double-sided cantilever rack?

LayoutPotential useQuestions
Single-sidedAgainst a suitable boundary or for one-face accessClearance, wall/building separation and stability
Double-sidedFree-standing rows with access from both sidesAisles, load balance, row spacing and handling flow

Do not treat a single-sided row as wall-supported unless the approved design explicitly includes a building connection. Reserve material overhang and truck maneuvering space at row ends.

How are arm length, spacing and levels determined?

  • Arm length supports the material depth without creating unsafe projection or inadequate support.
  • Arm spacing follows material length, stiffness, weight and allowed overhang.
  • Arms per load provide stable support for the governing product.
  • Vertical level spacing includes material height, handling clearance and future adjustment.
  • Arm and upright limits are checked together for the proposed arrangement.

Similar-looking arms are not automatically interchangeable. Keep component identity and approved configuration records.

How does handling equipment shape the system?

  1. Identify forklift, side-loader, crane or manual handling.
  2. Record lift height, fork spread, turning and load approach.
  3. Calculate aisle and end-of-row maneuvering space for the longest load.
  4. Check overhead, column and neighboring-rack clearances.
  5. Define pedestrian interaction and protected areas.
  6. Trial unusual products or attachments before finalizing the layout.

Long loads sweep through more space than the vehicle footprint suggests. Model the complete movement route.

Which floor, foundation and environment details are needed?

  • Slab or foundation records and proposed base positions.
  • Joints, edges, slopes and embedded services.
  • Indoor or outdoor location and environmental actions.
  • Corrosion exposure, drainage and finish requirements.
  • Base plates, anchors and any foundation work by others.
  • Inspection and maintenance access.

Outdoor use requires project-specific review of environmental loads, durability and foundation conditions—not only a different paint finish.

What should a cantilever racking system quote include?

  • Material schedule and load assumptions.
  • Plans and elevations showing rows, arms, levels and aisles.
  • Single- or double-sided configuration.
  • Arms, uprights, bases, bracing, anchors and restraints.
  • Component and complete-system limits for the approved arrangement.
  • Finish, environment and protection scope.
  • Engineering, freight, unloading, installation and inspection.
  • Final drawings, load notices and maintenance information.

Ask every supplier to list exclusions. Compare the same number of positions, supports, row length and service scope.

How should the system be used and modified?

Load only the approved materials and positions. Keep aisles clear, report impact or deformation, isolate unsafe areas and follow documented inspection and repair routes.

Change rule: moving arms changes the upright load arrangement. Review new levels, materials and weights before altering the configuration.

Do not drill, weld, force-fit or mix unidentified components. Update drawings and load notices after approved changes.

Cantilever racking system: common questions

What materials can cantilever rack store?

Common candidates include pipe, tube, bar, timber, profiles, sheet packs and irregular long items when the system is designed for their actual dimensions and weights.

Is double-sided rack always more efficient?

No. It can use a central row well, but aisle, access, handling flow, load arrangement and building geometry determine efficiency.

Can arms be repositioned?

Potentially within the documented system rules, but the revised arm and upright load arrangement must remain approved.

Can it be used outside?

Potentially, when environmental actions, durability, drainage, foundations or floor and maintenance are addressed.

What information makes a quotation useful?

Send material dimensions, maximum weights, stiffness and packaging, quantities, handling equipment, desired locations, site plan, floor/foundation data, environment, destination and installation scope.
