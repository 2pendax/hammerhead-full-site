Push Back Pallet Racking

Push back pallet racking stores pallets on nested carts or another specified moving support system. Loading and retrieval take place from the same aisle face.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 16 November 2026

Plan Your Rack Specification →
The short answer

Push back pallet racking is a multiple-depth storage system loaded and unloaded from one aisle. In a cart-based system, each incoming pallet moves previously stored loads backwards on inclined rails. When the front load is retrieved, the remaining carts return toward the aisle under the specified operating procedure. Each lane normally follows last-in, first-out rotation, so select lane depth around actual SKU quantities and retrieval needs.

How does a pushback lane work?

Nested carts carry pallets along rails that slope toward the service aisle. The truck works at the lane face rather than entering a drive-in lane. Cart geometry, wheels, rails, stops and pallet contact points form a matched system.

The MHI rack overview identifies pushback as a LIFO system using moving supports on an incline. Obtain the instructions for the specific supplied mechanism.

Which inventory is suitable for push back pallet racking?

Start with peak pallets per SKU and the permitted rotation rule. A lane should hold stock that can share one retrieval sequence without making an urgently needed pallet inaccessible.

QuestionGood fit to evaluatePotential difficulty
Pallet quantitySeveral pallets per stock groupMany single-pallet SKUs
RotationLIFO acceptable within the laneStrict FIFO or date sequence
Pallet conditionConsistent and documentedBroken or mixed undersides
RetrievalFront-facing lane pickingFrequent access to a specific rear pallet

Different levels may hold different stock groups when designed and managed accordingly. Identify lane, level and SKU in the location system. Mixing batches needs explicit controls where expiry or traceability matters.

How should lane depth be selected?

Deeper lanes can reduce aisle allocation, but increase the quantity of stock tied to one access point. Compare candidate depths using the same peak inventory snapshot.

  1. List pallets by SKU and rotation group.
  2. Allocate complete and partly occupied lanes.
  3. Count empty positions that cannot be assigned to other groups.
  4. Check receiving and dispatch peaks.
  5. Compare storage use with relocation and waiting moves.

For example, a four-position lane assigned three pallets has one temporarily unused position. This arithmetic describes allocation only. It does not establish structural capacity, achieved density or the suitability of any lane depth.

What pallet and load details must be supplied?

Moving supports need reliable contact with the pallet underside. Provide actual pallet samples or clear underside photographs as well as dimensions.

  • Maximum and minimum loaded weight.
  • Width, depth, height and load overhang.
  • Runner direction, bottom-board spacing and bearing surfaces.
  • Load distribution and stability.
  • Acceptable pallet condition and rejection rules.
  • Temperature, moisture and other relevant exposure.

Ask the supplier to review representative loads and define the accepted pallet envelope. A pallet suitable on static beams may interact differently with carts. Specify any container feet or unusual contact points separately.

Which forklift requirements should be checked?

Confirm the truck can handle the load at the required height, place it accurately on the supplied supports and control retrieval according to the system instructions.

Review fork dimensions, spread, attachment, side shift where specified, lift capacity, load centre, aisle envelope and visibility. The Steel King pushback manual is a manufacturer-specific example describing nested carts, inclined rails and controlled return during retrieval. Its equipment requirements belong to that product and must not be assumed for every pushback system.

Agree training and commissioning responsibilities before purchase. Product literature and this buying guide do not replace the supplied operating instructions.

How does pushback compare with drive-in and double-deep storage?

Use the drive-in guide and double-deep guide to compare the same stock profile across layouts.

What should commissioning and handover cover?

Agree acceptance criteria with the supplier before ordering. Review installed geometry, component identification, load signs and representative pallet behaviour through the supplied acceptance process.

  • Final lane and level drawings.
  • Approved pallet and load envelope.
  • Manufacturer operating and maintenance instructions.
  • Inspection responsibilities for carts, wheels, rails and stops.
  • Damage and abnormal-movement reporting.
  • Spare-part identification and replacement arrangements.

If a lane jams or returns abnormally, stop using the affected lane under site procedures and arrange the specified assessment. Uncontrolled entry or improvised release can expose people to moving loads.

Push back pallet racking: common questions

Is pushback rack FIFO?

It normally operates LIFO within each lane. Compare a flow system where strict FIFO is required.

Does the forklift enter the rack?

In the usual cart-based arrangement it works at the lane face. Confirm the supplied design and operating method.

Can any pallet be used?

No. The pallet underside, dimensions, condition and load must fit the approved moving-support system.

Is storage gain guaranteed?

No. Count usable positions from the final layout and inventory allocation, including partial lanes and working areas.
