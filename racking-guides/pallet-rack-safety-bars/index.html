Pallet Rack Safety Bars

Pallet rack safety bars span between front and rear beams to support or help retain pallets, containers and selected nonstandard loads. They must be chosen for the actual rack interface and loading task: a bar that fits between beams is not automatically strong enough, correctly positioned or suitable as a substitute for decking.

The short answer

Select pallet rack safety bars only after defining their job. A pallet support bar carries part of a pallet or load into the front and rear beams; a raised or shaped stop may help control position, but it does not guarantee that a pallet cannot fall. Confirm beam section and front-to-rear distance, bar end profile and engagement, clear span, load type and footprint, pallet construction and orientation, number and spacing of bars, bar capacity and the way load is shared. Check concentrated feet, runners, drums, bins and damaged pallets separately because they may not distribute weight like a sound standard pallet. The bar must seat securely without field cutting, drilling or improvised packing and must not obstruct beam connectors, sprinklers, flues or handling clearance. Capacity must come from traceable component data for the exact span and loading condition; do not divide the beam rating by the number of bars. Install bars at the approved positions, verify engagement at both beams and trial the actual load. Inspect displacement, bending, cracked welds, damaged ends, corrosion and impact, and assess beams and loads when a bar is damaged.

Support bar, pallet stop or fall-prevention system?

The name safety bar is used for different components. Define the required function before ordering.

FunctionWhat it doesWhat it does not prove
Pallet support barTransfers selected load into front and rear beamsWhole pallet or beam-level capacity
Raised support/stopSupports a load and may guide its rear positionGuaranteed fall prevention
Container supportSupports defined feet, skids or basesSuitability for every container
Rear stopLimits normal placement travel when designed to do soImpact barrier capacity
Decking or meshCreates broader support or retention coverageAutomatic replacement by a few bars

How a safety bar transfers load

Each contact point creates reactions in the bar and both beams. Beam capacity and bar capacity are separate checks, and unequal placement can prevent the assumed load sharing.

Beam depth, span and end fit

  • Identify the exact front and rear beam profiles.
  • Measure the approved bearing references, not an approximate bay depth.
  • Confirm minimum end engagement at both beams.
  • Use the bar at its verified clear span and orientation.
  • Check clips, tabs or formed ends that resist displacement.
  • Keep beam connectors and safety locks unobstructed.
  • Do not shorten, extend, drill or weld a bar in the field.
  • Do not use loose blocks or packing to make an incorrect bar fit.

Capacity depends on span and loading pattern

Obtain traceable capacity for the exact bar, span, support and load case. A uniformly distributed test result may not cover a concentrated foot or a narrow pallet runner.

  • Check single-bar bending, shear, end bearing and connection.
  • Verify the number of bars actually engaged by the load.
  • Consider unequal load sharing from pallet distortion or placement.
  • Check beam-level capacity and deflection separately.
  • Include impact or handling actions where the design requires them.
  • Use a qualified review for unusual, dynamic or high-consequence loads.

Never estimate bar capacity by dividing rack beam capacity by the number of bars. The components have different spans, sections and failure modes.

Quantity and spacing must follow the load footprint

  1. Map the load-bearing runners, feet or base.
  2. Allow for normal placement tolerance in both directions.
  3. Place every required bearing point over verified support.
  4. Check that the load cannot bridge onto only one bar.
  5. Confirm edge support and resistance to tipping or sliding.
  6. Coordinate bars with wire deck channels where both are used.
  7. Mark fixed positions if bars could be moved accidentally.
  8. Trial the actual pallet or container before commissioning.

Installation, commissioning and routine inspection

  1. Check bar identity against the approved component schedule.
  2. Inspect beams and bars for transport damage.
  3. Seat both ends fully in the correct orientation.
  4. Engage the specified anti-displacement feature.
  5. Set and record the approved spacing.
  6. Verify the load contacts all intended bars.
  7. Check flue and sprinkler clearances.
  8. Train operators not to move bars with forks or loads.

Routine inspections should look for missing or displaced bars, permanent bend, twist, cracked welds, damaged end hooks, corrosion, impact marks and changed loads. A damaged bar requires assessment of the supported load and adjacent beams.

Common safety-bar mistakes

  • Buying by bay depth without checking beam-seat geometry.
  • Calling a support bar a fall-arrest barrier.
  • Using two bars for every load without checking its base.
  • Assuming identical load sharing.
  • Applying a distributed-load rating to concentrated feet.
  • Substituting safety bars for full decking under loose items.
  • Leaving bars free to slide or lift during handling.
  • Field cutting, welding or packing an incorrect component.
  • Ignoring damaged pallets and shifted loads.
  • Changing the stored product without reviewing support layout.

Pallet rack safety bars: common questions

What do pallet rack safety bars do?

They span front and rear rack beams to provide defined support or positioning for compatible pallets and loads.

Are safety bars the same as wire decking?

No. Bars provide support at selected lines, while wire decking offers broader coverage; their capacities and suitable load types differ.

How many safety bars are needed per pallet?

The number follows the load footprint, bar capacity, spacing, placement tolerance and approved design, not a universal rule.

Can safety bars stop pallets from falling?

Some shaped components help position a load, but support bars alone are not automatically a tested fall-prevention system.

Can a bent safety bar be straightened?

Do not straighten and reuse it without an approved assessment; inspect the load, bar ends and beams and replace with a verified component.

Information to send for safety-bar selection

Send rack and beam identification, front-to-rear support dimension, bar end details, beam-level capacity, load and pallet drawings, maximum weight, bearing points, bars per load, desired spacing, decking and stops, handling equipment, environment and photographs.
