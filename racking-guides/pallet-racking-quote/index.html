Pallet Racking Quote

A useful pallet racking quote is based on a defined load, layout and project scope—not only a requested number of pallet positions. Buyers should issue the same technical and commercial information to every bidder, require assumptions and exclusions to be visible, and compare the complete engineered, delivered and installed system.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 24 November 2026

The short answer

For a complete pallet racking quote, send pallet dimensions, entry direction, underside, condition, loaded height and maximum verified weight; SKU and inventory profile; forklift model, lift height and aisle needs; building plan, clear height, floor and obstruction information; desired rack type, beam levels, pallet positions, protection and growth; project location, fire and seismic criteria, schedule and installation conditions. Ask each bidder to state the design basis, configuration capacities, drawings and engineering scope, exact component quantities, coating, guards and accessories, anchors and load signs, packing, freight, taxes or duties as applicable, installation, inspection, warranty, lead time, payment milestones and exclusions. Normalize these items before comparing price per position or total price.

What information is needed for a pallet racking quote?

The quality of a quotation cannot exceed the quality of the request. Create one revision-controlled enquiry package and require bidders to identify every assumption. If a site survey or pallet sample is needed, define it before final pricing.

Which pallet and inventory details affect the quote?

InputDetail to provide
PalletLength, width, entry direction, underside, tolerance and condition
LoadMaximum verified weight, distribution, stability, overhang and loaded height
InventorySKUs, pallets per SKU, seasonality, growth and reserve stock
RotationFIFO, LIFO, batch or expiry requirements
FlowReceipts, replenishment, dispatch and peak moves
ExceptionsDamaged pallets, odd sizes, non-palletized goods and quarantine

Do not use an average pallet weight as the design maximum. Separate common and exceptional loads so the supplier can zone them appropriately.

Which building and forklift inputs change rack pricing?

  • Scaled plan with usable dimensions, columns, doors, walls and staging.
  • Clear height, roof slope, lights, ducts, pipes and sprinkler locations.
  • Floor or slab information, joints, slopes and known anchor restrictions.
  • Forklift model, turning radius, mast height, capacity and required clearances.
  • Pedestrian routes, exits, fire equipment and protected building elements.
  • Project location, seismic and other destination design criteria.
  • Operating hours, installation access, occupied areas and shutdown windows.

Ask bidders to show the aisle and clearance assumptions on the layout. A capacity gain based on an incompatible forklift is not usable value.

What equipment quantities should the quotation identify?

  • Rack type, row lengths, bays, frames, beam levels and pallet positions.
  • Upright, beam, bracing and connection families tied to the design.
  • Pallet supports, wire decks, timber or steel shelves where required.
  • Row spacers, ties, stops, guides and system-specific accessories.
  • Frame guards, column protectors, end barriers and pedestrian protection.
  • Bases, shims, anchors, connector locks and load notices.
  • Spare components and future expansion interfaces.

Require a bill of quantities linked to the approved layout. Position count alone hides member combinations, accessories and protection that can materially change price.

Which engineering deliverables belong in a pallet rack quote?

DeliverableQuestion to resolve
Design basisWhich loads, criteria, standards and assumptions apply?
LayoutAre plans, elevations, clearances and protection shown?
CapacityIs capacity tied to frame, beam, level and configuration?
Floor interfaceAre base reactions, anchors and slab responsibilities defined?
ReviewWho provides destination engineer or authority submissions?
HandoverAre approved drawings, load signs and as-built records included?

Clarify the number of drawing revisions included and the process for scope changes. A quotation labeled engineered should state what engineering evidence will actually be delivered.

Which freight and installation costs are often missed?

  • Packing, labeling, loading, freight basis, insurance and destination handling.
  • Taxes, duties, permits or fees assigned under the agreed commercial terms.
  • Unloading, storage, material movement and waste disposal.
  • Installation labor, supervision, travel, tools, lifts, access equipment and anchors.
  • Site inductions, permits, restricted hours and occupied-area controls.
  • Floor survey, setting out, final inspection and punch-list correction.
  • Training, handover documents and spare-part delivery.

State the delivery term and exact named place. For international offers, compare total landed and installed scope rather than the ex-factory number.

How should lead time and payment milestones be evaluated?

  1. Separate survey, design, approval, production, inspection, transit and installation durations.
  2. Identify information and approvals required from the buyer at each stage.
  3. Define when lead time starts and which changes reset it.
  4. Tie payments to measurable deliverables rather than calendar dates alone.
  5. Require notice and recovery plans for critical schedule risk.
  6. Retain final acceptance obligations and closure of documented defects.

A short production promise is not the complete project schedule. Include drawing approval, shipping availability, customs and site readiness.

Mark every difference as included, excluded, allowance or unresolved. Calculate adjustment values before ranking bidders.

Which quotation red flags need clarification?

  • No defined pallet, maximum load or rack configuration.
  • Layout and position count without forklift or clearance assumptions.
  • Capacity claims based only on steel thickness or beam section.
  • Missing anchors, locks, guards, signs or installation consumables.
  • Undefined engineering, local approval or floor responsibilities.
  • Freight or installation described as estimated without a basis.
  • Substitutions permitted without buyer and engineering approval.
  • Warranty without response path, exclusions or replacement logistics.

Clarify red flags in writing and include the resolved answer in the final offer or contract documents.

Pallet racking quote: common questions

Can I request a quote using only pallet position count?

You can request a budget indication, but a firm engineered quote also needs pallet loads, configuration, forklift, building, floor, project criteria and scope.

Why do quotes for the same number of positions differ?

Frame and beam combinations, loads, levels, accessories, guards, coating, engineering, freight, installation and exclusions may be different.

Is price per pallet position useful?

Only after normalizing usable positions, capacity, configuration, complete equipment and delivered or installed scope.

Should installation be included?

Price it as a clear option or inclusion with labor, supervision, tools, lifts, anchors, site conditions, inspection and handover defined.

What should be attached to the purchase order?

Attach the approved quote, scope, assumptions, layout, capacities, bill of quantities, design criteria, exclusions, schedule, inspection, delivery, installation and acceptance terms.
