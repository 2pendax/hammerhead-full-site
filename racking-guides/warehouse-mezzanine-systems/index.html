Warehouse Mezzanine Systems

Warehouse mezzanine systems combine an elevated structure with access, protection and material transfer. Define the complete project package before comparing quotations.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 16 November 2026

Plan Your Rack Specification →
The short answer

Warehouse mezzanine systems bring together a supporting structure, floor surface, personnel access, edge protection, goods-transfer provisions and building interfaces. A useful procurement brief identifies who designs, supplies, installs and accepts each part. Coordinate the intended activities, loads, access routes and services so the finished installation works as one system. Compare complete scope and handover evidence rather than platform area alone.

What belongs in a complete mezzanine system?

Start with a list of functions. The structure supports the planned use, access lets people reach it, and transfer provisions move goods between levels. Building services and the operation below also need coordination.

For the initial space and activity decision, see the warehouse mezzanine planning guide. This page focuses on procuring the complete system.

How should supplier scope be recorded?

Use a scope matrix shared by all bidders. Every important item should be identified as supplied, by others, excluded or awaiting information.

PackageResponsibility to identify
Structure and deckDesign, manufacture, erection and final records
Stairs and protectionGeometry, connections and coordinated access
Goods transferEquipment, openings, landing and handoff
Building workFloor review, services and necessary site work
AcceptanceInspection, documentation and issue resolution

Identify one project contact for interface decisions. Separate suppliers can deliver a coordinated project when the drawing ownership and approval process are clear.

Which design inputs should be agreed first?

Record the planned use above and below the platform. The same area may need different design inputs when used for cartons, shelving, machinery or wheeled handling.

  • Stored items, maximum weights and placement.
  • Shelving, workstations and equipment.
  • Concentrated contacts and wheel routes.
  • People and working activities.
  • Building dimensions and clear heights.
  • Floor records and installation location.

Mark unknown information and assign a responsible person to resolve it. A quotation based on assumptions should state them so later changes can be evaluated fairly.

How should stairs and edge protection connect to the platform?

Personnel routes should fit the actual activity and the site requirements established by the project team. Show access positions and their effect on both floor levels.

  • Approach and circulation around stairs.
  • Connections and landing interfaces.
  • Exposed edges and openings.
  • Goods-transfer gates and their operating space.
  • Routes around workstations and stored items.
  • Inspection and maintenance access.

Confirm who supplies and checks each connection. A drawing of the platform alone may leave access or protection details unresolved.

What should be specified for lifts, gates or conveyors?

Define the goods, transfer rate and operating sequence before selecting equipment. A transfer point occupies space and creates loads and interfaces that the platform design must address.

  1. Describe the largest item and loaded handling unit.
  2. State normal and peak transfer demand.
  3. Show the approach, staging and receiving positions.
  4. Identify equipment and connection responsibilities.
  5. Define the authorised operating sequence.
  6. Agree inspection and handover requirements.

Mecalux's mezzanine overview discusses elevated space and ways of lifting goods. Its examples illustrate the need to coordinate the platform with material movement; they do not establish the equipment for another project.

Which building services should be reviewed?

Locate existing services and agree how the new structure affects them. Review required lighting, equipment connections and access with the responsible building specialists.

Include the supporting floor, columns, doors and operational routes on the coordinated plan. The area below may need to remain in use, so supports and installation zones must be understood before delivery.

  • Available floor and structural information.
  • Overhead services and clearances.
  • Equipment power and other connections.
  • Access for inspection and maintenance.
  • Site requirements and responsible reviewers.
  • Scope of any alterations by others.

How should installation be planned in an operating warehouse?

Agree a sequence that fits receiving, production and dispatch. Record temporary stock relocation and access arrangements as part of the project scope.

  1. Confirm site readiness and prerequisites.
  2. Reserve delivery, unloading and storage areas.
  3. Plan erection and equipment access.
  4. Coordinate work by other suppliers.
  5. Complete agreed inspections and corrective work.
  6. Hand over the installation before normal use.

Identify who controls each work area and how changes are communicated. Programme assumptions should be visible in the quotation rather than discovered after goods arrive.

What should a complete handover file contain?

RecordPurpose
Final drawingsShow the installed arrangement and interfaces
Load and use informationState the approved operational basis
Component referencesSupport identification and maintenance
InstructionsExplain use, inspection and reporting
Acceptance recordsRecord checks and resolved exceptions
Change processDefine review of later alterations

Keep records with the warehouse team that controls the space. Later changes in equipment or storage loads should be checked against the final design basis.

Warehouse mezzanine systems: common questions

Does a platform quotation include stairs and gates?

Only when the scope says so. List every access and transfer component in the comparison matrix.

Can several suppliers deliver the system?

Yes, when interface ownership, drawing coordination and acceptance responsibilities are agreed.

Are building services automatically included?

No. Identify required service work and the responsible supplier or building specialist.

Can equipment be added after handover?

Review its loads, contacts and interfaces against the installed design before adding it.

What improves the initial quotation?

Send intended uses, load placement, building plan and heights, goods movement, floor records, location and required package scope.
