Drive In Pallet Racking

Drive in pallet racking lets a forklift enter a storage lane to place pallets on side rails. It increases density by reducing aisles but limits direct pallet access.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 16 November 2026

Plan Your Rack Specification →
The short answer

Drive in pallet racking is a high-density system with pallets stored one wide and multiple positions deep on side rails. A forklift enters the lane from one aisle face to load and retrieve pallets. Because loading and unloading occur from the same side, the lane usually operates last-in, first-out. It best fits repeated pallets that can share a lane and requires tightly coordinated pallet, rail, forklift and clearance data.

How does drive in pallet racking work?

Unlike selective rack, drive-in rack has no front beam across each pallet opening. Pallets rest on rails running into the lane, leaving a path for the forklift to enter beneath the supported loads.

The RMI glossary describes drive-in rack as one-wide, multiple-depth storage using support arms and load rails, with loading and unloading from the same aisle.

Which inventory fits drive-in pallet rack?

Drive-in rack is a lane-storage system, so selection begins with pallets per SKU, rotation and batch rules. A lane works most simply when its pallets can be handled as one stock group.

What is the loading sequence in drive-in racking?

Same-side access normally creates a last-in, first-out sequence. A typical operating plan fills the deepest available position first and works toward the aisle, then unloads in the reverse order. The exact sequence must follow the supplied system instructions.

  1. Verify the assigned lane, load and pallet condition.
  2. Approach squarely and enter under the site's travel rules.
  3. Place the pallet at the designated deepest open position.
  4. Lower it onto both rails as intended and withdraw the forks.
  5. Reverse carefully out of the lane without contacting rack members.
  6. Update the warehouse location and lane status.

This is an explanatory sequence, not operator training. Equipment operation requires competent personnel, site procedures and the instructions for the actual rack and truck.

Which forklift and clearance data are required?

The forklift operates within the rack structure, so small dimensional errors can create repeated contact. Provide the equipment and load envelope before the lane is designed.

  • Truck overall width, overhead guard and mast geometry.
  • Pallet and load width, depth, height, weight and overhang.
  • Fork length, spread, lowered height and load-centre information.
  • Rail elevation, clear distance between rails and pallet bearing.
  • Upright, guide, arm and load clearances through the lane.
  • Floor condition, entry alignment, lighting and visibility.

Do not copy clearances from another building. Ask the rack and equipment suppliers to review one shared dimensioned interface drawing.

Does drive-in racking provide the highest usable capacity?

It can reduce aisle area, but the best usable capacity depends on the building and stock profile. Compare final candidate layouts and allocate real inventory into them.

  • Count all designed pallet positions.
  • Remove positions affected by columns, services or clearance restrictions.
  • Reserve staging, cross aisles, exits and equipment areas.
  • Apply whole-lane SKU and rotation rules.
  • Allow for seasonal stock changes and empty locations.
  • Compare moves per pallet and throughput with the density gain.
Illustrative lane arithmetic only

Ten lanes with four levels and five positions in depth contain 10 × 4 × 5 = 200 designed positions. This does not prove all positions are usable for the inventory, nor establish structural capacity or throughput.

How should forklift contact risk be managed?

Drive-in operation places the truck close to uprights, rails and support arms. Rack robustness does not replace alignment, training, visibility, protection and a prompt damage process.

  • Keep lane entries visible, square and free of temporary pallets.
  • Use guides or protection where the project risk assessment specifies them.
  • Set travel and placement procedures for the actual truck.
  • Report any rack contact immediately.
  • Isolate uncertain damage according to site procedures.
  • Use approved repair or replacement components and methods.

SafeWork NSW notes that specialised rack types including drive-in may require additional safety measures and that the rack should be designed for the product size, shape and weight. Apply the requirements relevant to the project location.

Drive in pallet racking: common questions

Is drive-in rack FIFO or LIFO?

With loading and unloading from the same face, a lane normally operates LIFO. Confirm the warehouse rotation rule for each product.

Does the forklift drive under stored pallets?

It enters the lane below supported pallet levels. The truck, load, rails and rack clearances must be coordinated in the project design.

Can different SKUs share one lane?

Mixing stock can block access and complicate rotation. Assign lanes only where the SKU, batch and retrieval rules remain workable.

Can any pallet be used on drive-in rails?

No. Provide pallet dimensions, underside, condition and load distribution so bearing and placement can be reviewed.

What information improves the first layout?

Send pallet dimensions and weights, peak pallets by SKU, rotation rules, a floor plan, forklift details, lift height, throughput and site location.
