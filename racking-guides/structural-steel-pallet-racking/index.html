Structural Steel Pallet Racking

Structural steel pallet racking should be purchased as an identified, engineered system—not as a list of heavy-looking steel channels.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 16 November 2026

Plan Your Rack Specification →
The short answer

Structural steel pallet racking uses hot-rolled structural steel members, commonly channels, in an engineered pallet-storage system. A useful specification identifies the exact upright, beam, bracing, connection, base, anchor, load and finish requirements plus the governing layout and handover documents. Do not approve a proposal from material thickness or total steel weight alone; compare complete systems for the same loads and site.

What belongs in a structural steel pallet racking specification?

A specification connects the stored loads and warehouse operation to the supplied rack. It should let the buyer trace each important assumption from enquiry through drawings, installation and handover.

Core specification sections
SectionInformation to state
ApplicationStorage arrangement, inventory access and handling process
LoadsPallet, level, bay and relevant site loads
MembersIdentified uprights, beams, bracing and load supports
ConnectionsBeam, brace, base and anchorage details
FinishPreparation, coating system, colour and repair method
DocumentsDrawings, calculations where required, instructions and load signs

The Rack Manufacturers Institute FAQ describes storage rack as a system of members and connections assembled into rows, bays and levels. Procurement should therefore evaluate the complete structure rather than isolated steel pieces.

Which structural steel rack components should be identified?

Component names should match the drawings and bill of materials. Generic labels such as "heavy channel" do not establish geometry, grade, connection behaviour or capacity.

Identify upright columns, horizontal and diagonal bracing, beams, end connections, fasteners, locks where used, base plates, anchors, pallet supports, decking and protective accessories.

Which design inputs should the supplier receive?

The steel section cannot be selected before the loads and geometry are known. Submit maximum values and the intended arrangement, not average stock data alone.

  • Loaded pallet width, depth, height, maximum weight and underside.
  • Number and placement of pallets on every supported level.
  • Beam span, frame depth and height, beam elevations and row length.
  • Forklift type, attachment, lift height and required clearances.
  • Dimensioned floor plan, slab information and building constraints.
  • Indoor or outdoor environment, temperature and other relevant exposure.
  • Installation location and project requirements identified with the local team.

RMI's standards catalogue describes the industrial steel storage rack standard as covering systems made from cold-formed or hot-rolled structural members. The applicable design and documentation requirements must be established for the actual project location.

What should be checked before approving structural rack fabrication?

Review drawings and schedules against the enquiry before production. Resolve substitutions, exclusions and missing site information in writing.

  1. Plan: rack rows, bays, aisles, work areas and building constraints.
  2. Elevations: frame heights, beam levels and stored-load clearances.
  3. Component schedule: member and connection references tied to each location.
  4. Load basis: unit, level and bay loads plus the assumed distribution.
  5. Base details: base plates, anchors and required slab inputs.
  6. Finish schedule: coating system and treatment of handling or installation damage.
  7. Responsibilities: design, permitting, freight, unloading, erection and inspection scope.

A manufacturer example such as Steel King's structural rack listing shows hot-rolled channels as product-specific uprights and beams. Do not copy that product's section, connection or rating into another supplier's submittal.

How should finish and exposure be handled?

State where the rack will operate and what the finish is expected to do. A colour name alone does not define surface preparation, coating type, thickness, inspection or repair.

Outdoor use requires a project designed for wind, weather and other relevant conditions; an indoor structural rack should not simply be moved outside.

What should be verified at delivery and handover?

Receiving records should connect delivered parts to the approved bill of materials. Keep components identified and protect them from uncontrolled substitution.

  • Check member and fastener references, quantities and visible transport damage.
  • Quarantine unidentified or nonconforming components.
  • Install from the approved drawings and manufacturer instructions.
  • Record authorised field changes and update affected documentation.
  • Confirm load signs match the final configuration.
  • Hand over inspection, damage-reporting and change-control information.

Steel King's structural rack assembly guide warns purchasers not to substitute parts or hardware without written approval and says project specifics can require special techniques. Apply the instructions for the actual supplied system.

Structural steel pallet racking: common questions

Is structural steel rack specified by steel weight?

No. Total steel weight does not establish member geometry, connection behaviour, configuration or project capacity. Review the complete system.

Should the steel grade be shown?

The supplier's project documents should identify the materials and component references required for traceability and design.

Can suppliers substitute a similar channel?

Do not accept an uncontrolled substitution. Require the proposed component and its effect on connections, loads and documentation to be reviewed and approved.

Does powder coating make rack suitable outdoors?

Not by itself. Outdoor use needs a project design and finish specification for the actual exposure and relevant environmental loads.

What belongs in the final handover file?

Include final drawings, load information and signs, component references, instructions, approved changes and the agreed inspection and reporting records.
