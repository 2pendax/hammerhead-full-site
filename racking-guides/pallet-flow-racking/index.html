Pallet Flow Racking

Pallet flow racking feeds pallets from a loading face toward a retrieval face on inclined rolling lanes. Plan stock rotation and replenishment together.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 16 November 2026

Plan Your Rack Specification →
The short answer

Pallet flow racking stores pallets in inclined lanes with rollers or wheels that move loads toward the retrieval face. Loading at the opposite face supports first-in, first-out storage within each correctly managed lane. Select it around repeated pallet quantities, rotation needs, pallet underside quality, weight range and the rate of replenishment and retrieval. The rack and moving components must be specified together.

How does pallet flow racking work?

Each lane connects a loading position with a retrieval position. Gravity supplies movement along the incline; the specified rollers, speed-control components, stops and separation devices manage the pallet movement and presentation.

Interroll's pallet-flow overview describes gravity lanes with speed control and separation components. Product-specific slopes and ratings must be confirmed for the selected system.

When is pallet flow racking worth evaluating?

It is a candidate when several pallets of the same stock group need predictable rotation and a compact storage footprint. Evaluate the peak stock profile before choosing lane quantities or depths.

Keep different products or date groups separated wherever sharing would block the intended sequence. A lane is a queue: older stock must remain ahead of newer stock.

Does pallet flow guarantee first-in, first-out inventory?

It supports FIFO within a lane when stock enters at the loading face and leaves at the retrieval face. Warehouse-wide FIFO also depends on which lane is selected and how receipt dates, batches and exceptions are recorded.

  1. Assign a clear stock group to each lane.
  2. Label loading and retrieval faces.
  3. Record pallet identity and date at receipt.
  4. Select the oldest eligible lane for retrieval.
  5. Prevent incorrect loading from the retrieval face.
  6. Investigate mixed stock and blocked pallets.

A physical flow lane cannot correct an incorrect warehouse allocation. Agree how the inventory system directs replenishment and dispatch before ordering the rack.

Which pallet information should be supplied?

Rollers contact the underside rather than supporting a pallet exactly as static beams do. Supply samples or clear photographs and a documented load envelope.

  • Minimum and maximum loaded weight.
  • Pallet width, depth, height and runner orientation.
  • Bottom-board dimensions, gaps and contact surfaces.
  • Load distribution, centre of gravity and stability.
  • Packaging, overhang and loose wrapping.
  • Temperature, moisture and other operating exposure.

Include the lightest intended load as well as the heaviest. Agree representative pallet trials and acceptance criteria with the supplier. Variations in underside condition can change rolling behaviour even when nominal pallet dimensions match.

How should storage and throughput be compared?

Count positions from the dimensioned layout, then test whether stock allocation and peak handling can use them. Include working aisles and staging at both faces.

  1. List peak pallets by SKU and rotation group.
  2. Allocate full and partly occupied lanes.
  3. Reserve building access and service clearances.
  4. Map hourly receiving and retrieval peaks.
  5. Check staging and equipment availability.
  6. Compare cost per usable position and expected handling effort.

For example, eight lanes with five positions each provide forty designed positions. If stock allocation leaves six unusable for other groups, only thirty-four are occupied in that snapshot. This example establishes neither structural capacity nor a guaranteed utilisation rate.

What belongs in a pallet flow racking proposal?

Request a coordinated rack and dynamic-component package. The offer should connect the pallet envelope to lane geometry and the final acceptance process.

  • Layout, lane depths, levels and usable positions.
  • Rack loads, configuration and relevant site inputs.
  • Roller or wheel arrangement and identified components.
  • Specified speed control, separation and end-stop functions.
  • Accepted pallets, loads and operating environment.
  • Representative-load trials and commissioning responsibilities.
  • Freight, unloading, installation, inspection and handover scope.

Ask for exclusions and unresolved inputs in writing. A price for the rack steel alone cannot be compared directly with a complete installed flow system.

What should handover and maintenance cover?

Operators need the supplier's procedures for placement, retrieval, abnormal movement and damage reporting. Agree inspection responsibilities for rolling components and structural members.

Keep the approved pallet envelope, final drawings, load signs, operating instructions and spare-part references together. Changes in pallet type, weight range or environment should trigger a review with the supplier.

If a pallet stalls or moves abnormally, follow the supplied isolation and recovery procedure. Accessing a loaded lane or releasing a blockage needs an assessed method. This buying guide does not provide recovery instructions.

Pallet flow racking: common questions

Is pallet flow normally FIFO?

Yes, when pallets load at one face and leave at the opposite face, with lane assignment preserving the intended sequence.

Can any pallet roll on the lanes?

No. The underside, dimensions, condition, weight range and stability must match the approved system.

Is one slope suitable for every installation?

No. The selected component system, pallets, loads and environment determine the engineered arrangement.

Does pallet flow remove all forklift handling?

No. Loading and retrieval still need the specified handling equipment and suitable working areas.

What is needed for the first quote?

Send pallet samples or underside photos, dimensions, weight range, quantities by SKU, rotation rules, throughput, floor plan and installation location.
