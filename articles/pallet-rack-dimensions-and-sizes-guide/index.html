RACK SIZING GUIDE

Pallet Rack Dimensions and Sizes

Build rack dimensions from the loaded pallet and operation instead of forcing the load into a catalogue size.

Original guide by zhengdarack · Updated 21 November 2026

Short answer

Pallet-rack size is a coordinated set of dimensions: clear beam length, frame depth, beam elevations, upright height, row width, aisle width and building clearances. Start with the maximum real loaded-pallet envelope and weight, choose the number of pallets per level, apply approved horizontal and vertical clearances, coordinate forklift and fire-protection needs, and then select engineered rack components. There is no universal “standard rack size” suitable for every pallet or warehouse.

Measure the loaded pallet, not only the empty pallet

Record the greatest envelope expected in normal operation. Product can overhang, lean, bulge or shift beyond the pallet deck. Stretch wrap, labels and corner boards also affect clearance.

  • Pallet length and width, with fork-entry orientation identified.
  • Maximum load width, depth and height.
  • Maximum gross weight, including pallet and packaging.
  • Bottom-board and opening arrangement.
  • Load centre, instability, overhang and deformation.
  • Condition range for new, repaired and reusable pallets.
  • Special supports, drums, bins, stillages or non-pallet loads.
  • Temperature, moisture or contamination that can change the load.

Create families where the warehouse handles several load envelopes. The largest dimensions and heaviest weight may not belong to the same SKU, so keep both cases visible.

Useful field method: measure a representative sample of the worst loads in their actual stored condition. A supplier's nominal product dimension is not always the operating envelope.

How is pallet-rack beam length determined?

Clear beam length must fit the planned pallet count plus horizontal spaces between loads and uprights. The exact clearances come from the approved rack design, handling method and applicable requirements—not from a universal formula.

Planning relationship: clear beam length = total loaded-pallet widths + required spaces between pallets + required end spaces.

Example only: if a level holds two loaded pallets, add both maximum loaded widths and the approved left, centre and right clearances. Then choose an available engineered beam length that provides the required usable opening. Do not treat the arithmetic as structural selection.

Longer beams can reduce the number of upright frames but can change beam capacity, deflection, rack cost and load handling. RMI's FAQ notes that beam deflection criteria relate to beam length, illustrating why length and section must be evaluated together.

How deep should a pallet-rack frame be?

Frame depth is coordinated with the pallet dimension running into the rack and the intended support condition. Selective pallet rack often supports a pallet at the front and rear beams with controlled overhang, but the correct arrangement depends on pallet construction, rack type, load and accessories.

Too shallow can create excessive or unintended overhang and unstable support. Too deep can change where the pallet contacts the beams or interfere with placement. Decking does not automatically correct an unsuitable pallet or rack depth.

  • Confirm which pallet dimension runs into the rack.
  • Identify the pallet members that bear on the beams.
  • Define front and rear pallet position.
  • Check wall, column, sprinkler and adjacent-row clearances.
  • Coordinate pallet stops or load stops without using them as impact targets.
  • Review non-standard pallets and loads separately.

Use the manufacturer or engineer's approved load-support arrangement. Do not copy the frame depth from a different pallet orientation.

Calculate vertical spacing level by level

A beam elevation must allow the tallest approved loaded pallet to enter, clear the beam above and be placed without striking the rack, sprinklers or building services. Include beam depth and any deck or support thickness when calculating the next level.

Planning relationship: level pitch = loaded-pallet height + approved vertical handling clearance + beam/support depth affecting the next opening.

The lowest level may sit on the floor or on beams. Floor storage changes forklift approach and can expose lower uprights to different impact risks. For each level, show:

  • Top of supporting beam or floor elevation.
  • Maximum load height.
  • Required handling clearance.
  • Beam depth above.
  • Top of load elevation.
  • Sprinkler or overhead-service relationship.

Do not raise a beam in the field simply to fit a taller pallet. Beam spacing affects upright capacity and can affect the approved rack configuration.

What controls total pallet-rack height?

Upright height and top load height are different. The highest usable level depends on the forklift, load, building, fire protection and rack design.

OSHA's forklift guidance tells operators to check adequate overhead clearance before raising a load. The rack layout should remove known conflicts before operation rather than rely only on operator reaction.

How wide should the forklift aisle be?

Aisle width is a truck-and-load dimension, not a rack-only dimension. Obtain the equipment manufacturer's right-angle stacking or equivalent data for the actual truck configuration, load length, attachment and clearance assumptions. Then coordinate project operating allowance, guidance systems, pedestrian routes and rack protection.

  • Truck model, wheelbase and overall dimensions.
  • Fork, carriage and attachment geometry.
  • Load length and pallet overhang.
  • Required manoeuvre and stacking direction.
  • Turning, travel and safety clearances.
  • Floor condition, slopes and column pinch points.
  • One-way or two-way traffic and pedestrian controls.
  • Upright guards or barriers projecting into the aisle.

OSHA's narrow-aisle eTool illustrates that different storage systems and trucks have different aisle needs. Treat any example dimension as contextual, not a design value for another vehicle.

Coordinate back-to-back rows and flue spaces

A double row includes two rack depths plus the designed separation and any pallet overhang, row spacers, in-rack sprinklers or longitudinal flue requirements. The operational outside-to-outside width may be greater than the steel-frame dimension.

Show transverse and longitudinal flue spaces on the plan where required by the approved fire-protection design. Pallet stops, loads, wrap and misplaced stock must not close spaces that the system depends on.

Planning relationship: double-row envelope = rack depth + designed row separation + rack depth + approved front/rear load projections.

At walls or building columns, coordinate actual load overhang, frame protection, inspection access and services. Do not dimension only to the upright steel.

Dimensions do not establish rack capacity

Two beams with the same length or depth can have different capacity because of section, material, connector, manufacturing and design differences. Upright capacity depends on frame section, bracing, beam spacing, loads and configuration. Never infer capacity by measuring a used component.

  • Use identified, compatible components.
  • Obtain approved configuration and load information.
  • Show maximum unit, level, bay or frame loads as applicable.
  • Keep beam elevations within the approved configuration.
  • Provide load plaques where required.
  • Review changes before moving beams or adding levels.

OSHA's warehousing guidance tells employers not to exceed rack and shelving load capacities. The capacity must first be established from valid design or manufacturer information.

Put units on every drawing and schedule. Mixed inch, foot and metric inputs can create expensive errors. Identify whether a dimension is clear, outside-to-outside, centreline or nominal.

Pallet-rack dimension questions

What is a standard pallet-rack bay size?

There is no single universal bay size. Beam length follows the pallet count, actual loaded-pallet width, required clearances and the selected rack system's approved components and capacity.

Should rack depth equal pallet depth?

Not automatically. The pallet must be supported as intended, with approved front and rear overhang or other support conditions. Pallet construction, load stability, decking and system type all affect frame depth.

Does a taller upright always create more storage?

No. Top load height, sprinkler and roof clearances, forklift lift height, beam depth, vertical clearances, seismic design and load capacity can limit usable levels.

How wide should a forklift aisle be?

Use the handling-equipment manufacturer's data for the actual truck, load, attachment, operating method and required manoeuvre, then add project operating and safety allowances. Do not copy a generic aisle width.

Can the beam capacity be chosen from its height alone?

No. Capacity depends on the exact beam section, length, material, connectors, bracing assumptions, load distribution and manufacturer or engineered data.
