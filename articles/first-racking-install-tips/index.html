FIRST INSTALLATION GUIDE

First Pallet Racking Installation: Planning Tips

Control the information, site, delivery, erection, anchoring and handover—not just the rack components.

Original guide by zhengdarack · Updated 21 November 2026

Short answer

A successful first pallet-rack installation begins before the delivery truck arrives. Freeze the approved rack configuration and loads, verify the building and floor, coordinate permits and fire protection, define installation responsibilities, plan safe delivery and work zones, follow the manufacturer or engineer's erection and anchoring information, inspect the completed rack, and hand over drawings, load plaques and operating limits before loading.

1. Freeze the approved rack inputs

Do not install from a sales sketch. The erection team needs current, approved information that matches the supplied components and the intended use.

  • Final rack plan, elevations and row identification.
  • Pallet dimensions, maximum loads and support conditions.
  • Beam elevations and maximum bay or level loads.
  • Required accessories, decking, stops, guards and row spacers.
  • Base plates, anchors and supporting-floor requirements.
  • Aisles, exits, doors, equipment clearances and rack protection.
  • Sprinkler, lighting, building-service and code coordination.
  • Approved field-change and inspection process.

Give each drawing a revision and issue status. Remove superseded copies from the work area so different crews do not build different versions.

First-install mistake: ordering rack before the real pallet, load and forklift are confirmed. A layout can look efficient while the beam, upright, aisle or clearances are wrong for the operation.

2. Survey the building and supporting floor

Compare the approved layout with field dimensions. Record columns, walls, doors, pits, drains, joints, slopes, curbs, mezzanine columns, electrical panels, sprinkler risers and overhead obstructions. Check the route used to bring long frames and beams into the building.

Available floor information should be reviewed by the responsible parties. Mark joints, edges, trenches, embedded services and other conditions that can affect bases or drilling. Do not promise that every anchor location is clear before the survey and approved response process are defined.

3. Define exactly who supplies and installs each item

A rack quotation and an installation quotation may use the same project name but cover different work. Create a responsibility matrix.

  • Permits, engineering, calculations and stamped documents where required.
  • Survey, layout control and floor investigation.
  • Rack, anchors, shims, decking and protection accessories.
  • Freight, unloading, lifting equipment and component storage.
  • Erection, anchoring, cleanup and disposal.
  • Sprinkler, electrical, lighting and other building changes.
  • Inspection, correction, load plaques and handover documents.
  • Operational shutdowns, barricades and traffic detours.

State assumptions such as working hours, floor access, indoor storage, power availability and whether the building remains operational.

4. Plan delivery around the erection sequence

Confirm component quantities and labels before shipment. Arrange bundles so the next erection area can be accessed without repeatedly moving heavy material. Protect frames, beams and connectors from impact, weather and unstable stacking.

  1. Verify vehicle access, dock or unloading location and ground conditions.
  2. Reserve a controlled unloading and staging zone.
  3. Check lifting equipment capacity and fork length for the actual bundles.
  4. Inspect deliveries for shortage, wrong components and transit damage.
  5. Quarantine damaged or unidentified parts.
  6. Keep aisles, exits, fire equipment and operating routes clear.

Do not lean upright frames against walls or other rack without a controlled method. Long components can move unexpectedly and create struck-by hazards.

5. Separate installation work from warehouse traffic

Create a site-specific work plan appropriate to the erection method and facility. Control forklifts, mobile equipment, pedestrians, work at height, falling objects and temporary instability. Define who may enter the zone and who can change the plan.

OSHA's warehousing guidance emphasises rack inspection, isolation of damaged areas, secure storage and vehicle hazards. It is not an erection method; the employer and project team still need task-specific controls and applicable regulatory review.

  • Barricade the active erection and overhead-work area.
  • Use suitable access equipment; do not climb rack frames.
  • Keep incomplete frames temporarily stable under the approved sequence.
  • Control tools and loose parts at height.
  • Stop work when field conditions differ from the drawings.
  • Coordinate emergency routes throughout every phase.

6. Erect the rack to the approved configuration

Identify frames, beams, braces, connectors and accessories before assembly. Do not mix components merely because they appear to fit. Follow the manufacturer's assembly and handling information.

Establish rack lines and reference points. Assemble frames correctly, stand and temporarily stabilise them, install beams and locking devices, and progress in the approved sequence. Keep the rack plumb and aligned while finalising connections and bases.

One published example is an RMI-hosted manufacturer's selective pallet rack assembly instruction, which advises checking plumb and level before fully tightening certain accessories and checking anchor connections at completion. Use the instructions for the actual supplied system, not this example as a substitute.

No field improvisation: do not weld, drill, cut, heat, bend or substitute a structural rack component unless an authorised design instruction permits it.

7. Install bases and anchors under approved instructions

RMI's current FAQ says all rack columns are to be anchored and directs users to the manufacturer for the correct quantity and size. Extra holes in a base plate can be alternatives; they are not proof that every hole should be filled.

  • Verify the rack line, column position and base-plate bearing.
  • Use only the specified anchor and approved hole location.
  • Follow drilling, cleaning, embedment, setting and tightening instructions.
  • Record reinforcement, service, failed-hole and concrete conflicts.
  • Obtain an approved response before relocating a hole or column.
  • Complete required inspection or testing.

Shims, grout or levelling details are part of the base condition and must follow the approved rack information. Do not use loose scrap as packing.

8. Inspect before the first pallet is loaded

Create a completion checklist tied to the drawings and scope. The installer should perform its own quality check before presenting the rack for the agreed acceptance review.

Define who can release the rack for use. An installer leaving the site is not automatically the same as formal acceptance.

9. Hand over the information needed to operate the rack

  • Final layout and load application and rack configuration information.
  • Load plaques located where users can read them.
  • Manufacturer instructions and approved field changes.
  • Installation, anchor and inspection records required by the project.
  • Open-item list and evidence of closure.
  • Inspection, damage reporting and maintenance responsibilities.
  • Rules for changing beam levels, loads or rack locations.

Train supervisors and operators not to climb rack, overload positions, move beams outside the approved configuration, or continue using visibly damaged areas. Keep spare parts controlled and identifiable.

First pallet-rack installation questions

Can pallet rack be installed before the final layout is approved?

Do not treat a preliminary sketch as erection information. Confirm the approved layout, configuration, loads, base locations, clearances and project responsibilities before installation.

Can installers move a column when reinforcement blocks an anchor hole?

Not without an approved response. Moving the column or selecting another base-plate hole can affect rack geometry, aisle clearance and anchoring. Record the conflict and obtain the required direction.

When can pallets be loaded after installation?

Only after the agreed installation, anchoring, inspection, documentation and release steps are complete. The project team should define who has authority to release the rack.

Should beam levels be changed during installation?

Only within the approved configuration and instructions. Beam elevation changes can affect upright capacity, clearances, sprinkler coordination and load plaques.

What records should the owner receive?

Keep final layout and configuration drawings, load information, installation and inspection records, approved field changes, product instructions and maintenance guidance.
