MEZZANINE APPROVAL GUIDE

Mezzanine Permit Requirements

Define the use, structure, egress, accessibility and fire-protection interfaces before drawings enter permit review.

Original guide by zhengdarack · Updated 21 November 2026

Short answer

A warehouse mezzanine commonly requires review and permits because it adds an elevated occupied or storage level, structural loads, stairs, guards and building-system interfaces. Requirements depend on the adopted code, local amendments, jurisdiction, occupancy, use, size, openness and existing building. Contact the building and fire authorities early, classify the proposed level correctly, and do not order or install from an unapproved concept.

Which authority issues the mezzanine permit?

Start with the jurisdiction where the building is physically located. The building department may lead the structural and building-code review, while the fire authority reviews storage, suppression, alarm, access and operational conditions. Planning, accessibility, environmental or other agencies may also have a role.

  • Confirm the exact site address, parcel and suite.
  • Identify the building and fire authorities having jurisdiction.
  • Ask which code editions and local amendments are adopted.
  • Describe the existing building use and proposed mezzanine use.
  • Ask about pre-application meetings, required forms and electronic submittal rules.
  • Confirm professional-license and sealing requirements.
  • Record permit fees, review stages, inspection steps and expiry conditions.
Avoid the internet-exemption trap: a size limit or exception found online may belong to another city, code edition or type of platform. Obtain project-specific confirmation.

Is it a mezzanine, equipment platform or another structure?

Names used in sales materials do not control code classification. Describe the actual function, access, occupants, equipment, storage, enclosure and relationship to the room below.

The 2024 International Building Code's publicly available Chapter 5 treats mezzanines and equipment platforms separately and sends mezzanine egress to Chapter 10. Your jurisdiction may use another edition or local amendments, so use the adopted text and authority interpretation.

Define the mezzanine use and design loads

The design team needs more than a generic uniform load. Describe people, storage, shelving, mobile equipment, concentrated contacts, partitions, services and future changes.

  • Occupancy and activity on and below the platform.
  • Maximum stored unit and total storage by zone.
  • Shelving post loads, equipment feet and wheel loads.
  • Rolling routes, turning, braking and parking locations.
  • Partitions, ceilings, stairs, gates, conveyors and lifts.
  • Guard, handrail and falling-object protection needs.
  • Environmental exposure, vibration or special process conditions.
  • Planned expansion directions and explicitly prohibited future uses.

Record design assumptions in the submittal. If the user cannot define a load, do not quietly choose a convenient value—assign responsibility for resolving it.

What belongs in the structural permit package?

The authority may require drawings and calculations prepared or sealed by appropriately licensed professionals. Coordinate the elevated frame with the existing slab, foundations and building.

Structural subjectInformation to show
FramingColumns, beams, joists, deck, connections and stability system
LoadsDead, live, storage, concentrated, lateral and other applicable actions
BasesBase plates, anchors, reactions and floor or foundation support
Existing buildingInterfaces, separations, attachments and affected construction

An existing slab-on-grade should not be assumed adequate simply because the platform is prefabricated. Provide available floor records and assign the support review to the responsible qualified party.

How do egress and accessibility affect the design?

Plan people movement at the concept stage. The number, location and width of exits or access to exits can depend on occupant load, travel, use and adopted requirements. Stair geometry, landings, headroom, guards, handrails, doors, gates and the path to the final exit all need coordination.

The public 2024 IBC Chapter 10 specifically addresses egress from spaces including mezzanines. ICC's accessibility pocketbook also discusses accessible means of egress from accessible mezzanine levels. The adopted local provisions may differ.

  • Determine who uses the level and the occupant load basis.
  • Trace each required route from the mezzanine to a safe discharge.
  • Coordinate accessible routes and accessible means of egress.
  • Keep storage and equipment outside required paths.
  • Check gates at material-transfer points independently from personnel egress.
  • Coordinate emergency lighting, signs and alarm notification.

Coordinate sprinklers, alarms and storage below the mezzanine

An added platform can obstruct ceiling sprinkler discharge and create a new concealed or lower-level area. It can also change storage height, commodity arrangements, smoke movement and emergency access.

  • Have the responsible fire-protection professional review the proposed platform.
  • Show existing and proposed sprinkler and alarm interfaces.
  • Coordinate deck type, openings, ceiling, lighting and services.
  • Define storage and activities above and below.
  • Maintain required access to valves, risers, panels and fire equipment.
  • Address penetrations and any required fire-resistance details.
  • Coordinate construction impairments and temporary protection.

Do not assume an open-grate deck removes the need for fire review. The actual geometry, obstruction and hazard still need evaluation.

Build one coordinated permit submittal

A complete package reduces contradictions between architectural, structural, rack, fire and equipment drawings.

  1. Cover sheet with address, scope, code basis and responsible parties.
  2. Existing and proposed plans with room and mezzanine areas.
  3. Sections and elevations showing heights and building interfaces.
  4. Use, occupancy, egress and accessibility analysis.
  5. Structural drawings, calculations and foundation or slab information.
  6. Stairs, guards, gates and material-transfer details.
  7. Fire-protection and alarm information or deferred-submittal plan.
  8. Electrical, lighting, mechanical and equipment coordination.
  9. Product information and special-inspection requirements.
  10. Construction phasing and occupied-building controls when relevant.

List deferred submittals clearly. A deferred item is not permission to ignore its impact on the main design.

Control permit comments and field changes

Create a comment log linking each authority comment to the responsible designer, response and revised sheet. Cloud or otherwise identify changes according to the authority's process, and remove superseded drawings from procurement and the jobsite.

When a review comment changes a stair, column, base, beam, opening or sprinkler arrangement, check every connected package. A small plan change can alter fabrication, floor reactions, egress and fire protection.

Procurement hold point: define which components may be released before approval and who accepts the risk. Ordering the full structure during review can turn a correct authority comment into expensive rework.

Which inspections and handover steps are needed?

Confirm required inspection stages before work begins so concealed anchors, connections, reinforcement, fire-protection work or other items are not covered too early.

  • Permit and approved documents available at the site.
  • Pre-installation verification of layout and support conditions.
  • Structural, anchor or special inspections required by the project.
  • Fire-protection and alarm inspection or testing.
  • Building, egress and accessibility completion inspection.
  • Correction notices documented and closed.
  • Final approval or occupancy documentation obtained as required.
  • As-built information, load signage and operating limits handed over.

For workplace use, OSHA's walking-working surface rule addresses fall protection for employees at elevated surfaces. Construction work has separate requirements, including the strength and integrity of working surfaces in 29 CFR 1926.501. Code approval does not replace employer duties.

Mezzanine permit questions

Does every warehouse mezzanine need a permit?

Do not assume an exemption. Permit and approval requirements depend on jurisdiction, adopted codes, project scope, occupancy, use and existing building. Ask the building and fire authorities about the actual proposal before ordering.

Is an equipment platform the same as a mezzanine?

Not necessarily. Building codes can define and regulate equipment platforms differently. The authority and design team should classify the proposed elevated level from its actual use and configuration.

Can a supplier drawing be submitted for a permit?

A supplier drawing may be one part of the package, but the authority may require site plans, structural calculations, professional seals, egress, accessibility, fire-protection and existing-building information. Confirm the complete submittal.

Can installation start while the permit is under review?

Do not assume it can. Starting early can create enforcement, rework and inspection problems. Confirm permit issuance, approved documents and allowed work with the authority and project team.

Does final inspection transfer responsibility to the inspector?

No. Approval or inspection does not replace the owner's, designer's, supplier's, installer's and employer's ongoing responsibilities for safe use, loads, maintenance and future changes.
