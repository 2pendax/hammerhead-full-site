PALLET-RACK ANCHORING GUIDE

Does Pallet Racking Need to Be Bolted Down?

Why rack columns are normally anchored, what the anchors do, and which information controls a safe installation.

Original guide by zhengdarack · Updated 21 November 2026

Short answer

Yes—industrial pallet rack is normally anchored to its supporting concrete floor in accordance with the rack design, manufacturer instructions and applicable project requirements. In US practice, the Rack Manufacturers Institute states that all rack columns, including short, aisle and rear columns, are to be anchored. The correct anchor is not selected by appearance: rack forces, base plate, concrete, embedment, spacing, edge distance, seismic conditions and installation all matter.

Why is pallet racking bolted to the floor?

Anchors connect each rack column's base plate to the supporting construction. They help keep the column at its designed position and transfer forces that the rack design assigns to the base. Those forces can arise from normal rack behaviour, loading patterns, horizontal effects, equipment interaction, seismic requirements or other project conditions.

An anchor does not make an unsuitable rack safe by itself. The upright, bracing, beams, base plate, anchors and supporting floor form one load path. Changing one item without review can move the weak point elsewhere.

Anchoring functionWhat it does not prove
Maintains the designed base positionThat the rack configuration is approved
Transfers specified base forcesThat the floor is adequate everywhere
Supports stability assumptionsThat damaged rack can remain loaded
Connects rack to supporting constructionThat any available anchor is acceptable

Who specifies the pallet-rack anchors?

The rack engineer or manufacturer normally provides the required anchoring information on approved drawings or installation documents. The building or slab professional may need to confirm the supporting construction. The installer follows the approved information and records exceptions; the owner protects and maintains the installed system.

RMI's current online anchoring FAQ says all rack columns are to be anchored and directs users to the manufacturer for the proper anchor quantity and size. It also explains that extra base-plate holes may be alternatives rather than instructions to fill every hole.

Important: copying an anchor from a nearby rack is not a design method. Two racks that look similar may have different forces, base plates, floor conditions or project requirements.

Which inputs control anchor selection?

  • Rack type, height, depth, bay arrangement and beam elevations.
  • Maximum loads and the approved load application and rack configuration.
  • Base reactions or other design forces.
  • Base-plate geometry, hole locations and column orientation.
  • Concrete thickness, strength and condition where required by the design.
  • Anchor material, diameter, embedment and installation system.
  • Distance to slab edges, joints, trenches, openings and neighbouring anchors.
  • Reinforcement, embedded services and drilling constraints.
  • Seismic, environmental and corrosion conditions.
  • Installation, inspection and verification requirements.

The base-plate hole is only one geometric limit. It does not disclose the anchor's required resistance or whether the surrounding concrete can support it.

What should be checked in the concrete floor?

Obtain available slab drawings, surveys and records before drilling. Mark proposed column locations and identify floor joints, edges, pits, trenches, embedded services and other conflicts. A scanner or other survey method may be needed, but its limitations and the response to detected reinforcement must be defined.

Floor conditionRequired decision
Anchor falls near a joint or edgeReview location, edge distance and approved alternative
Reinforcement blocks the holeDo not cut or shift by guesswork; obtain an approved response
Concrete is cracked or damagedAssess support and anchor suitability before installation
Slab records are unavailableAssign investigation and design responsibility
Rack is on a raised or suspended floorReview the complete supporting structure, not only the topping
Existing holes are presentDo not reuse without an approved assessment and method

If the slab cannot support the required base and anchor arrangement, the answer may involve a revised layout, different base design, local foundation work or another engineered solution.

How should anchors be installed?

Follow the approved anchor manufacturer's instructions and project drawings. Installation requirements can include drill diameter, drill depth, hole cleaning, setting method, embedment, tightening procedure, cure time and inspection. These details vary by anchor system.

  1. Confirm the final rack line and column locations.
  2. Verify floor conflicts and the approved drilling method.
  3. Use the specified drill, bit and depth control.
  4. Clean and prepare the hole exactly as required.
  5. Install the specified anchor without unapproved substitutions.
  6. Apply the approved setting or tightening procedure.
  7. Record exceptions, failed holes and authorised changes.
  8. Complete the required inspection before handover.

A torque value is not universal. It belongs to a particular anchor and installation procedure. Excessive or insufficient tightening can both be problematic.

Can existing pallet rack be anchored later?

Potentially, but first identify the rack and approved configuration. Check that the frames are correctly positioned, plumb within the applicable tolerance, undamaged and compatible with the intended loads. Review the base plate, existing holes and supporting floor before drilling.

Do not use new anchors to conceal a larger problem such as damaged columns, missing bracing, unapproved beam elevations, overloaded rack or a layout that conflicts with exits and traffic. Corrective work should restore an approved system rather than only add visible bolts.

OSHA enforcement records include cases where unanchored pallet rack was identified as a struck-by hazard. One example is OSHA's 2018 citation record. A citation is specific to its facts; use current requirements and competent project review rather than treating one case as a universal specification.

What if an anchor is missing, loose or damaged?

Treat the observation as a condition requiring control and evaluation. The suitable immediate response depends on the rack, load and damage, but it may include isolating the area and unloading affected positions.

  • Do not simply tighten a spinning, pulled or visibly damaged anchor.
  • Do not move the column to a convenient new hole without approval.
  • Check the base plate, column, concrete and nearby anchors.
  • Look for forklift impact, rack movement, distortion and floor cracking.
  • Record the location and protect it from further loading or contact.
  • Use an approved repair or replacement instruction.

If an anchor repeatedly loosens, investigate the cause rather than repeating the same action.

Which anchoring records should be handed over?

  • Approved rack layout and configuration drawings.
  • Anchor product and installation information.
  • Approved responses to reinforcement or floor conflicts.
  • Inspection and test records required by the project.
  • Final rack location and documented field changes.
  • Load plaques and operating limitations.
  • Maintenance, inspection and damage-response instructions.

The owner should keep these records with the rack system so future relocations, repairs and configuration changes are not based on memory.

Pallet-rack anchoring questions

Does every pallet-rack column need an anchor?

For US-style industrial steel storage rack, RMI guidance states that all rack columns, including aisle and interior or rear columns, are to be anchored according to the manufacturer or approved drawings. Project requirements and other jurisdictions must be confirmed for the actual installation.

Can I choose an anchor from the base-plate hole size?

No. Hole size alone does not establish the required anchor type, diameter, embedment, edge distance, spacing or installation method. Use the rack design and approved installation information.

Do all holes in a base plate need anchors?

Not necessarily. Extra holes may be alternatives for reinforcement interference. Install the quantity and locations shown on the approved rack drawings; do not fill or omit holes by guesswork.

Can pallet rack be anchored to asphalt?

Do not treat asphalt as equivalent to a verified structural concrete support. The rack and supporting construction require a project-specific solution from the responsible parties.

What should I do if an anchor is missing or loose?

Restrict the affected area as appropriate, prevent further loading or impact, and have the rack, anchor and supporting floor evaluated. Replace or repair only under approved instructions.
