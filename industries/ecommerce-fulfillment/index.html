PIECE, CASE AND PARCEL OPERATIONS

Ecommerce Fulfillment Racking

Connect reserve pallets, forward picking, replenishment, packing, returns and seasonal demand in one operating design.

Original industry guide by zhengdarack · Updated 22 November 2026

Core rule: ecommerce storage should reduce total touches and congestion, not only walking distance. Fast picking fails when replenishment, packing, returns or inventory accuracy cannot keep up.

Profile orders, not only inventory

Build demand distributions by day and hour.

DataDesign use
Orders and lines per hourPicker, workstation and conveyor demand
Units per line and orderPick method and container size
SKU order frequencyForward-pick selection and location
Item cube and weightShelf, tote, carton and ergonomic design
AffinityZone, path and co-location decisions
Inventory peakReserve and forward capacity
Returns rate and dispositionReverse-flow area and labour
Cut-offs and carrier wavesPacking, sortation and outbound staging

Use promotion and holiday stress scenarios. Annual averages can miss the weeks that define required capacity.

Separate reserve, pick and exception storage

Pallet reserve

Selective or denser systems hold inbound and replenishment stock, matched to pallet and SKU profiles.

Forward pick

Shelving, carton flow, totes or pallet pick faces present active inventory at suitable access heights.

Bulky and irregular

Dedicated floor, cantilever or special support avoids forcing oversize items into standard shelves.

Returns and quarantine

Controlled zones separate unidentified, damaged, inspection-pending and resale-ready goods.

Automation can support repetitive high-volume paths, but exceptions, maintenance, recovery, controls and inventory accuracy remain part of the design.

Size forward pick faces

For each SKU, estimate demand during replenishment response time plus appropriate uncertainty. Then check whether the face can hold that quantity without unsafe stacking or excessive reach.

  • Provide minimum and maximum quantities.
  • Define trigger and task priority.
  • Use reserve locations compatible with replenishment units.
  • Prevent simultaneous picking and replenishment exposure.
  • Measure stock-outs, emergency fills and face overflow.
  • Review face size when velocity or package changes.

A larger pick face reduces replenishment frequency but consumes prime space. Optimize total labour and congestion.

Slot SKUs using multiple factors

FactorQuestion
VelocityHow often is the SKU visited?
QuantityHow many units are picked each visit?
Cube and weightWhich shelf and access height are safe and efficient?
AffinityWhich items commonly share orders?
ReplenishmentHow much reserve movement will the face create?
CompatibilityAre there security, contamination or damage constraints?
SeasonalityHow long will the current velocity remain true?

Do not cluster every fast SKU in one short aisle. Distribute work or provide enough faces to avoid a congestion hotspot.

Choose a picking method from the order profile

  • Discrete: one order at a time; simple but can create long travel.
  • Batch: pick multiple orders together; requires accurate sortation.
  • Zone: workers or automation serve defined areas; needs balanced workload and handoff.
  • Wave: release groups by carrier or cut-off; can create sharp peaks.
  • Goods-to-person: equipment presents inventory; needs controls, buffers and recovery design.

The rack and shelving must support the selected method, replenishment and peak work content.

Protect packing and outbound flow

Packing often becomes the bottleneck after picking is improved.

  • Balance workstations with released pick volume.
  • Provide packaging and replenishment without blocking operators.
  • Separate completed, exception and carrier-ready parcels.
  • Control labels, weight checks and documentation.
  • Size sortation and staging by carrier wave and dwell.
  • Keep fire routes and doors clear during peak.

Measure first-pass completion and rework. Hidden repacking makes upstream productivity appear stronger than the complete process.

Design returns as a real process

  1. Receive and identify the return.
  2. Confirm item, order and reason.
  3. Inspect condition and safety.
  4. Assign disposition: restock, rework, quarantine, vendor return or disposal.
  5. Update inventory and financial status.
  6. Move to a controlled destination.

Returns should not share an uncontrolled corner with new receipts. Provide enough locations and worktables for peak events.

Inventory accuracy and location control

  • Scan or confirm source, item, quantity and destination.
  • Use clear shelf, tote and bin identifiers.
  • Control partial containers and substitutions.
  • Cycle-count high-risk and high-velocity locations.
  • Investigate repeated shorts by process step.
  • Reconcile automated and manual recovery moves.

Fast travel is worthless if the picker reaches an empty or incorrect location.

Rack, shelving and safety controls

  • Verify shelf and rack loads and display accurate limits.
  • Match decking and support to cartons, totes and pallets.
  • Keep products within shelf edges and aisles.
  • Separate pedestrians and lift equipment where practical.
  • Maintain exits, sprinklers, flues and top-of-storage limits.
  • Report impacts and isolate damaged storage equipment.
  • Control beam and shelf reconfiguration.

Peak readiness test

  1. Load the model with peak inventory and order mix.
  2. Check receiving and put-away surge.
  3. Test replenishment against pick demand.
  4. Balance zones, packing and carrier cut-offs.
  5. Include returns and exception volume.
  6. Test one equipment or system outage.
  7. Confirm temporary labour, training and supervision.
  8. Define trigger points for overflow and recovery.

Ecommerce racking questions

Which storage is best?

Most operations need a mix of pallet reserve, forward shelving or flow, returns capacity and possibly automation.

How should SKUs be slotted?

Use velocity, units, cube, weight, affinity, replenishment, ergonomics, seasonality and congestion.

How much forward inventory is needed?

Size for demand during replenishment response time plus appropriate uncertainty.

Why are returns important?

They need identification, inspection, disposition, rework and status control or they will block other processes.
