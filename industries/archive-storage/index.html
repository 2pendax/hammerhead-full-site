RECORDS AND DOCUMENT STORAGE

Archive Storage Racking

Design shelf capacity, retrieval, preservation, security and growth around the real record box and retention process.

Original industry guide by zhengdarack · Updated 22 November 2026

Start with the records program: racking can organize boxes, but it cannot define retention, legal hold, confidentiality, preservation or authorized destruction. Coordinate the physical store with records, legal, security, fire and facilities requirements.

Measure the archive unit load

Archive boxes often look uniform but differ in external size, board strength, fill weight and lid projection. Sample the real population.

Use the heaviest credible filled box. A shelf rated from an empty-box sample can be seriously underestimated.

Select storage by access and volume

Static steel shelving

Simple direct access for active records, with shelf sizes matched to boxes and manual handling limits.

Longspan shelving

Wider bays and heavier shelf loads for dense carton storage, subject to support and ergonomic review.

Mobile shelving

Can reduce fixed aisles, but requires floor, guidance, controls, egress, fire and operating review.

Palletized reserve

Useful for sealed bulk transfers or low-access cartons when pallets, wrapping, rack and retrieval processes are compatible.

High-density storage can reduce floor area but may slow retrieval and complicate simultaneous access. Model daily and peak requests.

Calculate usable box capacity

  1. Choose an approved box orientation.
  2. Fit whole boxes across shelf width.
  3. Confirm shelf depth supports the box without unsafe projection.
  4. Allow hand clearance and label visibility.
  5. Fit shelf levels within safe access and structural limits.
  6. Subtract uprights, braces, doors, columns and unavailable locations.
  7. Add aisles, work areas, quarantine and growth reserve.

Report box positions by compatible box family. A total cubic volume can hide unusable gaps or shelves that do not fit a common carton.

Manage shelf loads and floor demand

Paper is dense. Confirm maximum box weight × boxes per shelf, then verify shelf, beams, frames, base, anchors and floor for the actual configuration.

  • Post accurate shelf and bay limits.
  • Place heavier boxes at accessible lower elevations where the design allows.
  • Do not add shelf levels without configuration review.
  • Keep loads evenly supported and within shelf edges.
  • Inspect deflection, damage, corrosion and loose connections.
  • Review concentrated floor demand for dense or mobile storage.

Do not assume an existing office floor or slab can accept a compact archive installation without appropriate review.

Aisles should not become temporary workstations. Provide enough processing space for pulls, returns and reboxing.

Preservation and housekeeping

  • Keep records off the floor and away from uncontrolled water sources.
  • Monitor conditions appropriate to the collection.
  • Control dust, pests and food in storage areas.
  • Provide cleanable access around shelving.
  • Replace crushed, wet or weakened boxes.
  • Plan response for leaks, fire-system discharge and other incidents.
  • Avoid placing records against hot equipment or unreviewed exterior conditions.

Preservation requirements differ by record type. Consult the responsible archivist or records professional for valuable or sensitive collections.

Fire protection and emergency access

Paper records create a substantial combustible load. Coordinate commodity, carton storage, shelf type, aisle, storage height, flues, ceiling and in-rack sprinklers with the fire-protection designer and local authority.

  • Keep exits and fire equipment accessible.
  • Maintain approved top-of-storage limits.
  • Do not add solid shelves or covers without review.
  • Keep required flue spaces clear.
  • Control temporary boxes in aisles.
  • Maintain an emergency contact and salvage priority plan.

Security and chain of custody

Use role-based access, controlled keys or credentials, visitor rules and transaction records. Define who may request, retrieve, transport, return and destroy each record class.

  • Use unique box and location identifiers.
  • Record every movement and temporary custody.
  • Separate legal hold, restricted and destruction-ready material.
  • Verify destruction authorization and evidence.
  • Protect location data where it is sensitive.
  • Audit missing, overdue and misfiled records.

Physical cages or rooms should support, not replace, records-system controls.

Archive storage questions

Which shelving is best?

Match box size, shelf load, retrieval, aisle, environment, fire design and growth. Different zones may need different systems.

How is capacity calculated?

Use external box dimensions, orientation, shelf clearances, structure, aisles, access and growth.

Can records be stored on the floor?

Avoid uncontrolled floor storage because of moisture, impact, access, housekeeping and fire risks.

How should confidential records be stored?

Use access control, permissions, chain-of-custody transactions, secure retrieval and authorized destruction.
