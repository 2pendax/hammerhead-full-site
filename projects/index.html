How to use a scenario

Borrow the questions, not the claimed outcome

Use the checklists and decision sequence to identify missing data. Do not treat an example as proof that the same rack type, layout, schedule, or approval will suit another facility.

Replace assumptions with site data

Insert actual unit loads, inventory, movement, lift equipment, building dimensions, slab information, fire protection, approvals, operational limits, and future changes.

Assign responsible reviewers

Identify who owns engineering, operations, safety, fire protection, building approvals, procurement, security, records, installation, inspection, and final acceptance.

Compare alternatives and tradeoffs

Evaluate access, travel, replenishment, changeability, congestion, protection, disruption, maintenance, lifecycle cost, and usable—not merely theoretical—capacity.

Document the approved baseline

Retain drawings, calculations, loads, permits, product data, changes, inspections, capacity signs, training, warranties, and as-built information.

What these pages do not establish

A scenario does not establish a customer relationship, contract, government eligibility, manufacturer authorization, local office, stocked inventory, nationwide service, code approval, engineering credential, certification, project result, or performance percentage. Those claims require current, specific evidence beyond educational content.

Likewise, a URL that preserves an old project title is not evidence that the event happened. The old addresses remain so existing visitors and search results reach a transparent, corrected explanation rather than an unexplained error.

Minimum real-project data

Loads and inventory

Maximum weights and dimensions, support geometry, pallet or container condition, rotation, quantity, velocity, commodity, hazards, security and future change.

Facility and operation

Measured building, slab, columns, doors, docks, utilities, fire protection, egress, equipment, people, staging, peak activity and change constraints.

Approval and evidence

Responsible professionals, authorities, submittals, permits, inspection points, installation records, acceptance, training and ongoing configuration control.

Success criteria

Define measurable baseline and target outcomes without inventing percentages. Include safety, flow, accuracy, service, disruption and maintainability.

Turn a scenario into your own brief

Choose the nearest example, replace its assumptions with verified data, and record every decision that still needs a responsible reviewer.
