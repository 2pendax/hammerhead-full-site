Density with controlled aisle access

Mobile Pallet Racking Planning Guide

Mobile pallet racking places rack rows on powered bases that move to open a selected aisle. Reducing fixed aisles can increase storage within a footprint, but the system adds rails or floor interfaces, drives, controls, sensors, emergency modes, maintenance, and access time.

Short answer: model real inventory and simultaneous aisle demand, verify loads and moving-base design, coordinate the floor and rails, engineer safety functions, review fire and emergency access, and fund lifecycle support. This page does not promise 80–100% more pallets or nationwide design, installation, and permitting.

Test the operational fit

Inventory access

Measure retrievals and puts by aisle, hour and peak. Identify how often multiple users need different aisles at the same time and the cost of waiting.

Load profile

Document maximum pallet weights and dimensions, pallet condition, overhang, stability, commodity, rotation, storage height and future variation.

Equipment

Verify lift-truck model, aisle, turning, wheel loads, clearances, guidance, charging, travel across rails, floor transitions and operator visibility.

Environment

Define ambient, cold, freezer, moisture, dust, cleaning, corrosion, floor heave, condensation, power and sensor conditions.

Access restrictions

Consider security, maintenance, emergency retrieval, fire response, inventory counts, high-priority loads, visitors and manual override authority.

Growth and change

Test changing SKUs, loads, rack levels, fleet, operating hours, expansion, relocation, software, vendor support and system end of life.

System elements to coordinate

Density must be calculated from the actual layout. Compare usable pallet positions after fixed clearances, end aisles, transfer aisles, fire and egress requirements, maintenance access, incompatible loads and operating reserves. A marketing percentage from another building is not a result.

Planning sequence

Model static and mobile alternatives

Use the same loads, building, fire constraints, equipment and forecast. Compare usable capacity, aisle wait, travel, throughput, expansion, resilience and lifecycle cost.

Survey floor and building interfaces

Verify slab, foundation, joints, embedded services, levelness, doors, columns, utilities, sprinklers, exits, charging, drainage and construction phasing.

Define functional and safety requirements

Specify movement, speed, stops, presence detection, access, warnings, permissions, emergency modes, power loss, manual recovery, faults and records.

Coordinate design and approvals

Integrate rack, bases, rails, controls, electrical, building, fire protection, lift equipment, permits, installation, inspections and responsible professionals.

Test and hand over

Verify loads, movement, all safety devices, faults, emergency modes, power recovery, aisle access, interfaces, performance, training, spares and maintenance.

Safety and emergency operation

A moving aisle must not close on people, equipment, pallets, debris or unauthorized work. The safety concept should identify detection zones, entry conditions, reset location, line of sight, warnings, safe speed, stop performance, maintenance lockout, trapped-person response, manual operation and testing frequency.

Coordinate firefighter and emergency access with the responsible fire-protection team and authorities. Define rack row positions for alarms or power loss, access to valves and equipment, smoke and sprinkler strategy, evacuation, emergency power if applicable, and how aisles are opened when controls fail.

Design review questions

Simultaneous demand

How often will users wait for an aisle, and what happens to picking, replenishment, counts and urgent retrieval during peaks?

Floor and rails

Are foundation, tolerances, wheel loads, joints, drainage, frost, debris control, installation and future repair fully assigned?

Safety validation

Who designs, verifies, tests and maintains every sensor, stop, warning, reset, access control and emergency function?

Resilience

Can priority inventory be reached after drive, sensor, PLC, network or power failure, and who is authorized to recover the system?

Maintenance

Are safe access, lockout, diagnostics, spare parts, inspections, remote support, software backups and obsolescence planned?

Change control

Who approves new loads, rack levels, base loads, row length, floor work, lift trucks, controls, software and fire-system changes?

Information for a mobile rack brief

Provide inventory and transaction data, simultaneous aisle demand, unit loads, rack needs, lift equipment, building and slab information, environmental conditions, utilities, fire documents, emergency needs, growth, schedule, maintenance capability and responsible reviewers.

Require proposals to state usable capacity assumptions, rack and base load basis, floor and rail scope, safety concept, controls, interfaces, fire assumptions, power, fault recovery, testing, permits, installation, training, maintenance, service response, spares, software, warranty and exclusions.

Prepare a mobile-rack evaluation brief

Organize inventory, aisle demand, loads, floor, controls, fire, emergency and lifecycle requirements before comparing concepts.
