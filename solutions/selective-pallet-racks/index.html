Selective Pallet Racking

Selective pallet racking gives a handling aisle direct access to the pallet positions along each rack face. Plan the load, equipment and working areas together.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 16 November 2026

The short answer

Selective pallet racking stores palletized loads on beam levels with individual positions facing an aisle. It is useful when many SKUs require direct pallet access, but the aisles consume floor area. Specify pallet dimensions and weights, beam levels, forklift requirements, building constraints and peak inventory before comparing layouts.

When does selective pallet racking fit the stock profile?

Selective rack is a strong candidate when a warehouse holds many products and workers must retrieve a chosen pallet without moving another pallet first. Direct access supports changing stock allocation, but every rack face needs an appropriate handling aisle.

How is a selective rack layout developed?

Start with working areas and equipment routes, then fit rack rows into the remaining storage area. Mark receiving, dispatch, staging, doors, columns and required clear routes before counting pallet positions.

Obtain the equipment requirements for the actual forklift and load. Do not copy an aisle dimension from another building. Test simultaneous receiving, replenishment and dispatch activities, including where pallets wait.

Which load limits should a selective rack proposal show?

Separate the loaded pallet, beam-level load and complete bay load. Show the number and position of pallets at every supported level. A component rating without the proposed span, connection and arrangement is incomplete.

  • Loaded pallet: pallet, goods and packaging handled as one unit.
  • Beam level: number, weight and placement of pallets on one level.
  • Bay: combined supported loading across the approved levels.
  • Accessories: decking, supports and protection identified by location.

The Rack Manufacturers Institute FAQ describes storage rack as a system of frames, beams, connections, rows, bays and levels. Use applicable project documentation to establish the proposed rack's limits.

How should usable pallet positions be counted?

Count positions from the final candidate drawing and map the peak inventory into them. A position blocked by a column, reserved for quarantine or incompatible with a tall pallet is not freely available to every stock group.

Illustrative position count, not a design

Twelve bays with three supported levels and two positions per level give 12 × 3 × 2 = 72 beam-supported positions. This arithmetic establishes neither capacity nor whether the stock allocation will use all 72 positions.

Show floor-level positions separately if they are included. Keep the position schedule beside the quotation so the buyer can compare usable storage, components and service scope together.

What belongs in a selective pallet racking quotation?

  1. Layout: rack rows, bay quantities, aisles and working areas.
  2. Configuration: upright heights and depths, beam spans, levels and position schedule.
  3. Loading: pallet, level and bay assumptions for the proposed arrangement.
  4. Components: frames, beams, locking devices, supports, decking and protection.
  5. Project scope: destination, freight, unloading, installation and handover documentation.

Ask suppliers to state unresolved site information and exclusions. A steel-only offer cannot be compared directly with an installed project until the missing work and responsibilities are visible.

For an extension, provide manufacturer identification and current configuration details. Similar colour or connector shape does not establish compatibility.

Selective pallet racking: common questions

Does selective rack provide direct access to every stored pallet?

In the intended layout, individual positions face an aisle, but actual access still depends on the equipment, load, aisle and operating conditions. Confirm the completed proposal.

Is selective rack the highest-density option?

Not generally. Its access aisles use floor area. Compare usable capacity and handling needs with lane-based systems for the actual stock profile.

Can beam levels be moved later?

Have the revised configuration reviewed first. Moving levels changes spacing and load distribution, so update drawings and load information when approved.

Can different pallet sizes share one bay?

Provide every pallet size, underside and load placement for review. Supports, clearances and the approved arrangement must address the intended variation.

What information improves the first quotation?

Send pallet dimensions and weights, SKU quantities, target positions, a floor plan, forklift details, destination and required services.
