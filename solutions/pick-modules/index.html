Warehouse Pick Module Planning Guide

A pick module combines storage, work areas, platforms, stairs, conveyors, chutes, carton flow, replenishment, controls, people, fire protection, and egress. Its performance depends on balanced work across every level and interface.

Short answer: model orders and replenishment by zone, size work and buffers, coordinate structure and conveyors, design ergonomics and safe access, prove egress and fire strategy, test controls and exceptions, and ramp with measured data. This page does not guarantee maximum picks per hour or nationwide design and installation.

Build the order and work profile

Orders

Lines per order, units per line, channel, cutoff, wave or waveless logic, priorities, split shipments, consolidation, peaks and growth.

SKUs

Dimensions, weight, velocity, affinity, seasonality, lot or expiry, security, damage, storage method, forward quantity and change frequency.

Picking tasks

Travel, scan, reach, open carton, extract, confirm, pack, exception, empty handling, replenishment interaction and representative task time.

Replenishment

Reserve source, trigger, quantity, route, equipment, timing, lane or shelf capacity, stockout response and separation from pickers.

Containers

Order totes, cartons, bins, pallets, label position, conveyor compatibility, maximum weight, stability, damage and empty-container return.

Exceptions

Short pick, wrong item, unreadable label, damaged product, full takeaway, conveyor fault, fire alarm, power loss and urgent order.

Picks per hour is a system result. Define the order mix, task boundary, staffing, breaks, replenishment, congestion, equipment availability, conveyor capacity, exceptions and measurement period. A headline rate from another operation is not a project promise.

Planning sequence

Model demand by zone and time

Use transaction data to group SKUs and estimate tasks, replenishments, container flow, labor and peaks on every level. Test seasonal and assortment change.

Balance work and takeaway

Compare pick production with conveyor, chute, merge, accumulation, consolidation and packing capacity. Define buffers and stop logic without blocking egress.

Design structure, access and ergonomics

Coordinate loads, stairs, guards, gates, reaches, work heights, lighting, noise, climate, manual handling, equipment, maintenance and accessible routes as required.

Coordinate fire, building and controls

Review occupancy, egress, sprinklers, storage commodity, smoke, alarms, emergency modes, utilities, permits, software, electrical and other trades.

Test and ramp

Verify installation, loads, safety functions, controls, normal flow, peaks, faults, evacuations, recovery, inventory, training, maintenance and staged performance.

Replenishment and picking separation

Where possible, give replenishment a distinct face or time window. Define who can enter, what equipment is permitted, how a pick face is locked or signaled, and what happens when replenishment arrives during active picking. Do not let reserve pallets, empty cartons or carts narrow stairs, platforms, aisles or exits.

Forward quantity should cover demand until the next reliable replenishment plus a defined buffer. Revisit slotting as velocity changes. A permanent location for every SKU may waste valuable frontage, while constant re-slotting can create errors and uncontrolled physical changes.

Design review questions

Flow balance

Can conveyors, merges, chutes, packing and consolidation absorb production from every level under the tested peak?

Egress

Can all occupants reach approved exits during normal, congested, power-loss and equipment-fault conditions without passing through hazards?

Ergonomics

Are reach, lift, twist, repetition, container handling, work height, visibility, noise, temperature and fatigue reviewed for actual tasks?

Fire protection

Have platforms, concealed spaces, conveyors, storage, commodity, sprinklers, alarms, smoke, fire stopping and responder access been coordinated?

Recovery

What happens after conveyor, scanner, network, control, power or downstream failure, and how are containers and inventory reconciled?

Change control

Who approves new SKUs, loads, pick faces, lane settings, platform loads, conveyors, software, staffing and building changes?

Information for a pick module brief

Provide order and SKU data, transaction peaks, item and container dimensions, forward and reserve rules, replenishment tasks, current performance, building survey, structural and slab information, fire documents, equipment, systems, utilities, staffing, ergonomics, maintenance, schedule, growth and responsible reviewers.

Require proposals to state modeling assumptions, zone rates, buffers, loads, structure, stairs, egress, fire strategy, conveyors, controls, interfaces, exceptions, testing, permits, installation, training, ramp-up, maintenance, spares, software, performance boundaries and exclusions.
