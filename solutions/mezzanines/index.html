Warehouse Mezzanine

A warehouse mezzanine adds an elevated area within a building. Plan the upper workspace, lower clearance and movement between levels together.

Published by zhengdarack · Updated 16 November 2026

Plan Your Rack Specification →
The short answer

A warehouse mezzanine is an elevated floor or platform that creates additional space within a warehouse. Its useful area depends on the footprint, stairs, transfer points, columns, circulation and intended activity. Define stored goods, people, equipment, load placement, available height and the supporting building information before requesting a design. Extra floor area should be measured from the proposed layout rather than assumed to double the warehouse.

What should a warehouse mezzanine be used for?

Start with the activity rather than the platform size. Carton storage, picking, packing, production support and equipment areas can require different loads, surfaces and movement arrangements.

ActivityInformation to provide
StorageGoods, weights, locations and replenishment
PickingSKU locations, people and cart movement
PackingWorkstations, materials and equipment
Production supportMachines, interfaces and maintenance access
Future changesExpected activities and load variations

Mecalux's mezzanine overview describes using building height for additional working or storage area. The actual benefit depends on the project layout and operation.

How much useful area does a mezzanine add?

Separate gross platform area from the area available for the intended activity. Stairs, openings, transfer positions and circulation occupy space, while supports may affect use below.

For example, a 300 m² platform with 60 m² allocated to circulation and transfer leaves 240 m² for other planned activities. This illustrative arithmetic does not establish a design or a guaranteed warehouse capacity gain.

Which loads must be included in the brief?

Provide both the total activity and how loads contact the floor. An average weight per area does not describe every concentrated load from a shelf foot, machine or wheel.

  • Maximum stored items and their placement.
  • Shelving, benches and equipment weights.
  • Point contacts, wheel loads and movement routes.
  • People and operational activity.
  • Transfer and temporary staging loads.
  • Expected future changes.

The project designer needs the intended arrangement and supporting building data to establish the appropriate design. Do not select a platform solely from a generic advertised load rating.

What building information should be collected?

Supply a dimensioned plan and section that show more than the roof height. Services, beams, doors and existing equipment can restrict the usable space above and below the new floor.

  • Building dimensions and existing columns.
  • Roof and beam levels.
  • Services, lighting and other overhead equipment.
  • Floor and slab records available for review.
  • Doors, routes and operational access.
  • Installation location and relevant project requirements.

Agree who reviews the supporting structure and building interfaces. Identify missing information early so the quotation can show assumptions and unresolved scope clearly.

How will people and goods reach the mezzanine?

Design the movement cycle with the storage or work area. Staff access and material transfer are separate functions that both need appropriate provision.

  1. Map where goods arrive at ground level.
  2. Define the transfer equipment or loading method.
  3. Reserve landing and staging space.
  4. Plan the route to the final storage or workstation.
  5. Include returns, waste and empty containers.
  6. Check the busiest replenishment and dispatch period.

Stairs, gates, lifting equipment and conveyors should be coordinated with the platform and site requirements. Specify responsibilities for their design, supply, installation and acceptance.

How can the area below remain usable?

Place supports and access routes around the activity below. The platform footprint may be attractive while columns or stairs interfere with forklift turning, workstations or maintenance.

  • Mark truck and pedestrian routes.
  • Check equipment and load heights.
  • Reserve door operation and service access.
  • Review lighting and other building services.
  • Locate exposed supports and protection needs.
  • Check how installation will affect production.

Evaluate the upper and lower layouts together. A gain above should be compared with any space or handling constraints introduced below.

What should project delivery include?

Agree the sequence of site preparation, delivery, erection and commissioning. An operating warehouse may need temporary stock relocation or phased access.

Request the final drawings, agreed load information, component references, instructions and inspection responsibilities at handover. Record authorised changes so the warehouse team has information matching the installed structure.

A later change from carton storage to machines, heavy shelving or wheeled equipment should trigger a design review. Keep the intended-use records available to the person controlling the area.

Compare the complete installed scope and intended activity. A price per square metre can hide access, building work or equipment that another proposal includes.

Warehouse mezzanine: common questions

Does a mezzanine always double usable space?

No. Calculate useful area from the actual footprint, circulation, transfer positions and effect on the level below.

Can the existing slab support it?

The supporting floor and structure require project-specific review using available records and any additional investigation needed.

Can pallets be stored upstairs?

Only when the intended loads, handling equipment and transfer method are included in the approved design.

Can the use change later?

A change in activity or loading should be reviewed against the final design information before implementation.

What information improves a first proposal?

Send building plan and heights, intended activities, load placement, equipment, goods flow, floor records and installation location.
