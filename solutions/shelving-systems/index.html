Fit shelves to items and people

Industrial Shelving System Planning Guide

Industrial shelving stores parts, cartons, tools, bins, supplies, records, and work-in-process at manual or assisted pick locations. A reliable system connects item geometry and weight with shelf construction, reach, access, stability, labels, replenishment, fire protection, and future change.

Short answer: profile the heaviest and most awkward items, verify shelf and bay loads, test container fit and retrieval, place inventory within safe handling zones, stabilize the complete system, and control configuration changes. This page does not promise a 1,500-pound shelf rating or automatic design and installation.

Profile items and containers

Geometry

Width, depth, height, shape, protrusions, handles, lids, open containers, overhang, nesting, stackability and space needed to grasp or remove the item.

Weight

Maximum item and container weight, shelf quantity, concentrated or distributed loading, center of gravity, partial bins and future heavier products.

Access

Pick frequency, replenishment, lot or expiry, security, tool control, scanning, labels, visibility, carts, ladders, lifts and exception handling.

Ergonomics

Reach, lift height, depth, posture, repetition, two-person handling, sharp edges, unstable parts, container removal and suitable handling aids.

Environment

Ambient, cold, freezer, moisture, dust, corrosion, chemicals, food or clean-area needs, cleaning, static, light and product contamination.

Operation

SKU count, velocity, reserve, forward quantity, replenishment trigger, WIP, returns, quarantine, seasonality, growth and re-slotting frequency.

“Per shelf” ratings are configuration-specific. Shelf type, span, support, load distribution, uprights, braces, connections, bay arrangement, anchorage and installation all matter. Concentrated or uneven loads may not fit a published uniformly distributed rating.

Planning sequence

Create the item-to-location profile

Group SKUs by dimensions, weight, velocity, affinity, handling, security, environment and replenishment. Identify items unsuitable for manual shelving.

Prototype representative shelves

Test the heaviest, largest, smallest and most awkward containers. Verify support, overhang, grasp, labels, scan, retrieval, replenishment and falling-object control.

Design aisles and work

Coordinate carts, people, ladders or lifts, pick faces, staging, lighting, replenishment, pedestrian flow, doors, columns, exits and emergency equipment.

Coordinate structure, building and fire

Review level and bay loads, stability, anchors or ties, floor, seismic criteria, storage height, commodity, sprinklers, flue spaces, egress and permits.

Install and control use

Inspect the approved configuration, post load information where required, assign locations, train users, inspect damage and require approval for changes.

Manual handling and access

Put frequently handled items where workers can retrieve them with controlled posture and force. Heavy, large, unstable or sharp items may need a lower position, mechanical aid, two-person method, special container or a different storage system. A shelf that carries the weight is not automatically safe to pick from.

Avoid placing small items on open wire or wide gaps without suitable support. Prevent boxes or bins from projecting into aisles or falling from rear faces. If ladders or order-picking equipment are used, coordinate floor condition, aisle width, gates, fall protection, storage presentation and emergency response with competent safety review.

Design review questions

Capacity

Do exact shelf and bay ratings match maximum concentrated and distributed loads, spans, supports and the installed configuration?

Stability

Are height-to-depth, bracing, back-to-back ties, wall or floor attachment, loading sequence and seismic or site criteria addressed?

Ergonomics

Can people see, grasp, lift, carry and replace items without excessive reach, force, twisting or climbing?

Fire protection

Have shelving construction, solid or open shelves, commodity, storage height, aisle, sprinklers, flue spaces and permits been reviewed?

Inventory control

Do locations, labels, dividers, replenishment, lot or expiry, security, returns and exceptions keep items identifiable?

Change control

Who approves new items, heavier loads, level moves, shelves, decks, dividers, anchors, ties, aisle changes and multi-level additions?

Information for a shelving brief

Provide item and container dimensions, maximum weights, quantity, SKU and velocity data, access and replenishment, environment, security, current shelving, equipment, building survey, floor and fire information, ergonomics concerns, growth, schedule and responsible reviewers.

Require proposals to state exact shelving product and configuration, level and bay capacities, load assumptions, shelf material and support, stability, anchors or ties, accessories, labels, fire assumptions, installation, inspection, permits, maintenance, training and exclusions.

Prepare an industrial shelving brief

Organize items, containers, weights, access, ergonomics, environment and building information before comparing systems.
