Automation begins with data

Automated Storage and Retrieval System Planning

An automated storage and retrieval system joins rack, machines, conveyors, controls, software, inventory data, safety functions, building services, fire protection, people, and maintenance. Its value depends on the complete operating system, not the machine alone.

Short answer: profile loads and orders at transaction level, model normal and peak throughput, define exceptions and recovery, coordinate the building and protection systems, test integrations, and fund lifecycle support. This page does not promise that zhengdarack designs, integrates, installs, or services AS/RS nationwide.

Data required before concept selection

Unit loads

Dimensions, maximum weight, pallet or tote type, support, stability, overhang, deformation, barcode, quality checks, damage frequency and future variation.

Inventory

SKU count, quantity, velocity, affinity, lot or expiry, rotation, reserve, seasonality, growth, quarantine, returns, security and environmental needs.

Transactions

Receipts, puts, retrievals, replenishments, order lines, peaks, waves, cutoffs, dwell, sequences, simultaneous tasks and service levels.

Exceptions

Unknown load, damaged pallet, failed scan, overweight unit, blocked location, short pick, inventory mismatch, equipment fault and urgent manual request.

Building

Footprint, clear height, columns, slab, tolerances, roof, doors, docks, utilities, power, network, environment, sprinklers, egress and maintenance access.

Organization

Operating hours, staffing, controls ownership, IT support, maintenance capability, spare parts, cybersecurity, safety, training and change governance.

Do not compare headline transactions per hour. Define the transaction, load mix, travel, dwell, peak duration, availability, replenishment, station capacity, software rules, exceptions and measurement boundary. A machine rate is not an end-to-end warehouse rate.

Evaluation sequence

Build a validated baseline

Clean transaction and inventory data, observe current work, separate normal from peak periods, document exceptions, and establish measurable service, labor, space and error baselines.

Define functional requirements

State loads, capacity, throughput, sequencing, availability, environment, interfaces, safety, recovery, maintenance, reporting, cybersecurity, growth and acceptance tests.

Model complete concepts

Include induction, storage, retrieval, stations, conveyors, staging, replenishment, packing, rejects, manual backup, fire response and downstream constraints.

Coordinate design and approvals

Review rack and machine structure, slab, building, power, network, controls, egress, fire protection, permits, access, maintenance and construction phasing.

Test and ramp responsibly

Use factory and site tests, load cases, interfaces, faults, recovery, safety, performance, inventory reconciliation, training, spares and a controlled ramp to production.

Resilience and lifecycle support

Define what happens when a crane, shuttle, robot, lift, conveyor, scanner, PLC, server, database, network, power supply or station is unavailable. A recovery procedure should identify safe access, inventory status, work priorities, manual alternatives, specialist support, communication and restart validation.

Compare maintenance staffing, remote access, cybersecurity controls, software licenses, source code or escrow where relevant, spare parts, obsolescence, response terms, preventive maintenance, inspection access, training, backups, upgrades and expansion. Vendor dependence and end-of-life risk belong in the business case.

Design review questions

Performance

Are throughput and capacity tied to documented load mix, peaks, availability, stations, downstream constraints and acceptance tests?

Safety and fire

Are human access, machine safety, egress, storage commodity, sprinklers, smoke control, firefighter access and emergency modes coordinated?

Controls and data

Who owns WMS/WCS/PLC interfaces, master data, messages, exceptions, security, backups, audit trails, testing and change control?

Maintenance

Can technicians access components safely, diagnose faults, obtain parts, recover loads and return the system without corrupting inventory?

Commercial

Which party owns integration, performance, building works, utilities, permits, testing, ramp-up, service, upgrades and long-term support?

Exit and growth

Can capacity, stations or fleet expand? What happens if the product mix changes or the vendor stops supporting a critical component?

Business-case discipline

Include survey, simulation, equipment, rack, software, integration, building, slab, fire protection, utilities, network, permits, installation, testing, training, inventory migration, ramp-up, support, spares, licenses, energy, maintenance, upgrades and contingency. Compare the automated concept with improved conventional or semi-automated alternatives using the same forecast.

Do not count benefits twice. Labor, space, accuracy, service, damage and inventory improvements require a measured baseline, a causal link, an implementation plan, and sensitivity tests. State what happens under lower volume, different SKU mix, delayed ramp, higher maintenance and shorter asset life.
