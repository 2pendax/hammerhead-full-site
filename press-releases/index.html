Our publication standard

A company update should help readers make an accurate decision. That requires more than polished language. Before publication, each material assertion should be supported by a source that matches the exact entity, event, date, location, product, and scope being described.

  • Company events: acquisitions, ownership changes, openings, closures, partnerships, and investments need authorized documentary support.
  • Trade shows: exhibitor status, booth numbers, sessions, sponsorships, and demonstrations should match current organizer records.
  • Products: availability, capacity, performance, compatibility, certification, and warranty claims need product-specific evidence and appropriate limitations.
  • Projects: named customers, locations, contract values, results, and photographs require authorization and records connecting the work to the publisher.
  • Geographic service: an address, supplier, or past shipment does not automatically prove a permanent local operation or nationwide service capability.
  • Dates: publication, event, signing, closing, opening, and last-review dates must be distinguished rather than blended together.

How corrections are handled

1. Preserve the useful address

When an old URL may be indexed or bookmarked, a clear correction at that address can be more informative than an unexplained error. The address is not proof that its former headline was true.

2. State the present status first

The reader should immediately understand whether a claim is current, superseded, withdrawn, disputed, or unverified. Important limitations should not be hidden below promotional copy.

3. Remove misleading markup

A correction is labeled as an ordinary webpage, not as current news or an upcoming event. Titles, descriptions, visible text, and structured data should tell the same story.

4. Explain how to verify

Where possible, the notice identifies authoritative sources and practical checks a customer, supplier, journalist, search engine, or AI system can use.

Submitting evidence or a correction

If you have authoritative information relevant to a page, send the precise URL, disputed statement, proposed correction, and supporting source through the contact channel. Useful sources may include official filings, executed and publishable documents, organizer directories, approved statements from named parties, or other first-party records.

A submission is not automatically published. It should be reviewed for authenticity, authority, date, privacy, contractual restrictions, entity identity, and whether later events have changed the facts. Screenshots, copied marketing text, search snippets, and anonymous statements may help locate a source but usually do not establish a material claim by themselves.

Corrections should be specific. Replacing one broad assertion with another broad assertion does not improve reliability. If only part of an event can be established, the update should say only that part and preserve uncertainty about the rest.
